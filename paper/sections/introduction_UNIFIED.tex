\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language model (LLM) inference increasingly draws from multi-provider portfolios~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt}, spanning lightweight open-weights models to frontier APIs. Because the optimal model varies across tasks, domains, and individual prompts, \textbf{LLM routing}, which dynamically selects the best model per request, has emerged as a critical lever for managing cost and quality at scale~\cite{ong2024routellm, chen2024routerdc, feng2025graphrouter, panda2025pilot}.

Many existing routers train a static classifier on offline evaluation data~\cite{ong2024routellm, chen2024routerdc, feng2025graphrouter}, but frozen policies risk degradation in production due to three factors. First, \textbf{model preference is heterogeneous}: no single model optimally addresses all tasks~\cite{hu2024routerbench, ong2024routellm}, rendering ``best-on-average'' static policies suboptimal. Second, \textbf{deployment distributions often drift} from offline training data: benchmark prompts are generally not representative of production traffic, and the distribution of interest varies by use case~\cite{ailem2024benchmarkrobustness}. Third, \textbf{model portfolios are non-stationary}: providers silently update APIs in ways that shift downstream performance~\cite{chen2023chatgptdrift, ma2024prompt}, and rapid frontier releases~\cite{thompson2026timeline} require routers to adapt without costly retraining.

While recent bandit-based routers~\cite{panda2025pilot, wang2025barp, li2025llmbandit} adapt online to deployment feedback, they lack explicit hedging against misspecified or misleading priors. Under simulated distribution shift, our ablation confirms a single-expert bandit leaves $+1.3$\,pp of statistically significant reward on the table compared to architectures that can safely escape bad priors.

To address these gaps, we present \textbf{banditGPT}, an open-source, cost-aware contextual bandit framework for dynamic LLM routing. \textit{banditGPT} makes three core contributions, each validated across portfolio sizes ($K{\in}\{2, 5, 10\}$) and prior sources:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Context-aware online routing with offline-to-online prior transfer.} To exploit prompt-level preference heterogeneity while continuously adapting to distribution and model drift, \textit{banditGPT} uses a cost-aware contextual LinUCB whose per-prompt embeddings enable model selection to vary with input content. A modular pipeline converts historical offline preference data, such as 80k cross-distribution battles ($K{=}2$) or 355 same-distribution evaluations ($K{\in}\{5, 10\}$), into LinUCB sufficient statistics. This substantially reduces the cold-start penalty, delivering 0.838--0.903 holdout quality \textit{on our benchmarks} from the first request. A continuous cost penalty traces a Pareto frontier, enabling the system to close \textbf{61--75\%} of the gap to a theoretical oracle within a few hundred online interactions (300--400 at $K{=}2$; 533 at $K{\in}\{5, 10\}$), yielding up to a 91\% cost reduction with only 6\% quality loss compared to frontier-only routing \textit{in our tested portfolios}. We validated robustness of the offline-to-online transfer across two prior sources (cross-distribution and same-distribution); the source has no significant effect on steady-state quality ($p{>}0.18$).
    
    \item \textbf{Corralling meta-learning for prior safety.} To address the lack of hedging in existing bandit routers, \textit{banditGPT} employs a Corralling~\cite{agarwal2017corralling} meta-learner that dynamically balances an expert initialized with warmup priors against a \textit{tabula rasa} expert learning from scratch. A controlled factorial ablation \textit{on LMSYS Arena data} confirms that online learning yields a $+7.0$\,pp gain over frozen priors, while the Corralling architecture provides a further $+1.3$\,pp steady-state advantage over a single-expert bandit ($p < 0.0001$, Cohen's $d{=}0.98$), achieving 7--13\% lower cumulative regret than warmup-only under good-to-moderate priors and providing detectable degradation signals under adversarial priors.
    
    \item \textbf{Multi-model portfolio routing with family parameter sharing.} For larger portfolios ($K \geq 3$), \textit{banditGPT} supports hard per-request budget and latency constraints alongside Hybrid LinUCB, which pools observations across semantically related models via data-driven family assignments. At $K{=}5$ \textit{in our multi-provider benchmark}, family sharing yields a $+3.6$\,pp holdout quality advantage over independent per-model learning ($p{=}0.028$) with $4.7\times$ narrower confidence intervals; this advantage attenuates at larger $K$ as per-model data becomes sufficient ($p{=}0.166$ at $K{=}10$). Conversely, a leave-one-out ablation confirms one-shot semantic transfer provides no statistically significant benefit over continuous family sharing ($p{>}0.5$, $d{<}0.05$ at $K{=}10$).
\end{enumerate}
