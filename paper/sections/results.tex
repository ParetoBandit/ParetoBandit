\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate \textit{banditGPT} through the following research questions:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{RQ1}: How does banditGPT's cost--quality trade-off compare to state-of-the-art static routers?
    \item \textbf{RQ2}: How does banditGPT scale to larger model portfolios ($K{\in}\{5, 10\}$)?
    \item \textbf{RQ3}: Does family-level parameter sharing improve sample efficiency?
    \item \textbf{RQ4}: How robust is banditGPT to distribution shift and prior mismatch?
    \item \textbf{RQ5}: How does banditGPT compare to alternative bandit baselines, and how quickly does it converge?
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Comparison Across Cost Regimes}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}a reveals two distinct regimes reflecting the strengths of supervised pre-training versus online adaptation:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{RouteLLM} is pre-trained on ${\sim}$109k out-of-distribution (OOD) supervised preference pairs, providing strong out-of-the-box performance without deployment data.
    \item \textbf{banditGPT} combines 80k OOD battle priors with 1,121 label-free online learning steps, adapting directly from routing outcomes.
\end{itemize}

At \textbf{low budgets} (\$0.0003 to \$0.005 per request), RouteLLM's pre-trained classifier is competitive or superior. Its high-capacity matrix factorization and massive supervised training allow fine-grained prompt-level discrimination when only a handful of prompts can be routed to the expensive model.

At \textbf{higher budgets} (\$0.005+), \textit{banditGPT} surpasses RouteLLM. Online learning discovers the optimal aggregate model ordering for the deployment distribution, achieving a peak quality of $0.903 \pm 0.004$ (95\% CI, $n{=}50$ trials), a $+2.3\%$ improvement over RouteLLM's peak (0.883). The theoretical Oracle upper bound (0.953) routes only 13\% of prompts to the expensive model.

\subsubsection{The Value of Online Adaptation}

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}b demonstrates why online adaptation matters. Starting from a cold-start quality of $0.838 \pm 0.003$ (priors only), \textit{banditGPT}'s 95\% CI lower bound exceeds RouteLLM's peak after just \textbf{${\sim}400$ in-distribution prompts}, ultimately converging near $0.905$. This illustrates that a modest amount of in-distribution experience can outweigh massive out-of-distribution supervision.

\subsubsection{Non-Monotonicity of Static Routing}
\label{sec:nonmonotone}

A critical finding is the non-monotonic behavior of static routing at high costs. When RouteLLM's threshold is lowered to admit more traffic to the expensive model, its quality actually \emph{degrades}---dropping from a peak of 0.883 to 0.812 at maximum cost. At this point, RouteLLM pays ${\sim}44\times$ more than the static cheaper model for $1.3\%$ lower quality. This confirms that static policies assuming monotonicity systematically over-provision expensive models on subsets where they underperform.

\subsubsection{Gap Closure Analysis}

On the frozen holdout ($N{=}750$), \textit{banditGPT} closes \textbf{61.5\%} of the optimality gap to the theoretical oracle ($\frac{0.903 - 0.823}{0.953 - 0.823}$), compared to 45.9\% for RouteLLM at its best threshold. Crucially, \textit{banditGPT} achieves this while only incurring inference costs for the single model selected per request, avoiding the parallel evaluation required to collect supervised labels.

\subsection{Additional Evaluations and Limitations}
\label{sec:robustness_adaptation}
\label{sec:prior_degradation}
\label{sec:transfer_null}
\label{sec:limitations}

Additional analyses in the supplementary material include robustness under prior mismatch, synthetic catastrophic-failure stress tests, dynamic model onboarding, and a leave-one-out semantic transfer ablation which confirms that one-shot parameter bootstrapping provides no significant benefit beyond the continuous family sharing of Hybrid LinUCB ($p{>}0.5$).

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Different portfolios and traffic will shift quantitative crossover points. While our distribution shift analysis (Section~\ref{sec:distribution_shift}) shows adaptation renders prior source statistically irrelevant, magnitudes will vary with distributional mismatch. Our current treatment of non-stationarity relies on meta-level mixing rather than explicit forgetting within experts.
