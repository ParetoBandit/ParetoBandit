\subsubsection{The ``Over-Provisioning Penalty'' of Static Routing}

A critical finding is the non-monotonic behavior of the RouteLLM benchmark. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}, RouteLLM's performance collapses when the threshold is lowered to admit more GPT-4 traffic ($\tau < 0.10$).

\textbf{The Phenomenon}: Users paying \$0.013 (Maximum Cost) receive a reward of $0.812$---worse than the \$0.0003 Mistral baseline ($0.823$). We term this the ``Over-Provisioning Penalty'': paying a $43\times$ cost premium to \emph{decrease} quality by $1.3\%$.

\textbf{The Cause}: Static routers interpolate based on general ``hardness.'' They cannot distinguish the $6\%$ of prompts where GPT-4 is actually better from the $94\%$ where it is simply slower and more verbose.

\textbf{The Solution}: banditGPT (Blue Line) learns this inversion online. By using GPT-4 only when statistically justified, it achieves a peak reward of $0.909$, enabling a $+10.4\%$ quality gain while still reducing costs by $27\%$ compared to the naive GPT-4 baseline.

\subsection{The Cost of Autonomy}

In the ultra-low budget regime ($< \$0.004$), RouteLLM initially outperforms banditGPT. This reflects the ``Cost of Autonomy'': the online learner must pay an exploration penalty (regret) to discover the decision boundary, whereas the static router exploits pre-computed knowledge for free. However, this upfront investment pays dividends: once the budget permits ($> \$0.008$), banditGPT breaks through the static ceiling that confines RouteLLM.
