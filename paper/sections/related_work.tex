\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Current approaches to LLM efficiency generally fall into four categories: Cascades, Static Routers, Generic Bandits, and recent Cost-Aware Bandit Routers. We contrast banditGPT against each, highlighting the specific limitations our architecture addresses.

\subsection{Cascading Architectures}

Cascading systems, exemplified by FrugalGPT~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt}, optimize cost by sequentially querying models from cheapest to most expensive. The router evaluates the response of a smaller model using a scoring function; if the score is insufficient, the query is escalated.

\textbf{The Limitation}: Cascades introduce non-deterministic latency. A difficult query incurs the sum of all failed inference times plus the final success. For real-time applications requiring strict SLA compliance (e.g., $<500$ms TTFT), the ``latency spikes'' of cascading chains are prohibitive.

\textbf{Our Approach}: banditGPT is a \emph{Single-Shot Router}. It predicts the optimal model before inference, guaranteeing deterministic latency and strictly one inference cost per request.

\subsection{Static Preference Predictors}

Predictive routers, such as RouteLLM~\cite{ong2024routellm}, train a classifier (e.g., Matrix Factorization or BERT) to predict model performance based on offline datasets like LMSYS Chatbot Arena~\cite{zheng2023judging}.

\textbf{The Limitation}: These policies are static. As demonstrated in Section~\ref{sec:pareto}, they suffer from \emph{Prior Rigidity}: when the production distribution exhibits ``Quality Inversion'' (where cheap models outperform expensive ones on specific tasks), static routers fail to adapt, incurring an ``Over-Provisioning Penalty.'' Furthermore, they suffer from the Cold Start problem: adding a new model requires collecting thousands of new samples and retraining the router.

\textbf{Our Approach}: banditGPT is an \emph{Online Learner}. Through continuous feedback, it updates its beliefs in real-time. New models are admitted with tabula rasa initialization and converge to their appropriate role within a few hundred interactions of online learning, without requiring retraining of the full router.

\subsection{Contextual Bandits in NLP}

The use of Multi-Armed Bandits (MAB) for model selection is an emerging field. Recent works like LLM-Bandit and C2MAB apply standard LinUCB~\cite{li2010contextual} or Thompson Sampling~\cite{agrawal2013thompson} to routing.

\textbf{The Limitation}: Standard bandit algorithms assume stationarity and well-specified priors. They lack safety mechanisms for ``Negative Transfer,'' where a transferred prior could become harmful under severe domain mismatch. They also struggle with ``Expert Death,'' where a bad start permanently marginalizes a potentially good model.

\textbf{Our Approach}: We introduce Expert Corralling~\cite{agarwal2017corralling} to the LLM routing domain. By maintaining a portfolio of experts (Warmup vs.\ Tabula Rasa) and dynamically mixing them, banditGPT provides a worst-case safety guarantee that standard LinUCB implementations lack.

\subsection{Preference-Conditioned and Cost-Aware Bandit Routers}

A recent wave of work specifically addresses cost-aware LLM routing via contextual bandits.
PILOT~\cite{panda2025pilot} learns a shared query--LLM embedding space from offline human preferences and refines it online via LinUCB, pairing this with a multi-choice knapsack cost policy for budget-constrained routing.
BaRP~\cite{wang2025barp} trains a preference-conditioned contextual bandit under partial (bandit) feedback, enabling operators to adjust the performance--cost trade-off at inference time without retraining.
LLM Bandit~\cite{li2025llmbandit} formulates model selection as a preference-conditioned MAB with generalization to unseen models.

These methods advance cost-aware routing but differ from banditGPT in three structural ways.
First, none employs a meta-ensemble: each uses a single contextual bandit, whereas banditGPT runs \emph{bandits over bandits} via Corralling, providing safety insurance against prior mismatch (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}).
Second, banditGPT invests in rich prior machinery---80k-battle warmup priors and progressive model registration---rather than learning an embedding space from scratch.
Third, banditGPT enforces hard cost and latency constraints via Dynamic Pareto Filtering before the bandit selects, whereas PILOT and BaRP fold cost into the reward or a separate knapsack/scalarization policy.
None of these concurrent systems provides an open-source implementation as of this writing; a detailed architectural comparison is provided in Appendix~\ref{appendix:positioning}.
