\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Current approaches to LLM efficiency generally fall into three categories: Cascades, Static Routers, and Generic Bandits. We contrast banditGPT against each, highlighting the specific limitations our architecture addresses.

\subsection{Cascading Architectures}

Cascading systems, exemplified by FrugalGPT~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt}, optimize cost by sequentially querying models from cheapest to most expensive. The router evaluates the response of a smaller model using a scoring function; if the score is insufficient, the query is escalated.

\textbf{The Limitation}: Cascades introduce non-deterministic latency. A difficult query incurs the sum of all failed inference times plus the final success. For real-time applications requiring strict SLA compliance (e.g., $<500$ms TTFT), the ``latency spikes'' of cascading chains are prohibitive.

\textbf{Our Approach}: banditGPT is a \emph{Single-Shot Router}. It predicts the optimal model before inference, guaranteeing deterministic latency and strictly one inference cost per request.

\subsection{Static Preference Predictors}

Predictive routers, such as RouteLLM~\cite{ong2024routellm}, train a classifier (e.g., Matrix Factorization or BERT) to predict model performance based on offline datasets like LMSYS Chatbot Arena~\cite{zheng2023judging}.

\textbf{The Limitation}: These policies are static. As demonstrated in Section~5.1, they suffer from \emph{Prior Rigidity}: when the production distribution exhibits ``Quality Inversion'' (where cheap models outperform expensive ones on specific tasks), static routers fail to adapt, incurring an ``Over-Provisioning Penalty.'' Furthermore, they suffer from the Cold Start problem: adding a new model requires collecting thousands of new samples and retraining the router.

\textbf{Our Approach}: banditGPT is an \emph{Online Learner}. Through continuous feedback, it updates its beliefs in real-time. Our Latent Semantic Transfer mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}) enables admission of new models by bootstrapping from learned priors of semantic neighbors, avoiding the retraining bottleneck.

\subsection{Contextual Bandits in NLP}

The use of Multi-Armed Bandits (MAB) for model selection is an emerging field. Recent works like LLM-Bandit and C2MAB apply standard LinUCB~\cite{li2010contextual} or Thompson Sampling~\cite{agrawal2013thompson} to routing.

\textbf{The Limitation}: Standard bandit algorithms assume stationarity and well-specified priors. They lack safety mechanisms for ``Negative Transfer,'' where a transferred prior could become harmful under severe domain mismatch. They also struggle with ``Expert Death,'' where a bad start permanently marginalizes a potentially good model.

\textbf{Our Approach}: We introduce Expert Corralling~\cite{agarwal2017corralling} to the LLM routing domain. By maintaining a portfolio of experts (Warmup vs.\ Tabula Rasa) and dynamically mixing them, banditGPT provides a worst-case safety guarantee that standard LinUCB implementations lack.
