\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

LLM routing has matured from cascading heuristics to supervised classifiers, and recently to bandit-based adaptive routers. We position banditGPT within this landscape, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}.

\subsection{Static and Supervised Routers}
Early cost-aware routing utilized cascading (e.g., FrugalGPT~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt}), escalating queries to more expensive models until a quality threshold was met. This reduces average cost but introduces non-deterministic latency. More recently, supervised static routers like RouteLLM~\cite{ong2024routellm} (our primary baseline), GraphRouter~\cite{feng2025graphrouter}, and RouterDC~\cite{chen2024routerdc} train offline classifiers to predict the best model per query. While these systems offer strong out-of-the-box routing when deployment traffic matches the training distribution, their frozen policies struggle with non-stationary deployments and exhibit non-monotonic behavior when distribution shift occurs (Section~\ref{sec:pareto}).

\subsection{Bandit-Based Adaptive Routers}
To enable online adaptation, concurrent works formulate routing as a contextual bandit problem. PILOT~\cite{panda2025pilot} refines a shared embedding space online and optimizes cumulative cost over a horizon via a knapsack policy. BaRP~\cite{wang2025barp} and LLM Bandit~\cite{li2025llmbandit} train contextual bandits under partial feedback, matching deployment constraints where only the selected model's reward is observed. Other approaches leverage combinatorial multi-armed bandits (C2MAB-V~\cite{dai2024c2mab}) or RL agents (xRouter~\cite{zhang2025xrouter}).

\subsection{Positioning banditGPT}
banditGPT complements concurrent bandit-based routers by introducing three structural advances designed specifically for production reliability:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Meta-ensemble via Corralling:} Unlike prior systems using a single policy, banditGPT introduces a meta-learning layer (Corralling~\cite{agarwal2017corralling}) over two expert bandits. This provides a regret-hedging safety mechanism against prior misspecification that standard bandits lack.
    \item \textbf{Offline-to-online prior transfer:} Instead of learning entirely from scratch or pure online feedback, banditGPT converts offline battles into LinUCB sufficient statistics, eliminating the cold-start penalty.
    \item \textbf{Hard constraints via budget filtering:} Rather than scalarizing cost into the reward or optimizing over a horizon, banditGPT strictly enforces per-request hard constraints (cost ceiling, quality floor) by pruning ineligible models before the bandit selects.
\end{enumerate}
