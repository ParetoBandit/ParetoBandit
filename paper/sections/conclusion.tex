\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented ParetoBandit, an adaptive LLM routing system that integrates cost-aware contextual bandits with online budget pacing and geometric forgetting.
Production LLM serving is inherently non-stationary: providers revise pricing, model quality can regress silently, and new endpoints need to be absorbed without downtime, and ParetoBandit is the first router evaluated under all of them.
ParetoBandit addresses this gap directly.
Across four experiments the system never exceeds the ceiling by more than ${\sim}4\%$ for any binding budget; under cost drift it autonomously exploits a price drop and maintains compliance when pricing is restored; under silent quality degradation it detects the regression, redistributes traffic, and holds budget, while baselines without closed-loop cost control overshoot by up to $\ppMaxOvershootX{\times}$; and cold-start model onboarding proceeds without reconfiguration.

Beyond the empirical results, the Pareto knee-point hyperparameter protocol (Appendix~\ref{sec:hparam_sweep}) provides a principled method---the first we are aware of---for jointly calibrating bandit hyperparameters across two competing production requirements, stationary routing efficiency and rapid adaptation to price and quality shifts, an advance over single-objective tuning, which invariably selects zero forgetting.
As multi-provider LLM portfolios become the norm, these results suggest that bandit-driven routing with integrated budget pacing and non-stationarity awareness is not merely desirable but essential for reliable, cost-controlled LLM serving in production.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Six limitations scope the current contribution, most of which are addressable through extensions of the existing framework (see Future Work).
\emph{(i)~Offline evaluation:} all experiments use a fixed reward matrix with injected non-stationarity; live deployment would add stochastic feedback delays and distribution shift.
\emph{(ii)~Deterministic rewards:} we evaluate with LLM-judge scores; the noise and sparsity of human-in-the-loop feedback remain untested.
\emph{(iii)~Rate vs.\ aggregate budget:} $B$ is a per-request rate, so traffic spikes can exceed an aggregate dollar cap even while per-request compliance holds.
\emph{(iv)~Onboarding exploration cost:} online model discovery exposes a bounded burn-in cost to production traffic.
\emph{(v)~No latency awareness:} the current formulation does not model downstream inference latency or endpoint congestion.
\emph{(vi)~Cost-only constraint:} ParetoBandit constrains cost and maximizes quality, but some deployments require the dual formulation---minimizing cost subject to a quality floor.

\paragraph{Future work.}
\emph{Live deployment}~(i,\,ii) is the most immediate next step. This requires computing prompt embeddings online rather than from a cached offline matrix, and tuning $\gamma$ for the reality that reward labels arrive for only a fraction of requests and with variable delay. Closing this gap would validate that the adaptation and budget-pacing behaviours observed in simulation transfer to real serving conditions.

\emph{Latency awareness}~(v) maps naturally onto the BwK framework as a second dual variable. Adding it would let the router jointly respect cost ceilings and tail-latency SLAs, avoiding the scenario where a budget-optimal route violates a response-time guarantee.

\emph{Aggregate budget enforcement}~(iii) can be layered on via a token-bucket mechanism, capping total spend over a billing window. That said, practitioners who set the per-request ceiling at a profitable operating point may find an aggregate cap unnecessary, since higher traffic then increases both spend and revenue.

\emph{Quality-constrained routing}~(vi) inverts the pacer to track reward against a floor~$\tau$, providing an online counterpart to PROTEUS~\cite{bhatti2026proteus}. This would serve deployments where a minimum quality SLA is the binding requirement and cost should be minimized subject to it.

Validating these extensions under realistic deployment conditions is the natural next step.
