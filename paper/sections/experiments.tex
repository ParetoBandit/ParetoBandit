\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

We first describe the shared datasets, evaluation metrics, and implementation details used across all research questions. Additional robustness stress tests are reported in the supplementary material.

\subsubsection{Data, Reward Signal, and Feature Pipeline}
\label{sec:data}
We draw prompts from LMSYS Chatbot Arena~\cite{zheng2023judging} (1,871 prompts across dev and holdout splits) and the RouteLLM battle corpus~\cite{ong2024routellm} (80,000 pairwise battles). Each prompt is encoded by Sentence-BERT (\texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2}~\cite{reimers2019sentence}, 384D) and projected to 32 dimensions via unsupervised PCA trained once on the 80K RouteLLM battles.

At routing time, the system observes only the reward of the selected model. For $K{=}2$, 1,871 LMSYS prompts are judged deterministically by GPT-4o, yielding $r \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$. For $K{>}2$, 10 models are evaluated on LMSYS prompts by a cross-provider panel of 3--4 LLM judges using a structured chain-of-thought protocol, yielding a continuous reward $r \in [0, 1]$.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Regimes and Data Splits}
Our evaluation operates at two portfolio scales requiring different warmup prior sources:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{$K{=}2$ (RouteLLM comparison):} We initialize warmup priors for Mixtral-8x7B and GPT-4-Turbo from the 80K RouteLLM battles. This creates a realistic \emph{cross-distribution} setup, matching RouteLLM's training condition against a 750-prompt LMSYS Arena holdout.
    \item \textbf{$K{=}5$ and $K{=}10$ (Multi-model scaling):} Since RouteLLM data covers only 2 models, we construct \emph{same-distribution} priors from a 355-prompt LMSYS prior training split, evaluating on the shared 750-prompt holdout.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics \& Implementation Details}

We compare against static single-model baselines, random routing, RouteLLM-MF~\cite{ong2024routellm}, and an oracle upper bound. Performance is evaluated via Average Reward ($\bar{r}$), Normalized Cost ($\bar{c}$), and Gap Closure (\%) (the fraction of the oracle-cheaper performance gap recovered by the router).

Unless noted otherwise, we report mean and 95\% confidence intervals over 20 random seeds for each cost penalty $\lambda$. For family assignment (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}), we use syntactic inference for $K{\leq}3$ and data-driven tetrachoric correlation (threshold $r_{\text{tet}} \geq 0.6$) for $K{\geq}5$.
