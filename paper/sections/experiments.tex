\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate banditGPT on the data and splits described in Section~\ref{sec:data}, using commercial per-request costs for each model. The remainder of this section validates the system across six dimensions: cost-quality Pareto optimality for $K{=}2$ (Section~\ref{sec:pareto}), multi-model scaling to larger portfolios (Section~\ref{sec:multimodel}), the benefits of family parameter sharing (Section~\ref{sec:k_scaling}), robustness to distribution shift (Section~\ref{sec:distribution_shift}), comparisons to standard Thompson Sampling baselines (Section~\ref{sec:lints_comparison}), and cumulative regret over time (Section~\ref{sec:cumulative_regret}). Additional robustness stress tests are reported in the supplementary material.

\subsubsection{Model Topology \& Baselines}

We mirror standard $K{=}2$ evaluation topologies~\cite{ong2024routellm}:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Cheaper Open-Weights Model} (Cost: $\approx$ \$0.30 per 1M tokens).
    \item \textbf{Expensive Frontier Model} (Cost: $\approx$ \$10.00 to \$30.00 per 1M tokens).
\end{itemize}

We compare against static single-model baselines, random routing, RouteLLM-MF~\cite{ong2024routellm}, and an oracle upper bound that selects the better model per prompt.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We evaluate performance using three key metrics standard in cost-aware routing literature:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Average Reward ($\bar{r}$)}: The mean quality score of the selected responses.
    
    \item \textbf{Normalized Cost ($\bar{c}$)}: The average inference cost, normalized such that the expensive frontier model $\approx 1.0$.
    
    \item \textbf{Gap Closure (\%)}: The percentage of the performance gap between the cheaper model and the Oracle that the router recovers, defined as:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Gap Closure} = \frac{R_{\text{router}} - R_{\text{cheaper}}}{R_{\text{oracle}} - R_{\text{cheaper}}} \times 100\%
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Implementation Details}

\textbf{Trials:} Unless noted otherwise, we report mean and 95\% confidence intervals over 20 seeds for each cost penalty $\lambda$ in the sweep. Additional statistical details and robustness checks are in the appendix (supplementary material).

\textbf{Family assignment:} For the $K{=}2$ main evaluation and $K{=}3$ Corralling experiments, each model belongs to a different provider, so syntactic inference produces singleton families (equivalent to disjoint LinUCB). For the $K{\geq}5$ multi-model scaling experiments (Appendix), where multiple models share a provider, families are assigned via data-driven tetrachoric correlation on held-out rewards (threshold $r_{\text{tet}} \geq 0.6$; see Section~\ref{sec:methodology}).
