\begin{abstract}
Large language model (LLM) inference is costly, and the best model to use can depend on the prompt and change over time. We present \textbf{banditGPT}, an open-source cost-aware contextual bandit framework for LLM routing that combines offline warmup priors with online adaptation.

On held-out LMSYS prompts ($N{=}750$), model preference varies by prompt, and the router's PCA features predict this variation (Spearman $\rho=-0.370$, $p<0.0001$, exceeding 100 random projections). In our evaluation, this heterogeneity can make ``spend more'' policies suboptimal: when RouteLLM is pushed toward high-cost operation, it spends $43\times$ more for $1.3\%$ lower average quality on this distribution.

banditGPT addresses this setting with (1)~\textbf{cost-aware LinUCB} initialized from 80k offline battles and refined online without labels, and (2)~\textbf{Corralling} meta-learning to hedge against prior mismatch. In a two-model benchmark, banditGPT surpasses RouteLLM's peak quality after $\sim$200 in-distribution prompts and achieves 70.0\% gap closure to oracle (vs.\ 46.2\%) at moderate-to-high budgets; at low budgets, RouteLLM remains competitive. The framework supports multi-model portfolios ($K\geq 3$) with continuous cost--quality Pareto optimization. We also report a null result for per-model semantic transfer in cold-start experiments, clarifying where prior-based bootstrapping helps in our current setting.
\end{abstract}
