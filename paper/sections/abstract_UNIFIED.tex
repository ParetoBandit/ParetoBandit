\begin{abstract}
The large language model landscape evolves rapidly and unevenly, with the number of major LLM and multimodal releases per year increasing substantially between 2023 and 2025~\cite{thompson2026timeline}.
Deployment distributions drift from offline training data, prompt-to-model preferences are heterogeneous, and in our evaluation 14.1\% of prompts favor a cheaper model over the frontier option.
Static routers degrade silently as these conditions shift; bandit-based alternatives can adapt but rely on linear reward assumptions that may not hold, risking convergence to suboptimal policies with no mechanism for self-correction.

We present \textbf{banditGPT}, an open-source cost-aware contextual bandit framework with three interlocking mechanisms:
(1)~Cost-aware LinUCB, initialized from offline preference battles, closes \textbf{70\%} of the gap to an oracle router within 300--400 online interactions;
(2)~a \textbf{Corralling} meta-learner that hedges between warmup-prior and tabula-rasa experts, reducing cumulative regret by \textbf{30--40\%} and falling back gracefully under adversarial priors; and
(3)~\textbf{Hybrid LinUCB}, which pools observations across model families to yield a $+2.9$~pp quality gain at $K{=}5$ ($p{=}0.008$) with $3\times$ narrower confidence intervals.

Across six providers and a $600\times$ cost range, banditGPT traces a cost-quality \textbf{Pareto frontier}, achieving up to 89\% cost reduction with less than 6\% quality loss.
We report honest results throughout, including a null finding: one-shot semantic transfer adds no significant benefit over family-level sharing ($p{>}0.5$).
Code, warmup priors, and experimental logs are released in full.
\end{abstract}
