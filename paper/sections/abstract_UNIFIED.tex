\begin{abstract}
Deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) at scale requires balancing generation quality and inference cost across diverse user queries. While static LLM routers trained on offline preference data optimize this tradeoff initially, their performance often degrades in production due to shifting user distributions. Empirically, we find that 14.1\% of prompts favor a cheaper model over a more expensive frontier model. This is a nuance static routers miss, leading to suboptimal routing and overspending. While contextual bandits offer a promising adaptive alternative, existing bandit frameworks often face a cold-start penalty, struggle to hedge against misleading offline priors, or rely on soft cost penalties rather than strict per-request budgets. To address these gaps, we introduce \textit{banditGPT}, an open-source contextual bandit library for dynamic LLM routing. \textit{banditGPT} complements the emerging landscape of adaptive routers with three structural contributions: (1) it enables offline-to-online prior transfer via a Hybrid LinUCB mechanism, providing production-quality routing from the first request; (2) it employs a Corralling meta-learner that robustly balances these offline priors with online \textit{tabula rasa} exploration to safely hedge against severe distribution shifts; and (3) it enforces hard, per-request budget and latency constraints. Extensive evaluations across model portfolios spanning six commercial providers and a 600$\times$ cost range demonstrate our approach's effectiveness. \textit{banditGPT} achieves up to an 89\% cost reduction with less than 6\% quality degradation compared to routing exclusively to frontier models. Rigorous ablation analysis confirms that online learning yields a dominant $+7.0$ percentage-point gain over static baselines, while the Corralling meta-learner provides a further $+1.3$\,pp improvement. Ultimately, the system closes 70.0\% of the optimality gap to a theoretical oracle router within just 400 online interactions. Code, warmup priors, and experimental logs are open-sourced to facilitate future research in adaptive, cost-effective LLM routing.
\end{abstract}
