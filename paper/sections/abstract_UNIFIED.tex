\begin{abstract}
Deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) at scale requires balancing generation quality and inference cost across diverse user queries and growing model portfolios. While static LLM routers trained on offline preference data optimize this tradeoff initially, their performance often degrades in production due to shifting user distributions and evolving model landscapes. Empirically, we find that prompt embeddings predict per-prompt model preference (Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.370$, $p{<}0.0001$), and that this heterogeneity renders ``always route to the best-on-average model'' policies suboptimal across portfolio sizes from $K{=}2$ to $K{=}10$. While contextual bandits offer a promising adaptive alternative, existing bandit frameworks often face a cold-start penalty, struggle to hedge against misleading offline priors, or rely on soft cost penalties rather than strict per-request budgets. To address these gaps, we introduce \textit{banditGPT}, an open-source contextual bandit library for dynamic LLM routing. \textit{banditGPT} complements the emerging landscape of adaptive routers with three structural contributions: (1) it enables source-agnostic offline-to-online prior transfer via a Hybrid LinUCB mechanism, accepting any offline model-quality data to provide production-quality routing from the first request; (2) it employs a Corralling meta-learner that robustly balances these offline priors with online \textit{tabula rasa} exploration to safely hedge against severe distribution shifts; and (3) it enforces hard, per-request budget and latency constraints. Extensive evaluations across model portfolios spanning six commercial providers and a 600$\times$ cost range demonstrate our approach's effectiveness. \textit{banditGPT} achieves up to an 89\% cost reduction with less than 6\% quality degradation compared to routing exclusively to frontier models. Rigorous ablation analysis confirms that online learning yields a dominant $+7.0$ percentage-point gain over static baselines, while the Corralling meta-learner provides a further $+1.3$\,pp improvement; prior source has no significant effect ($p{=}0.18$). Across portfolio sizes from $K{=}2$ to $K{=}10$ and two distinct prior regimes, the system closes 69--75\% of the optimality gap to a theoretical oracle router within a few hundred online interactions. Code, warmup priors, and experimental logs are open-sourced to facilitate future research in adaptive, cost-effective LLM routing.
\end{abstract}
