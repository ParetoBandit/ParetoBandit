\section{Empirical Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

LLM routing literature often assumes \emph{monotonicity}: that allocating more budget to a frontier model consistently yields equal or higher quality~\cite{ong2024routellm, chen2023frugalgpt}.
We present an empirical finding challenging this assumption, motivating our bandit-based architecture.

We evaluate $K{=}10$ models spanning three cost tiers on $N{=}750$ held-out prompts, with rewards computed as $\text{mean}(\text{vote} \times \text{confidence})$ from a multi-judge panel (\S\ref{app:cot_panel}).
Per-model OLS regressions on the router's PCA features reveal that context affects models differently: a likelihood-ratio test rejects the shared-slope null ($\chi^2 = 771.3$, $\text{df}{=}54$, $p < 10^{-15}$; Figure~\ref{fig:contextual_heterogeneity}A).
All ten per-model contextual slopes are significant, but magnitudes span ${\sim}8\times$ (Figure~\ref{fig:contextual_heterogeneity}B), and the regression lines cross---confirming that no single model dominates the full feature space.

These findings establish three architectural requirements for \textit{banditGPT}:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Exploit Contextual Signal:} Embeddings carry actionable model-preference information whose strength varies across models, enabling a contextual bandit to route each prompt to its best model.
    \item \textbf{Avoid Static Policies:} No single model dominates: the best model varies with prompt features. A static policy is constrained to the best single model (GPT-4.1, mean reward $0.910$), leaving the 79.5\% of prompts where contextual selection improves unaddressed.
    \item \textbf{Safe Online Learning:} Because the preference landscape depends on the deployment distribution, routers must adapt online while hedging against misleading offline priors (verified in Section~\ref{sec:distribution_shift}).
\end{enumerate}
