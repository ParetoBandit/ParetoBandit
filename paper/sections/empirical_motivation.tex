\section{Empirical Motivation: The Semantic Structure of Routing}

Before presenting our routing algorithm, we first establish that LLM routing is a structured problem amenable to learning, rather than a random optimization task.

\subsection{Forensic Cluster Analysis: The ``Alignment Tax''}

We projected 1,871 prompts from the LMSYS dev and holdout sets onto the first two principal components of a 32-component PCA model trained on sentence embeddings. A spatial analysis of the semantic manifold (PC1 cutoff at 0.3) reveals two distinct clusters. \textbf{Critically}, checking the ground-truth reward gaps within these spatial regions exposes a \textbf{Performance Inversion} that contradicts standard ``complexity'' assumptions (Table~\ref{tab:cluster_inversion}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{The Cluster Inversion: Spatial Analysis Validated Against Reward Gaps}
\label{tab:cluster_inversion}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Cluster} & \textbf{Semantic Type} & \textbf{Mean Gap} & \textbf{Winner} \\
& & $(R_{\text{GPT-4-Turbo}} - R_{\text{Mixtral}})$ & \\
\midrule
Low PC1 ($< 0.3$) & Open-Ended Chat & $+0.133$ & GPT-4-Turbo \\
High PC1 ($\geq 0.3$) & Strict/Constrained & $-0.682$ & Mixtral-8x7B \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{The Low PC1 ``Nuance Zone'' (82.4\%).}
Contrary to the assumption that routine tasks are ``easy,'' the Low PC1 cluster is dominated by open-ended conversational prompts where the frontier model's superior instruction following and world knowledge yield a significant advantage ($+0.133$). This is the ``Quality Moat.'' A naive router that offloads this entire cluster to Mixtral would degrade performance. banditGPT succeeds not by dumping this cluster, but by surgically preserving it for the expensive model.

\paragraph{The High PC1 ``Strictness Zone'' (17.6\%).}
The ``Hard'' cluster---visually distinct in Figure~\ref{fig:pca}---is actually defined by strict formatting constraints (forensic analysis reveals 85\% are ``text completion only, no chat'' templates). Here, the frontier model suffers from an \textbf{Alignment Tax}: its RLHF conditioning toward helpfulness causes verbosity that violates negative constraints. The open-weights model, being less aggressively aligned, adheres strictly to the format, winning by a massive margin ($-0.682$).

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The routing opportunity is counter-intuitive. We do not save money by routing ``easy'' tasks to cheap models; we save money by identifying the ``Strict'' tasks where the expensive model actively fails, effectively turning a quality problem (Alignment Tax) into a cost-saving solution.

\subsection{Scale Validation: The ``Economic Catastrophe''}

To ensure these findings were not an artifact of the smaller holdout set, we extended our analysis to the complete LMSYS Chat-1M dataset, comprising 594,199 unique prompts---a $317\times$ increase in scale.

Remarkably, the semantic structure exhibits \emph{spectral invariance}. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:spectral}, the decision boundary remains stable at $\text{PC1}=0.3$. However, the distribution shifts significantly: production traffic is overwhelmingly natural language (Low PC1: 94.1\%), with the strict constraint artifact shrinking to just 5.9\%. This makes the artifact more valuable: it is a rare, high-confidence exploitation opportunity.

\label{tab:spectral}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Holdout ($N=1.8$k)} & \textbf{Chat-1M ($N=594$k)} \\
\midrule
PC1 Variance & 3.10\% & 3.10\% \\
Natural Language (Low PC1) & 82.4\% & 94.1\% \\
Strict Constraints (High PC1) & 17.6\% & 5.9\% \\

\textbf{Implication}: This reveals that our initial holdout evaluation represents a conservative stress test. In production (Chat-1M), the Alignment Tax artifact becomes rarer (5.9\%) but more valuable: it is a high-confidence exploitation zone where Mixtral consistently wins. Meanwhile, the natural language zone (94.1\%) requires careful sub-manifold discovery to avoid quality degradation. This dual challenge---exploiting rare artifacts while preserving quality on common tasks---necessitates adaptive, context-aware routing that static classifiers cannot achieve.

\subsection{Distribution Shift: The ``Negative Transfer'' Trap}

A fundamental challenge in routing is \emph{covariate shift}: the phenomenon where the distribution of prompts in production ($Q$) differs from the offline training data ($P$). If significant shift exists, static priors (like those used by RouteLLM) will be miscalibrated, leading to \emph{Negative Transfer}.

To quantify this, we calculated the Population Stability Index (PSI) between our Warmup Priors (Source) and the RouteLLM deployment data (Target). As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:shift}, we observe a significant domain mismatch:
\begin{equation}
\text{PSI} = \sum (\%P_i - \%Q_i) \ln(\%P_i / \%Q_i) = \mathbf{0.275}
\end{equation}

This exceeds the critical industry threshold of $0.25$, indicating substantial distribution shift.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{figures/figure2_distribution_shift.png}
\caption{Feature Distribution Shift. (Top) The deployment distribution (Red) is significantly left-shifted compared to the training data (Blue), with PSI=0.275. (Bottom) This shift is driven by a higher prevalence of Low PC1 tasks in production than anticipated.}
\label{fig:shift}
\end{figure}

\textbf{The Consequence} (Table~\ref{tab:prior_mismatch}): This shift is not harmless; it actively degrades performance.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GPT-4-Turbo}: Prior expectation $0.94$ $\to$ Observed $0.84$ (Overestimated utility).
    \item \textbf{Mixtral}: Prior expectation $0.45$ $\to$ Observed $0.81$ (Underestimated utility by $+80\%$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Conclusion}: The offline priors are structurally biased against the cheaper model. A static router trained on $P$ would erroneously route traffic to GPT-4-Turbo based on outdated ``hardness'' beliefs. This necessitates the Adaptive Corralling mechanism (Section~3) to dynamically down-weight these misspecified priors in real-time.
