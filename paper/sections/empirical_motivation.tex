\section{Empirical Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

LLM routing literature often assumes \emph{monotonicity}: that allocating more budget to an expensive frontier model consistently yields equal or higher quality. We present an empirical finding challenging this assumption, motivating our bandit-based architecture.

On 14.1\% of 750 held-out prompts, human judges actually preferred a cheaper open-weights model over a frontier model (Table~\ref{tab:outcome_distribution}). Crucially, this preference heterogeneity correlates significantly with the prompt's semantic embedding. When prompts are projected onto the first principal component (PC1) of our unsupervised PCA feature space, we observe a significant Spearman correlation with the reward gap ($R_{\text{Frontier}} - R_{\text{Cheaper}}$): $\rho = -0.370$, $p < 0.0001$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lmsys_holdout_structure}A). The Router PCA exceeds 100 random orthonormal projections (Figure~\ref{fig:lmsys_holdout_structure}B).

These findings establish three architectural requirements for \textit{banditGPT}:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Exploit Semantic Structure:} Embeddings carry actionable model-preference signals, allowing a router to discover when expensive models are genuinely needed.
    \item \textbf{Avoid Static Monotonicity Priors:} A frozen policy assuming monotonicity systematically over-provisions the expensive model where it underperforms (verified in Section~\ref{sec:pareto}).
    \item \textbf{Safe Online Learning:} Because preference structure shifts with deployment distributions, routers must adapt online while hedging against misleading offline priors (verified in Section~\ref{sec:distribution_shift}).
\end{enumerate}
