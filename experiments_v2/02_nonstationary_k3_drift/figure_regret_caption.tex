    \caption{Cumulative cost-adjusted regret under model quality shift
    ($K{=}3$; 40~seeds, $\pm$1\,SE shading).
    %
    Three conditions of increasing sophistication are compared:
    \textbf{Fixed Policy (offline)} deploys warmup priors without
    online learning---the industry-standard pattern of training
    offline and deploying statically.
    \textbf{Naive Bandit ($\gamma{=}1.0$)} adds online LinUCB
    with infinite memory.
    \textbf{BanditGPT ($\gamma{=}0.997$)} adds geometric
    forgetting with a tuned effective memory of ${\sim}333$ steps.
    %
    Each component yields a significant regret reduction:
    online learning reduces regret by 8.5\%
    ($208.4 \to 190.6$; $p < 10^{-6}$),
    and geometric forgetting reduces it by a further 37.0\%
    ($190.6 \to 120.0$; $p < 10^{-26}$).
    All pairwise comparisons are significant after
    Holm--Bonferroni correction (paired $t$-tests, 39~d.f.).
    %
    The divergence between BanditGPT and the Naive Bandit
    becomes visible immediately after the swap boundary
    (step~893): BanditGPT discounts stale Phase~1 evidence
    and re-learns the new optimum within ${\sim}200$ steps,
    while the Naive Bandit's infinite memory dilutes the
    Phase~2 signal with obsolete Phase~1 observations.
    The Fixed Policy is deterministic ($\sigma{=}0$) and
    helpless---it continues routing to the now-worst arm.
    }
    \label{fig:cumulative_regret}
\end{figure*}
