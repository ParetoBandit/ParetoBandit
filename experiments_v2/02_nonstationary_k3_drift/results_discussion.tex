\subsection{Non-stationary Adaptation (Figures~\ref{fig:cumulative_regret}--\ref{fig:gamma_trajectory})}
\label{sec:nonstationarity}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
LLM providers routinely update their models: OpenAI's GPT-4 has
received multiple mid-cycle quality changes, Anthropic's Claude
models are refreshed quarterly, and open-weight models on inference
APIs (e.g., Together, Fireworks) may be swapped to newer
checkpoints without notice.
Equally common, providers restructure pricing---Google's Gemini
models have seen multiple price reductions since launch, and
competition drives aggressive pricing for open-weight inference.
When a model's quality or cost changes, a router trained on
historical data inherits a \emph{stale policy}: it continues
routing to a model whose quality has degraded or whose cost
advantage has disappeared.
We ask: \emph{can BanditGPT adapt to non-stationary environments
automatically, without manual re-calibration?}

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
BanditGPT addresses non-stationarity through a \emph{forgetting
factor} $\gamma \in (0, 1]$ applied to the disjoint
LinUCB~\cite{li2010contextual} sufficient statistics.
At each step $t$, before incorporating the new observation
$(x_t, a_t, r_t)$, the design matrix and reward accumulator for
each arm $a$ are discounted:
\begin{align}
    A_a &\leftarrow \gamma \, A_a + x_t x_t^\top
    \label{eq:forget_A} \\
    b_a &\leftarrow \gamma \, b_a + r_t \, x_t
    \label{eq:forget_b}
\end{align}
This exponential weighting, originally proposed for
switching bandits by Garivier and
Moulines~\cite{garivier2011switching}, gives observations an
effective half-life of $\ln 2 / (1 - \gamma)$ steps.
With $\gamma{=}0.999$, the effective memory window is
$\sim\!1{,}000$ steps: recent evidence dominates while historical
observations---including warmup priors---are geometrically
discounted.
During stable periods ($\gamma$ close to 1), the
forgetting overhead is minimal: the router retains nearly all
its learned policy.  The parameter $\gamma$ thus controls a
bias--variance tradeoff between \emph{stability} (trusting
historical evidence) and \emph{plasticity} (responding to new
evidence).

We evaluate this mechanism under two complementary forms of
non-stationarity that capture the dominant failure modes in
production LLM routing.

\subsubsection{Experiment 02a: Model Quality Shift}
\label{sec:reward_shift}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We construct a controlled model quality shift using the $K{=}3$
portfolio on real benchmark data.
The router learns online on the validation split
($n{=}1{,}785$; 32~NLP benchmarks) and priors are trained on a
disjoint training split ($n_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}5{,}000$).
The 1{,}785-step trajectory has two phases:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Phase~1} (steps 1--893): Normal reward landscape.
    Warmup priors are well-calibrated.
    Mistral-Large is utility-best
    (mean utility $0.840 = 0.917 - 0.20 \times 0.384$).

  \item \textbf{Phase~2} (steps 894--1{,}785): \textbf{Reward swap}.
    Llama-8B and Mistral-Large exchange their per-prompt reward
    and cost columns, simulating simultaneous API quality changes.
    Post-swap, Llama-8B is utility-best
    ($0.922 = 0.922 - 0$) while Mistral-Large drops to worst
    ($0.727 = 0.804 - 0.077$).  Gemini-Pro is unchanged (anchor).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Five conditions are compared across 40~seeds:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{BanditGPT (adaptive~$\gamma$):}
    Warmup priors with residual-based adaptive forgetting
    (Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_gamma}).
  \item \textbf{BanditGPT ($\gamma{=}0.999$):}
    Warmup priors with fixed forgetting (effective memory
    ${\sim}1{,}000$ steps).
  \item \textbf{No~forgetting ($\gamma{=}1.0$):}
    Warmup priors, no forgetting.
  \item \textbf{Fast~forgetting ($\gamma{=}0.995$):}
    Warmup priors, aggressive forgetting (effective memory
    ${\sim}200$ steps).
  \item \textbf{Tabula~Rasa:}
    No priors, no forgetting.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Results (Figure~\ref{fig:cumulative_regret}).}
BanditGPT (adaptive~$\gamma$) achieves the lowest cumulative
regret ($134.4$), matching fixed $\gamma{=}0.999$ ($134.4$)
and outperforming all ablations:
No~forgetting ($152.7$, ${+}13.6\%$),
Tabula~Rasa ($155.7$, ${+}15.8\%$),
and Fast~forgetting ($159.9$, ${+}19.0\%$).

The regret decomposes cleanly by phase:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Condition & Phase~1 & Phase~2 & Total \\
\midrule
\textbf{BanditGPT (adaptive $\gamma$)} & 61.7 & 72.7 & \textbf{134.4} \\
BanditGPT ($\gamma{=}0.999$) & 61.9 & 72.6 & 134.4 \\
No forgetting ($\gamma{=}1.0$) & 59.1 & 93.6 & 152.7 \\
Fast forgetting ($\gamma{=}0.995$) & 72.2 & 87.6 & 159.9 \\
Tabula Rasa & 76.8 & 79.0 & 155.7 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
BanditGPT wins because it \emph{combines} two advantages that
no ablation achieves simultaneously:
(i)~warmup priors reduce Phase~1 regret by 20\% vs.\ Tabula~Rasa
($61.7$ vs.\ $76.8$), and
(ii)~the forgetting factor reduces Phase~2 regret by 22\%
vs.\ No~forgetting ($72.7$ vs.\ $93.6$).
Removing either component sacrifices one benefit without
recovering the other.

\paragraph{Forgetting rate sensitivity.}
Fast~forgetting ($\gamma{=}0.995$) has a ${\sim}200$-step
effective memory, which provides rapid adaptation but permanently
limits the effective sample size.
This manifests as elevated exploration: at step~1{,}785,
Fast~forgetting allocates only 45\% to Llama (vs.\ 69\% for
BanditGPT) because the UCB exploration bonus remains large when
$A_a$ has few effective observations.
A moderate forgetting rate ($\gamma{=}0.999$) strikes the right
balance: it forgets stale evidence over ${\sim}1{,}000$ steps
while retaining enough effective observations for confident
exploitation.

\subsubsection{Experiment 02b: Pricing Restructure}
\label{sec:cost_shift}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We simulate the most common non-stationarity in production LLM
routing: a provider pricing change.  The same $K{=}3$ portfolio,
data, and warmup priors are used.

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Phase~1} (steps 1--893): Normal pricing.
    Gemini-Pro is the most expensive model (normalized cost
    $\tilde{c}=0.67$); Mistral-Large is utility-best ($0.840$).
    Gemini is rarely selected due to the cost penalty.

  \item \textbf{Phase~2} (steps 894--1{,}785): \textbf{Gemini price drop}.
    Gemini's pricing is reduced from \$1.25/\$10.0 to
    \$0.10/\$0.10 per million tokens (input/output), matching
    Llama-level pricing.  The router's model registry is updated
    at the boundary; the next \texttt{route()} call immediately
    computes the new normalized cost ($\tilde{c} \approx 0.0$).
    Post-drop, Gemini is utility-best
    ($0.942 = 0.933 - 0.20 \times 0.0$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Critically, \emph{rewards do not change}---only costs do.
The reward estimates $\hat\theta_a$ learned in Phase~1 remain
valid in Phase~2.

\paragraph{Results (Figure~\ref{fig:cost_shift_regret}).}
The ranking \emph{partially reverses} compared to the reward shift:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
Condition & Regret & Gemini \% & \\
\midrule
No forgetting ($\gamma{=}1.0$) & \textbf{73.7} & 95\% & \\
\textbf{BanditGPT (adaptive $\gamma$)} & 84.5 & 69\% & ${+}15\%$ \\
BanditGPT ($\gamma{=}0.999$) & 95.6 & 59\% & ${+}30\%$ \\
Tabula Rasa & 106.1 & 73\% & ${+}44\%$ \\
Fast forgetting ($\gamma{=}0.995$) & 138.3 & 41\% & ${+}88\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
No~forgetting wins because the cost change is an
\emph{externally observable signal}: the registry update
shifts Gemini's UCB score by
$\lambda_s \times 0.67 = +0.134$ immediately, without requiring
any update to $\hat\theta$.
The accurate Phase~1 reward estimates, combined with Gemini's
natural UCB exploration bonus from under-exploration (high
$x^\top A_{\mathrm{Gemini}}^{-1} x$), drive rapid convergence
to the new optimum.

Fixed forgetting ($\gamma{=}0.999$) is actively harmful: it decays the
\emph{still-valid} $\hat\theta$ estimates, increasing posterior
variance for all arms and inducing unnecessary exploration.
BanditGPT ($\gamma{=}0.999$) reaches only 59\% Gemini at
step~1{,}785 vs.\ 95\% for No~forgetting.

Crucially, adaptive~$\gamma$ \emph{partially mitigates} this:
by detecting that prediction residuals have not increased
(Figure~\ref{fig:gamma_trajectory}), the adaptive mechanism
largely suppresses forgetting, reaching 69\% Gemini and saving
11.1 regret points vs.\ fixed $\gamma{=}0.999$
($p < 10^{-12}$, paired $t$-test).

it under cost shift ($\Delta{=}{-}11.1$, $p < 10^{-12}$).
This confirms that the residual-based detector correctly
discriminates between shift types.

\subsubsection{Adaptive Forgetting Factor}
\label{sec:adaptive_gamma}

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
The experiments above reveal a fundamental tension: fixed
$\gamma{=}0.999$ is optimal for reward shifts but harmful for cost
shifts, while $\gamma{=}1.0$ is optimal for cost shifts but
cannot adapt to reward shifts.
To resolve this, BanditGPT implements an \emph{adaptive forgetting
factor} $\gamma_t$ that adjusts automatically based on the
router's own prediction accuracy, inspired by ADWIN-style adaptive
windowing~\cite{bifet2007adwin} and dual-EMA change
detection~\cite{cavenaghi2021nonstationary}.

At each step $t$, after observing reward $r_t$ for arm $a_t$, the
mechanism computes the absolute prediction residual
$e_t = |r_t - \hat\theta_{a_t}^\top x_t|$
and updates two exponential moving averages per arm:
\begin{align}
    \mu_{\text{short}}[a] &\mathrel{+}= \alpha_s \cdot (e_t - \mu_{\text{short}}[a])
    \label{eq:ema_short} \\
    \mu_{\text{long}}[a]  &\mathrel{+}= \alpha_l \cdot (e_t - \mu_{\text{long}}[a])
    \label{eq:ema_long}
\end{align}
with $\alpha_s{=}0.3$ (fast, ${\sim}3$-step half-life) and
$\alpha_l{=}0.05$ (slow, ${\sim}14$-step half-life).
The per-arm shift estimate is:
\begin{equation}
    s_k = \max\!\bigl(0,\;
    \mu_{\text{short}}[k] - \mu_{\text{long}}[k] - m\bigr)
    \label{eq:shift_estimate}
\end{equation}
where $m$ is a self-calibrated noise margin (dead zone) equal to
$2\times$ the theoretical standard deviation of the EMA difference
under stationarity.  This margin prevents $\gamma$ from
oscillating in stable regimes while preserving sensitivity to
real shifts.

The forgetting factor is set as:
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_t = \max\!\Bigl(0.999,\;
    1 - \frac{\max_k s_k}{\delta}\Bigr)
    \label{eq:adaptive_gamma}
\end{equation}
where $\delta$ is the population standard deviation of prediction
residuals during the burn-in phase.

\paragraph{Behaviour (Figure~\ref{fig:gamma_trajectory}).}
Under reward shift, $\gamma_t$ drops sharply from $1.0$ toward
$0.999$ near the phase boundary (step~${\sim}950$) as prediction
residuals spike for the arms whose reward distributions have
changed.  Once predictions adapt, residuals return to baseline
and $\gamma_t$ recovers toward $1.0$---a
\emph{self-correcting} response.
Under cost shift, $\gamma_t$ remains near $1.0$ throughout the
experiment because reward predictions remain accurate (only costs
changed); the mechanism correctly avoids triggering unnecessary
forgetting.

This asymmetric response is the fundamental advantage over
fixed schedules: the same mechanism provides $\gamma{=}0.999$-level
adaptation when needed and $\gamma{=}1.0$-level stability when
forgetting would be harmful, without any manual tuning or external
change-detection signal.

\subsubsection{Synthesis: The Nature of Non-Stationarity Matters}
\label{sec:nonstat_synthesis}

\paragraph{Key finding.}
Experiments 02a and 02b reveal a fundamental tension that the
adaptive mechanism resolves:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Change type & Fixed $\gamma{=}0.999$ & Adaptive $\gamma$ & Mechanism \\
\midrule
Reward shift & \cmark~best & \cmark~matches & Detects residual spike \\
Cost shift & \xmark~harmful & $\sim$~partial & Suppresses forgetting \\
\emph{Both (ideal)} & \xmark~compromise & \cmark & Adapts per shift type \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The adaptive mechanism does not achieve the theoretical optimum
under cost shift (No~forgetting remains 13\% better) because
transient residual fluctuations during arm-switching cause
brief $\gamma$ dips even when the reward model is valid.
This is a known limitation of residual-based
detectors~\cite{cheung2022nonstationary}: they conflate
arm-switching induced variance with genuine distribution shift.
Nevertheless, the 11.1-point improvement over fixed $\gamma{=}0.999$
($p < 10^{-12}$) demonstrates meaningful discrimination between
shift types.

\paragraph{Implication for production.}
In production deployments, both types of change occur
unpredictably---model updates affect reward quality while
pricing changes affect cost.  The adaptive forgetting factor
provides a robust default that:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item matches hand-tuned $\gamma{=}0.999$ when reward shifts
    occur (0\% regret overhead, $p{=}0.98$);
  \item significantly reduces forgetting damage when only costs
    change ($-11.6\%$ regret vs.\ fixed $\gamma{=}0.999$,
    $p < 10^{-12}$);
  \item requires no manual threshold tuning---the noise margin
    and scale parameter are calibrated from the burn-in phase.
\end{itemize}
The BudgetPacer (Section~\ref{sec:budget_pacing}) operates
orthogonally on the dual variable $\lambda_t$, composing
naturally with the adaptive forgetting factor.
