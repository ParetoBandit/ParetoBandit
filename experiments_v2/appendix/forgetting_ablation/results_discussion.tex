\subsection{Forgetting Factor Ablation}
\label{app:forgetting_ablation}

The main-text experiments (Sections~\ref{sec:reward_shift}
and~\ref{sec:budget_drift}) compare three routing strategies of
increasing sophistication: Fixed Policy, Naive Bandit, and
BanditGPT.
Here we report the full $\gamma$ sensitivity analysis that informed
the choice of $\gamma{=}0.997$ and justify removing the
intermediate ablation conditions from the main figures.

\paragraph{Reward shift (Experiment~02 scenario).}
Table~\ref{tab:gamma_ablation} presents the complete $\gamma$
sweep on the same two-phase reward-swap setup used in
Experiment~02 (K=3, warmup priors, 20~seeds).

\noindent
The warmup-prior conditions form a U-shaped regret curve with
a minimum at $\gamma{=}0.997$ (total regret $125.5$).
Aggressive forgetting ($\gamma{=}0.99$) erases too much learned
evidence, inflating Phase~1 regret ($75.2$ vs.\ $69.1$).
No forgetting ($\gamma{=}1.0$) retains stale Phase~1 evidence,
inflating Phase~2 regret ($103.0$ vs.\ $54.3$).
The jointly-tuned $\gamma{=}0.997$ minimizes the sum.

The Tabula Rasa conditions (no priors) achieve lower Phase~2
regret because they carry no stale prior information, but their
Phase~1 regret is $35$--$45\%$ higher than warmup conditions.
This is the key tradeoff motivating the warmup + forgetting
combination presented in the main text.

\paragraph{Relationship to main-text conditions.}
The three main-text conditions map to rows in this table:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Fixed Policy (offline)} $\approx$ Warmup +
    $\gamma{=}1.0$ without online learning (regret $208.4$
    in the main text, higher than the Warmup $\gamma{=}1.0$ row
    because the Fixed Policy never calls \texttt{process\_feedback}).
  \item \textbf{Naive Bandit ($\gamma{=}1.0$)} $=$ Warmup +
    $\gamma{=}1.0$ row ($172.1$ in this table; $190.6$ in the
    main text with 40~seeds).
  \item \textbf{BanditGPT ($\gamma{=}0.997$)} $=$ Warmup +
    $\gamma{=}0.997$ row ($125.5$ here; $120.0$ with 40~seeds).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
The full sensitivity analysis confirms that $\gamma{=}0.997$ is
optimal and that the 3-baseline comparison in the main text
captures the essential performance ordering.
