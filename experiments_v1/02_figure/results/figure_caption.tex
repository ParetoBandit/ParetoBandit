\caption{\textbf{Calibration Convergence Analysis: Policy Shift Occurs During Calibration, Not Holdout.} 
We compare three frozen router policies to isolate convergence effects: (1) Warmup Only uses pre-trained priors from 80K RouteLLM samples with GPT-4-Turbo; (2) $\gamma$-Scaled applies covariance inflation ($\gamma=0.01$) to weaken priors without calibration data; (3) Fully Calibrated incorporates 1,121 target-domain samples with GPT-4o after $\gamma$-scaling. 
\textbf{(a) Policy Convergence:} Strong model usage drops from 100\% to 0.3\%, a 99.7 percentage point shift representing 95\% cost savings (\$7.50 $\rightarrow$ \$0.40 per 750 queries). Critically, $\gamma$-scaling alone (Scenario 1 $\rightarrow$ 2) produces minimal change (0.3 pp), while calibration (Scenario 2 $\rightarrow$ 3) drives the full convergence (99.4 pp). This demonstrates that convergence occurs \emph{during calibration}, not during holdout evaluation.
\textbf{(b) Quality-Cost Tradeoff:} The router trades 14.8\% quality reduction (0.971 $\rightarrow$ 0.823 win rate) for massive cost savings, discovering that the weak model (Mixtral-8x7B) suffices for 99.7\% of target-domain queries—a rational trade-off for cost-sensitive applications.
\textbf{(c) Bayesian Plasticity:} Effective sample size (Eff. N) quantifies the router's confidence: 426 (highly rigid) $\rightarrow$ 4 (highly uncertain) $\rightarrow$ 16 (moderately confident). The $\gamma$-scaling reduces prior strength by 99\%, making the router receptive to new patterns. Calibration data then adds +12 effective samples (2.78$\times$ the weakened prior), sufficient to dominate the posterior and reverse the routing strategy. This validates our design: covariance inflation creates plasticity; calibration data drives adaptation.
\textbf{Key Finding:} Cross-model transfer (GPT-4-Turbo $\rightarrow$ GPT-4o) succeeds despite capability-tier substitution, demonstrating practical robustness to model evolution.}
