    \caption{\textbf{Short-Term Model Adoption via Semantic Transfer (Conservative Learning Regime).} \textbf{(Left)} Four-way ablation on full task distribution ($N=30$ trials). Cold Start (red) and Warmup Only (orange) represent unrealistic baselines. Small LMSys Prior (blue) simulates realistic production deployment with $N_{\text{eff}}=20$ preliminary benchmarks. Semantic Transfer (green, our method) inherits preferences from GPT-4-Turbo. GPT-5.1 Release occurs at $t=300$. \textbf{Task characteristics:} GPT-5.1 wins only 19.6\% vs GPT-4, with 71.5\% ties, indicating low task variance. Results demonstrate \textit{short-term adoption speed} improvement rather than full contextual routing. \textbf{(Right)} Production router with heterogeneous experts strategy using \textbf{conservative meta-learning rate} ($\eta=0.1$). Meta-learner dynamics (bottom panel) show Conservative expert (blue, with semantic transfer) maintains $\sim$75\% weight throughout, while Adaptive expert (red, cold start) provides $\sim$25\% exploration capacity. \textbf{Critical Note:} Stable weights do NOT validate semantic transfer correctness—they indicate insufficient adaptation due to low learning rate. Comparison with Figure~\ref{fig:corralling_semantic} ($\eta=5.0$, 50$\times$ higher) shows that with aggressive learning, semantic transfer priors are ultimately \emph{unlearned}. This experiment demonstrates \emph{short-term} benefit (cold-start mitigation), while Figure~\ref{fig:corralling_semantic} demonstrates \emph{long-term} adaptation (convergence to optimal policy). Full-episode metrics reported to avoid evaluation bias.}
