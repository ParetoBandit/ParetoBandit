\section{Hyperparameter Robustness}
\label{appendix:hyperparameter_robustness}

To assess the robustness of Latent Semantic Transfer, we swept the effective prior 
strength $n_{\text{eff}} \in [1.0, 20.0]$. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity}, 
the performance trajectories for all $n_{\text{eff}}$ values overlap almost perfectly 
and consistently outperform the Cold Start baseline (Red Line). This confirms that 
the system's effectiveness is driven by the accuracy of the transferred mean 
($\theta_{\text{neighbor}}$), rather than the specific tightness of the prior 
confidence ($\mathbf{A}_0$). Consequently, the framework is robust to hyperparameter 
selection, requiring no fine-tuning to achieve Zero-Shot Readiness.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/figure7_sensitivity.png}
\caption{%
\textbf{Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis.} Performance trajectories for 
$n_{\text{eff}} \in \{1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0\}$ (Blue Lines) all overlap 
and consistently outperform Cold Start (Red Line). At $t=300$, GPT-5.1 is 
released with knowledge transferred from GPT-4-Turbo. All transfer configurations 
avoid the catastrophic Cold Start dip, demonstrating robustness across a 20$\times$ 
range of prior strengths.
}
\label{fig:sensitivity}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Interpretation}

The perfect overlap of all $n_{\text{eff}}$ curves demonstrates that the 
Bayesian formulation correctly preserves the mean prediction 
$\hat{\theta} = \theta_{\text{neighbor}}$ while scaling confidence 
(variance $\propto 1/n_{\text{eff}}$). When the semantic neighbor is 
accurate, performance is determined by the quality of the transferred 
knowledge, not the hyperparameter choice. This robustness is a fundamental 
property of the principled Bayesian transfer mechanism, not an artifact 
of careful tuning.
