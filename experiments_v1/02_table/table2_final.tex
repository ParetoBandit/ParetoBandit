\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Interpretation.}
Under moderate distribution shift (PSI=0.225; Figure~\ref{fig:distribution_shift}), warmup priors trained on offline RouteLLM battles remain well-calibrated for LMSYS Arena holdout prompts.
The warmup baseline achieves the lowest regret (32.9), outperforming tabula rasa (45.6) by 28\%.
Corralling hedges between the two experts, incurring 1--4\% overhead relative to tabula rasa.
This overhead represents the \emph{cost of safety insurance}: when priors are already well-matched to the target domain (as here), the sample-splitting cost of maintaining two parallel experts exceeds the benefit of hedging. However, Corralling would provide protection in scenarios with severe prior mismatch (e.g., cross-domain deployment to medical or legal domains), where warmup-only strategies could fail catastrophically.

\paragraph{Data-Order Sensitivity.}
All strategies exhibit variance across seeds due to data-order shuffling (N=10 seeds), 
confirming that online bandit regret is sensitive to prompt arrival order.
Warmup shows the lowest variance (std=2.6), suggesting that good priors reduce sensitivity to data ordering.
Tabula Rasa and Corralling show higher variance (std=5.8--7.1), reflecting the stochasticity of cold-start learning and expert selection.

\paragraph{Learning Rate Comparison.}
Both learning rates perform comparably: $\eta=1.0$ achieves 45.1 regret vs $\eta=0.1$ at 47.3 regret.
The similarity confirms that under moderate shift with already-good priors, the hedging overhead dominates over learning rate effects.
