\textbf{How to Read This Table:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Effective N} ($N_{\text{eff}} = \gamma \times 80{,}000$): How many ``equivalent samples'' the warmup contributes after scaling. At $\gamma=0.01$, your 80k warmup acts like 800 samples---helpful but not overwhelming.
    \item \textbf{Calib/Prior Ratio} ($\frac{1{,}121}{N_{\text{eff}}}$): Who's in charge? Ratio $<$0.5 means warmup dominates (no adaptation). Ratio $\approx$1.0 means balanced influence (ideal). Ratio $>$2.0 means calibration dominates (warmup wasted).
    \item \textbf{Strong\%}: Percentage of times GPT-4-Turbo selected. Baseline (53.3\% at $\gamma=1.0$) shows warmup alone is insufficient. Target: 65--68\% after adaptation.
    \item \textbf{Avg Reward}: Quality on calibration data. Zero reward ($\gamma=1.0$) means router is ``stuck'' at warmup policy---not adapting. Higher is better, but watch for overfitting at very low $\gamma$.
    \item \textbf{Conv. Rate}: How fast the policy stabilizes. Higher = faster learning, but too fast may sacrifice warmup benefits. Sweet spot: 0.01--0.02.
    \item \textbf{Why $\gamma=0.010$ (highlighted)?} Achieves balanced influence (ratio 1.401), increases GPT-4 usage by 12.4 pp, maintains quality (0.2284 reward), and stable convergence. Use this for production.
    \item \textbf{Why not $\gamma=1.0$?} Warmup dominates completely (80k $\gg$ 1.1k). Router cannot adapt, resulting in 0.0 reward. Don't use this!
    \item \textbf{Why not $\gamma=0.05$?} Fastest convergence (0.01346) and highest GPT-4 usage (68.3\%), but lower quality (0.1302). Use if you need aggressive adaptation.
\end{itemize}
