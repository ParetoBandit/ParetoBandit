\item \textbf{Sensitivity Analysis (Regime-Dependent Effects)}: Through multi-seed validation and ablation studies (Corralling ON vs OFF), we demonstrate that sensitivity to the effective prior strength parameter ($\neff$) is regime-dependent. When semantic transfer is forced (Corralling disabled), we observe a 6.2\% performance span from $\neff=1.0$ (optimal) to $\neff=20.0$ (worst), confirming the ``over-confidence trap'' where strong priors reduce exploration of expensive new models. However, in the production system with Corralling enabled, we observe no significant differences across $\neff$ values ($p>0.40$) due to binary regime switching: Corralling maintains semantic transfer in 33\% of seeds (where priors match data) and abandons it in 67\% of seeds (where priors mismatch data, characterized by 71.5\% ties). Stratified analysis reveals that $\neff$ effects are present (+4.6\%) only when semantic transfer is used, demonstrating that robustness emerges from Corralling's ability to detect and abandon failing priors rather than from universal parameter insensitivity. Expected production impact: $0.33 \times 4.6\% \approx 1.5\%$, highlighting the value of meta-learning for selective strategy application.
