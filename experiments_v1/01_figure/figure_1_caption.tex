        \textbf{The Geography of Difficulty: Semantic Task Specialization in Latent Space.}
        (Left) We project 80,000 RouteLLM prompt embeddings into a 2D PCA space (32 components capturing 35.14\% variance), visualizing task difficulty as a heatmap of the hard/easy density ratio.
        The visualization reveals a \textbf{Core-Shell Architecture}: a high-density ``Hard'' perimeter (Red, 68.6\% of data, $\Delta = 1.0$) encapsulating a varied ``Easy/Mixed'' core (Blue/White, 22.1\% easy, $\Delta = 0.0$).
        This geography proves that high-difficulty tasks cluster in semantically distinct neighborhoods---specialized domains, complex logic, technical edge cases---while general conversational tasks occupy the center.
        (Right) The bimodal distribution bar chart confirms a \textbf{Decisive Bimodality}: exactly 0.0\% of prompts fall in the moderate range ($0.3 < \Delta \le 0.6$), creating an absolute vacuum that justifies our discrete mode-isolation approach over continuous regressors.
        The 68.6\% ``Hard'' skew represents the \textbf{RouteLLM Anchor}---a legacy constraint from human preference bias that BanditGPT overcomes via Bayesian Recalibration.
