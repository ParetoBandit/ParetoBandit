\caption{\textbf{Routing Signal Validation on Held-Out Prompts ($N=750$).}
This figure validates the production router's feature extraction component: the PCA artifact loaded by \texttt{FeatureService.extract\_features()} (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\textbf{(A)}~PC1 projection (domain-adapted router PCA, trained on 80K RouteLLM battle prompts---an independent dataset from the held-out evaluation prompts) vs.\ reward gap $(R_{\text{GPT-4-Turbo}} - R_{\text{Mixtral}})$.
Outcomes are discrete (win${=}{+}1$, tie${=}0$, loss${=}{-}1$); the running mean smooths proportions for visual clarity.
The dashed line marks the silhouette-optimal threshold, selected without reference to reward labels.
\textbf{(B)}~Outcome proportions by PC1 region.
The Low~PC1 region (blue) is dominated by ties ($\sim$82\%); the High~PC1 region (red) shows $\sim$62\% Mixtral wins---a qualitatively different outcome distribution.
Cram\'er's $V$, odds ratio, and risk difference are reported as primary effect sizes (appropriate for categorical outcomes); a permutation test ($n{=}10{,}000$) provides distribution-free significance.
The router PCA ($V{=}0.667$) captures $6.5{\times}$ more signal than the median of 100 independent random projections ($V_{\text{random}}$ median${=}0.103$; Table~\ref{tab:pca_conditions}), confirming that the PCA concentrates routing-relevant variance rather than finding spurious correlation in a high-dimensional projection.
Note: these proportions are specific to this holdout sample ($\sim$5.9\% High~PC1 in the 1M dataset; see Section~\ref{sec:scale_validation}).
``Regions'' refer to segments of the PC1 axis, not well-separated groups in the full 384D embedding space (Section~\ref{sec:validation_methodology}).}
\label{fig:lmsys_holdout_structure}
\end{figure}
