\subsection{Scale Validation: Spatial Structure Persistence}
\label{sec:scale_validation}

To examine whether the spatial structure detected in the held-out set persists at scale, we analyzed the complete LMSYS Chat-1M dataset~\cite{zheng2023lmsys}, comprising 594,199 unique prompts---a 317$\times$ increase from the holdout evaluation ($N=750$).

\subsubsection{Spatial Stability at Scale}

The principal component structure exhibits stability across scale. Table~\ref{tab:scale_comparison} compares the PCA decomposition across both datasets.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Spatial Structure Comparison: Holdout vs. Full 1M Dataset}
\label{tab:scale_comparison}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Holdout} & \textbf{Chat-1M} \\
& $(N=750)$ & $(N=594,199)$ \\
\midrule
PC1 Variance & 3.10\% & 3.101\% \\
PC2 Variance & 2.29\% & 2.294\% \\
\midrule
Low PC1 (\%) & 80.8\% & \textbf{94.1\%} \\
High PC1 (\%) & 19.2\% & \textbf{5.9\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent\textbf{Key Observation:} The principal components and spatial clustering persist across a 317$\times$ scale increase. This is expected---PCA is a fixed linear projection applied to embeddings from the same sentence transformer model---and demonstrates that the spatial structure is a stable property of the prompt distribution.

\noindent\textbf{Critical Limitation:} The Chat-1M dataset \textbf{lacks reward labels}. We cannot validate whether the model preference heterogeneity (the reward gap inversion observed on the holdout set) persists at scale. The spatial stability observed here should not be interpreted as evidence that performance claims generalize to unlabeled production traffic.

\noindent\textbf{Data Provenance:} The holdout evaluation ($N=750$) includes reward labels for both Mixtral and GPT-4-Turbo from LMSYS Chatbot Arena human preference evaluations, enabling validation of the reward-semantic correspondence. The 1M dataset represents broader production traffic but lacks these evaluations. All performance metrics reported in our main results are based exclusively on the labeled holdout set.

\subsubsection{Distribution Shift Interpretation}

The Chat-1M dataset shows a pronounced shift toward Low PC1 (80.8\% $\rightarrow$ 94.1\%). The High PC1 cluster (strict-constraint prompts) shrinks from 19.2\% to 5.9\% of production traffic. This distribution difference could reflect:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item Different prompt sampling characteristics between the two datasets
    \item User behavior differences across collection periods
    \item The holdout set over-representing strict-constraint templates relative to production traffic
\end{itemize}

Without reward labels for the 1M dataset, we cannot determine which interpretation is correct.

\subsubsection{Summary}

The 1M scale analysis demonstrates:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Spatial stability:} PCA structure and cluster proportions persist at scale (expected property of fixed linear projection)
    \item \textbf{Distribution shift:} High PC1 cluster is rarer in production traffic (5.9\% vs 19.2\%)
    \item \textbf{Limitation:} Cannot validate reward-preference correspondence without 1M reward labels
    \item \textbf{Performance basis:} All claims based on labeled holdout ($N=750$) only
\end{enumerate}
