\subsection{Scale Validation: Spatial Structure Persistence}
\label{sec:scale_validation}

To validate the robustness of our semantic routing approach at scale, we analyzed the complete LMSYS Chat-1M dataset~\cite{zheng2023lmsys}, comprising 594,199 unique prompts---a 317$\times$ increase from the holdout evaluation ($N=1,871$).

\subsubsection{Spatial Invariance at Scale}

The principal component structure exhibits remarkable stability across scale. Table~\ref{tab:scale_comparison} compares the PCA decomposition and spatial clustering across both datasets.

\noindent\textbf{Key Finding:} The principal components and the $PC1 = 0.3$ spatial boundary remain stable across a 317$\times$ scale increase. This demonstrates that the PCA decomposition captures robust structural properties of the prompt distribution.

\noindent\textbf{Data Provenance:} The holdout evaluation ($N=1,871$) includes reward labels for both Mixtral and GPT-4-Turbo, enabling validation of the reward-semantic correspondence. The 1M dataset represents unfiltered production traffic but lacks reward evaluations, limiting analysis to spatial structure. Performance metrics reported in our main results are based on the labeled holdout set.

\noindent\textbf{Threshold Selection:} We validated the $PC1 = 0.3$ boundary through systematic analysis: grid search over 50 thresholds identified $0.317$ as optimal by composite score; unsupervised methods (k-means, GMM) converged to $0.320 \pm 0.105$; sensitivity analysis confirms robustness across $[0.2, 0.4]$ (all $p < 10^{-100}$). The threshold achieves strong cluster quality (silhouette: $0.495$) and lies within one standard deviation of the optimal mean.

\subsubsection{Distribution Shift and Interpretation}

The Chat-1M dataset shows a pronounced distribution shift toward Low PC1 (82.4\% $\rightarrow$ 94.1\%). While we validated the reward-semantic correspondence on the holdout set, we lack reward evaluations for the 1M dataset to confirm this mapping persists.

\paragraph{Interpreting Distribution Shift.}
The Chat-1M dataset exhibits a pronounced shift toward Low PC1 (82.4\% $\rightarrow$ 94.1\%). The spatial consistency (stable PC1 variance and boundary location) suggests the PCA decomposition captures robust structural properties of prompt distributions. However, semantic interpretation of this shift requires reward labels. The distribution shift could reflect differences in prompt difficulty, user behavior evolution, or sampling characteristics between the two datasets.

\paragraph{Validation Strategy.}
We base our performance claims on the holdout set ($N=1,871$), which provides validated reward-semantic correspondence through labeled evaluations. The 1M analysis serves to demonstrate spatial structure robustness at scale, providing supporting evidence that the PCA decomposition generalizes beyond the holdout sample.

\textbf{Methodological Transparency.}
By explicitly separating validated (holdout with reward labels) from exploratory (1M spatial clustering only) analyses, we provide clear ground truth for reproducibility while documenting scale-dependent observations that merit future investigation with production labels.

\subsubsection{Performance Evaluation and Generalization}

\paragraph{Validated Performance.}
Our primary evaluation on the labeled holdout set ($N=1,871$) demonstrates 27\% cost reduction with minimal quality degradation. The 17.6\% High PC1 prompts in this dataset validate the alignment tax phenomenon with strong statistical evidence (Mann-Whitney $p < 10^{-143}$, Cohen's $d = 1.90$).

\paragraph{Scale Generalization.}
The spatial structure persistence at 317$\times$ scale suggests the PCA decomposition captures robust properties of prompt distributions. The distribution shift to 94.1\% Low PC1 in production traffic indicates potential for even greater cost savings, conditional on the spatial-semantic correspondence extending to unlabeled production data.

\subsubsection{Robustness Analysis}

Beyond the primary holdout evaluation, we conducted additional validation to ensure methodological rigor:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Scale robustness:} Spatial structure stable at 317$\times$ scale (PC1 variance: 3.10\% $\rightarrow$ 3.101\%)
    \item \textbf{Threshold validation:} Grid search and unsupervised clustering confirm $PC1 = 0.3$ is principled (optimal: $0.320 \pm 0.105$)
    \item \textbf{High-dimensional validation:} Structure persists in 384D space ($\rho = -0.395$, $p < 10^{-70}$)
    \item \textbf{Data quality:} Zero exact duplicates, diversity score: 0.355 for High PC1 cluster
\end{itemize}

These analyses validate that findings are statistically robust, not artifacts of dimensionality reduction, threshold selection, or data peculiarities.

\subsubsection{Data and Methods}

\paragraph{Datasets.} 
\textbf{Holdout Set ($N=1,871$):} Stratified split from LMSYS Arena battles with Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct and GPT-4-Turbo evaluations. Reward labels enable validation of reward-semantic correspondence. Used for primary performance evaluation.

\textbf{Chat-1M Dataset ($N=594,199$):} Complete LMSYS Chatbot Arena conversations (April--August 2023) representing unfiltered production traffic. Used for spatial structure robustness analysis at scale.

\paragraph{PCA Model.} Trained on 80K RouteLLM battles (Mixtral vs. GPT-4-Turbo), reducing 384D sentence embeddings to 32D (35.14\% variance explained). Applied consistently to both datasets for comparable spatial analysis.
