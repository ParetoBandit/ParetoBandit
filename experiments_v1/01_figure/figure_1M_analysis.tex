\subsection{Scale Validation: Spatial Structure Persistence}
\label{sec:scale_validation}

The routing signal analysis (Section~\ref{sec:semantic_structure}) uses $N=750$ held-out prompts with reward labels.
A natural question for production deployment is: \emph{does the spatial structure persist at scale, and what fraction of real traffic falls in the routable region?}
We address the spatial question using the LMSYS Chat-1M dataset~\cite{zheng2023lmsys} (594,199 unique prompts, a $317\times$ increase).

\subsubsection{Why This Matters for Deployment}

Users deploying BanditGPT need to estimate how much of their traffic will benefit from routing.
The holdout analysis identifies $\sim$19.2\% of prompts in the high-signal region, but the holdout was constructed via stratified sampling that may over-represent prompts with clear model preferences.
The 1M dataset, drawn from broader production traffic, provides a more realistic estimate of the routable fraction---though it lacks reward labels, so we can only assess \emph{spatial} structure, not reward patterns.

\subsubsection{Spatial Stability at Scale}

The PCA projection is stable: PC1 and PC2 variance ratios are identical to three decimal places across a $317\times$ scale increase.
This is expected---PCA is a fixed linear transformation applied to embeddings from the same encoder---but confirms that the feature space is a stable property of the prompt distribution, not a small-sample artifact.

\subsubsection{Production Routable Fraction}

The High~PC1 region shrinks from 19.2\% (holdout) to 5.9\% (1M dataset).
This distribution shift has direct implications for production cost savings:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Upper bound on routing benefit:} If the reward pattern from the holdout generalizes (untested), at most $\sim$5.9\% of production prompts would fall in the high-signal region where the cheaper model is strongly preferred.
    \item \textbf{Tie-dominated majority:} The remaining $\sim$94\% of prompts are in the Low~PC1 region, which is dominated by ties ($\sim$82\% in the holdout). For these, both models perform comparably, and the cheaper model can be used with minimal quality impact---this is actually the \emph{primary} source of cost savings in practice.
    \item \textbf{Conservative deployment estimate:} Users should plan around the 5.9\% figure for the clearly-routable fraction, not the 19.2\% holdout figure.
\end{itemize}

The discrepancy likely reflects the holdout's stratified sampling, which enriches for prompts with clear model preferences to ensure adequate statistical power.
Production traffic is naturally dominated by prompts where models perform similarly.

\paragraph{Critical limitation.}
The Chat-1M dataset \textbf{lacks reward labels}.
We cannot validate whether the model preference heterogeneity observed in the holdout persists at scale.
Spatial stability should not be interpreted as evidence that reward patterns generalize.
All performance claims are based exclusively on the labeled holdout ($N=750$).

\subsubsection{Practical Guidance for Users}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Expect $\sim$5--6\% clearly-routable prompts} in general-purpose traffic. The majority of prompts are ties where the cheaper model suffices.
    \item \textbf{Domain-specific traffic may differ.} If your deployment is enriched for structured-output or instruction-following prompts (the High~PC1 category), the routable fraction could be higher.
    \item \textbf{The bandit adapts.} Even if the initial PCA-based estimate of routable fraction is off, the LinUCB bandit learns from online feedback. The PCA provides a useful starting point; the bandit corrects over time.
    \item \textbf{Monitor your traffic distribution.} After deployment, compare your prompt distribution against the PCA projection to estimate your actual routable fraction. The \texttt{FeatureService.extract\_features()} method returns the same PCA coordinates used in this analysis.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Summary}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep,topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Spatial stability:} PCA structure persists at $317\times$ scale (expected for fixed linear projection)
    \item \textbf{Production estimate:} $\sim$5.9\% of traffic in High~PC1 region (vs.\ 19.2\% in holdout)
    \item \textbf{Primary cost savings:} From the $\sim$94\% tie-dominated majority, where the cheaper model can serve traffic with comparable quality
    \item \textbf{Limitation:} Cannot validate reward patterns without 1M reward labels
\end{enumerate}
