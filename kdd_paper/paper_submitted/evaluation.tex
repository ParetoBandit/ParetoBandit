\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate \ours{} along three dimensions critical for production deployment: cold-start efficiency, plasticity under concept drift, and cost--quality alignment against state-of-the-art baselines.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Dataset \& Models.} We utilize 497 archetypal prompts distilled from the LMSYS Chatbot Arena~\cite{zheng2023judging} to train priors, and evaluate on a held-out set of 2,000 prompts sampled via K-Means clustering. The routing pool consists of 81 production LLMs accessed via OpenRouter, spanning frontier generalists (e.g., GPT-4o, GPT-5.1, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 3 Pro, Grok-4) and cost-optimized specialists (e.g., Gemini Flash Lite, DeepSeek-V3, Nova-Lite, Nova-Micro).

\paragraph{Ground Truth \& Evaluation Strategy.} We utilize GPT-4o-as-judge for scoring model outputs. This choice is motivated by three factors that ensure both internal consistency and adversarial robustness:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Human Alignment:} Our calibration confirms that GPT-4o achieves $>$80\% agreement with human preferences on the LMSYS Chatbot Arena~\cite{zheng2023judging}, serving as a robust proxy for user satisfaction.
    
    \item \textbf{Teacher-Student Consistency:} Since our ``Shippable Priors'' are distilled from GPT-4o supervision, utilizing the same model for evaluation ensures that measured regret reflects the router's learning efficiency rather than alignment noise between disparate teacher models.
    
    \item \textbf{Adversarial Baseline (Conservative Bias):} Utilizing a frontier model to judge itself introduces a risk of self-preference bias, where the teacher favors its own outputs. In our context, this acts as a \emph{conservative constraint}: it artificially inflates the baseline score of the expensive expert. Therefore, when \ours{} identifies a cheaper specialist (e.g., Nova-Lite) that the judge rates higher than itself, the efficiency gain is highly credible.
    
    \item \textbf{Validation via Benchmarks:} To ensure the system is not merely overfitting to the teacher's bias, we cross-reference our results with external ground-truth benchmarks (\S\ref{sec:rq3}). These experiments confirm that the bandit successfully overcomes the initial ``Conservative Bias'' of the priors, diverging from the teacher's preferences to discover cost-effective specialists that the teacher initially underestimated.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Metrics.} Cumulative regret: $\sum_{t=1}^{T}(r^* - r_t)$; regret reduction vs.\ cold-start; and system accuracy (macro-average of Math, Reasoning, and MMLU; excluding HumanEval due to sparse coverage).\footnote{All routing decisions use the production BanditRouter codebase. Model success is modeled via deterministic Bernoulli sampling based on Artificial Analysis benchmark scores, ensuring reproducibility.}

\subsection{Warm-Start Advantage}
\label{sec:rq1}

We compare cold-start (identity initialization) vs.\ warm-start (expert priors, $\lambdaboost = 50$) over 2,000 routing decisions.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/figure1_regret_curve.pdf}
    \caption{\textbf{Warm-Start Advantage.} Expert-distilled priors achieve \textbf{64.6\% lower regret} than cold-start, with the gap widening early when cold-start explores blindly.}
    \label{fig:regret_curve}
\end{figure}
\vspace{-0.5em}

\paragraph{Results.} Warm-start achieves \textbf{64.6\% regret reduction} (Figure~\ref{fig:regret_curve}). The gap is largest in the first 500 steps: warm-start makes useful decisions from step 1, whereas cold-start spends its initial budget on avoidable exploration.

\subsubsection{The Benchmark Trap}
Initializing from public benchmark leaderboards (MMLU, HumanEval, MATH-500) performs worse than random initialization (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_trap}). Benchmark-optimal models often differ from production-optimal models because benchmarks measure broad capability, while routing optimizes utility under specific prompt distributions and cost/latency constraints.

\subsubsection{OOD Generalization}
Despite the benchmark trap for initialization, few-shot calibration generalizes well out of distribution. On held-out benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval, MMLU) using published scores as ground truth, calibration ($\sim$50--100 examples) achieves \textbf{93.5\% average regret reduction}. This confirms that the router learns to map ``Prompt Difficulty'' to ``Model Capability'' effectively, even for tasks it wasn't explicitly trained on.

\subsection{Plasticity Under Concept Drift}
\label{sec:rq2}

A critical failure mode of static routers is the inability to adapt when model capabilities change (e.g., a ``distilled'' model update degrades performance). We tested this by injecting ``Poisoned Priors'' where the bandit was falsely initialized to believe GPT-4o was optimal for all queries.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/figure2_belief_recovery.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Belief Recovery.} The router overcomes poisoned priors within $\sim$200 steps, demonstrating self-correction without manual intervention.}
    \label{fig:belief_recovery}
\end{figure}
\vspace{-0.5em}

\paragraph{Belief Recovery.} With a memory decay factor ($W=0.90$), \ours{} successfully ``unlearned'' the poisoned prior and recovered the true optimal model (Nova-Lite) within $\sim$200 interactions (Figure~\ref{fig:belief_recovery}); without decay ($W=1.0$), it fails to recover.

\paragraph{Significance.} This demonstrates that ``Shippable Priors'' function as a flexible initialization, not a rigid constraint. The system retains the plasticity to reject the teacher's initial advice when contradicted by online evidence.

\label{sec:rq3}

\paragraph{Standard Mode (Cost Leader).} By establishing the low-cost anchor of the Pareto frontier, \ours{} (Standard) achieves 73.0\% accuracy at \$0.70/1k queries. This represents a \textbf{61\% cost reduction} compared to FrugalGPT (\$1.78) and a 44\% reduction compared to RouteLLM (\$1.25), making it the only viable solution for high-throughput, low-margin applications.

\paragraph{Hybrid Mode (High Assurance).} By activating the bandit-guided verification loop ($\lambda > 0$), the system recovers 95.0\% accuracy at a cost comparable to FrugalGPT (\$1.79). Crucially, Hybrid Mode outperforms FrugalGPT by 3\% on complex Instruction Following tasks (\textbf{98\% vs 95\%}).

\paragraph{Mechanism of Recovery (Overcoming Teacher Bias).} Crucially, our reliance on GPT-4o for offline prior distillation introduces a conservative bias: the teacher likely harbors a ``self-preference,'' initially steering the bandit toward the expensive frontier model. However, our benchmark-driven experiments (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}) demonstrate that \ours{} successfully overcomes this initialization bias. By leveraging objective external signals (e.g., Math-500 scores) during online updates, the policy diverges from the teacher's preference to discover cost-effective specialists (e.g., Gemini Flash Lite) that the teacher underestimated. This confirms that ``Shippable Priors'' act as a flexible warm-start, not a rigid constraint.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis}
\label{sec:qualitative}

We manually inspect representative routing decisions to assess whether model choices align with semantic structure.

\paragraph{Case 1: Specialist Win.} \emph{``Generate a SQL query to find top 5 customers by order value.''} $\to$ Nova-Lite (1.00) over GPT-4o (0.00). SQL is rigid syntax where smaller models often excel.

\paragraph{Case 2: Frontier Fallback.} \emph{``What is the definite integral of $\sin(x)/x$ from 0 to $\infty$?''} $\to$ GPT-4o (1.00) over Nova-Lite (0.00). Requires specialized reasoning (Dirichlet integral), where frontier models dominate.

\paragraph{Case 3: Efficiency Opportunity.} \emph{``Why do birds chirp in the morning?''} $\to$ Nova-Lite (0.78) over GPT-4o (0.75). Near-equal quality triggers substantially cheaper routing.

\paragraph{Aggregate Statistics.} Across 497 clusters, we find that 5.6\% are specialist wins, 7.0\% are frontier fallbacks, and 45.5\% are efficiency opportunities where expensive models add little or no quality. This confirms that the router's primary value comes from identifying cases where cost can be drastically reduced without quality loss.
