\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The LLM ecosystem is no longer dominated by a handful of frontier models. In production, teams increasingly choose from dozens of providers and model variants---frontier generalists, domain-tuned specialists, and ultra-cheap small models---each with different failure modes and price--latency profiles. This fragmentation creates an operational question: for a given query, which model should we call to meet a target quality level while respecting budget and latency constraints?

Prior routing systems struggle to exploit this long tail. Fixed cascades restrict access to a small chain of models and incur sequential latency~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt, ong2024routellm}. Static routers can be trained offline but do not adapt to shifting workloads or model updates~\cite{ong2024routellm}. Online contextual bandits can adapt~\cite{slivkins2019contextual}, but in practice suffer from cold-start: early exploration spends real budget on avoidable mistakes.

\paragraph{Key Idea.} While deployments differ, model expertise exhibits stable structure: some models are consistently strong on certain semantic regions (e.g., structured outputs) while others dominate reasoning-intensive prompts. We learn this structure offline and \emph{ship it} as a compact prior that provides day-one competence while still permitting online adaptation.

\paragraph{Scalability Without Re-Training.} A critical limitation of existing routers is $O(N)$ scaling: adding a new model requires running calibration benchmarks or re-training a classifier. Our approach decouples \emph{Prompt Difficulty} (learned once, model-agnostic) from \emph{Model Capability} (registered via metadata). The ``brain'' learns what makes prompts hard; new models simply join the pool with their public benchmark scores as coarse safety filters. The bandit then autonomously discovers their contextual strengths via exploration---no offline profiling required.

We introduce a \emph{Density-Based Warm-Start Framework} that solves cold-start via \emph{Expert Distillation}. We (1) run dense offline evaluation (81 models $\times$ 497 archetypes) using GPT-4o as a teacher judge, (2) compress the resulting statistics into $<$1MB covariance priors, (3) calibrate to new domains with few-shot feedback, and (4) continue updating online to retain plasticity under drift.

\paragraph{Key Results.} On 2,000 real-world queries, warm-start reduces regret by \textbf{63.6\%} vs.\ cold-start (Figure~\ref{fig:regret_curve}) while adding only \textbf{8.94ms} P99 routing overhead (1.1\%). Across cost--quality trade-offs, routing can improve average quality while reducing spend by shifting most traffic to low-cost specialists and reserving frontier models for high-risk prompts.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Shippable priors for cold-start routing:} we distill offline teacher supervision into compact covariance priors that enable day-one routing and few-shot calibration (\S\ref{sec:distillation}, \S\ref{sec:rq1}).
    \item \textbf{Zero-overhead scalability:} new models are onboarded in $O(1)$ time using only public metadata; the bandit verifies performance online rather than requiring expensive offline profiling (\S\ref{sec:scalability}).
    \item \textbf{Tiered routing for reliability:} we couple ex-ante risk prediction with selective verification, addressing ``confident failures'' that can evade reactive verifiers (\S\ref{sec:hybrid}, \S\ref{sec:confident_failure}).
    \item \textbf{Evidence across three evaluation themes:} we quantify cold-start reduction, plasticity under drift, and cost--quality efficiency against routing baselines over 80+ production models (\S\ref{sec:evaluation}).
\end{itemize}
