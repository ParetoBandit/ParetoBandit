\subsection{LLM Routing and Cascading}

Recent works like \textbf{FrugalGPT}~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt} and \textbf{RouteLLM}~\cite{ong2024routellm} have demonstrated the cost-saving potential of intelligent model selection. However, these approaches rely on supervised learning over static datasets (MMLU, GSM8K, Arena). While effective for benchmarks, they suffer from two production limitations:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Latency.} Cascading requires sequential calls: try cheap model $\rightarrow$ detect failure $\rightarrow$ call expensive model. This \emph{doubles} latency for hard queries. Our LinUCB approach makes a single routing decision in \textbf{<9ms}.
    
    \item \textbf{Drift Intolerance.} Supervised routers require retraining when provider APIs change---GPT-4o's capabilities shift monthly. Our bandit formulation adapts \emph{online} via rank-one updates.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The Hidden Cold-Start.} FrugalGPT assumes a pre-trained completion scorer; RouteLLM ships a 500MB classifier. But where does this training data come from? Both require labeled examples matching your deployment distribution---the exact data you lack on day one.

\subsection{Contextual Bandits in Systems}

Bandits are widely used in recommendation systems~\cite{li2010contextual}. However, standard bandit algorithms suffer from the \textbf{Cold-Start Problem}---requiring thousands of exploration steps before converging.

With 81 models and 384 dimensions, naive LinUCB needs $\sim$2,000 queries to accumulate sufficient statistics. Each exploration costs money:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item GPT-4o API calls: \$4.38/1M tokens
    \item Exploration budget for calibration: \$50--\$200
    \item Time to useful routing: \emph{weeks}
\end{itemize}

We differentiate by introducing \textbf{Expert Distillation} (\S\ref{sec:distillation}), which bootstraps the bandit with covariance priors derived from a teacher oracle. This eliminates the ``exploration penalty'' and allows the router to be \textbf{shippable immediately}---63.6\% lower regret from day one (RQ1).

\subsection{Static Leaderboards}

Public leaderboards (LMSYS Chatbot Arena~\cite{zheng2023judging}, HELM~\cite{liang2022holistic}, Open LLM Leaderboard) provide global model rankings. These are invaluable for general understanding but fail for routing:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{No Cost Awareness.} Arena rankings ignore price. A \#1 model at \$15/1M may be dominated by a \#5 model at \$0.10/1M for 80\% of queries.
    
    \item \textbf{``One Size Fits All.''} Global rankings average across all users. A model excellent at legal contracts may rank poorly due to weak creative writing---irrelevant if you only process contracts.
\end{enumerate}

Our system constructs a \textbf{``Personalized Leaderboard''} optimizing for \emph{your} utility function: $U = Q - \lambdacost \cdot C - \lambdalat \cdot L$. The bandit continuously re-ranks based on local performance.
