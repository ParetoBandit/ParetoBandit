\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Our work intersects three research areas: adaptive model selection, cost-aware inference optimization, and transfer learning for decision-making systems. We position \ours{} relative to each paradigm.

\subsection{LLM Routing and Model Selection}

\paragraph{Cascading Systems.} FrugalGPT~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt} pioneered cost-optimized LLM routing through sequential model invocation with learned verification thresholds. While effective for small model sets ($N \leq 3$), the approach suffers from linear latency scaling and requires manual chain curation. RouteLLM~\cite{ong2024routellm} extends this by training a classifier on preference data to route between two models. However, both systems are fundamentally static: adding new models requires offline recalibration (FrugalGPT) or classifier retraining (RouteLLM), creating an $O(N)$ maintenance bottleneck. In contrast, \ours{} supports $O(1)$ model onboarding through metadata-based filtering combined with autonomous online exploration, enabling seamless integration in rapidly evolving model ecosystems.

A critical limitation we identify is the \emph{confident failure} mode: cascading verifiers operate ex-post, evaluating outputs after generation. This creates vulnerability to plausible-but-incorrect responses that satisfy surface-level quality checks while violating semantic constraints. Our hybrid architecture addresses this by performing ex-ante risk assessment, routing high-uncertainty prompts directly to frontier models before generation occurs.

\paragraph{Static Model Selection.} Broader work on model selection for deep learning~\cite{hospedales2021meta} and neural architecture search~\cite{elsken2019neural} optimizes for accuracy without considering inference costs. Recent work on adaptive inference~\cite{han2021dynamic} introduces dynamic computation graphs but focuses on single-model early-exit strategies rather than multi-model routing.

\subsection{Multi-Armed Bandits and Online Learning}

\paragraph{Contextual Bandits.} Contextual multi-armed bandits~\cite{li2010contextual,chu2011contextual} provide a principled framework for sequential decision-making under uncertainty. LinUCB~\cite{li2010contextual} and its variants achieve provably optimal regret bounds~\cite{abbasi2011improved} under linear reward assumptions. However, standard bandits suffer from cold-start inefficiency: convergence requires hundreds to thousands of samples, translating to substantial exploration costs in LLM settings (\$50--200). Bayesian approaches~\cite{russo2018tutorial} and Thompson Sampling~\cite{agrawal2013thompson} offer improved sample efficiency but still require significant burn-in periods.

Our shippable prior mechanism addresses this through offline distillation: by pre-training covariance matrices on teacher supervision, we reduce day-one regret by 64.6\% while preserving the bandit's adaptive plasticity. This differs from standard transfer learning~\cite{taylor2009transfer} by explicitly encoding uncertainty structure (covariance) rather than point estimates alone.

\paragraph{Meta-Learning for Bandits.} Recent work explores meta-learning for bandits~\cite{kveton2020meta,zhou2020metalearning}, learning initialization strategies across task distributions. These methods typically require multiple related tasks for meta-training, whereas our approach leverages a single high-quality teacher. Closest to our work is knowledge distillation for reinforcement learning~\cite{rusu2015policy}, but we operate in the pure contextual bandit setting without state-transition dynamics.

\subsection{LLM Benchmarking and Evaluation}

\paragraph{Static Benchmarks.} Comprehensive evaluation frameworks like HELM~\cite{liang2022holistic}, BIG-bench~\cite{srivastava2022beyond}, and LMSYS Chatbot Arena~\cite{zheng2023judging} provide valuable model comparisons. However, these benchmarks optimize for global rankings averaged across diverse users and domains, ignoring deployment-specific constraints such as cost, latency, and local task distributions. We demonstrate empirically (\S\ref{sec:rq1}) that initializing routing policies from benchmark rankings actually degrades performance compared to random initialization---a phenomenon we term the \emph{benchmark trap}.

The fundamental innovation enabling \ours{}'s scalability is the decoupling of \emph{prompt difficulty learning} (performed once offline) from \emph{model capability profiling} (performed continuously online). This architectural separation allows new models to be integrated in constant time via public metadata as coarse safety filters, with the bandit autonomously refining estimates through production traffic. This addresses the central scalability bottleneck in existing systems: as model registries expand from 3 to 80+ options, \ours{} maintains constant onboarding cost while cascading systems face linear scaling.
