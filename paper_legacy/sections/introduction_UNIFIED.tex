\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language model (LLM) inference increasingly draws from multi-provider portfolios~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt}, spanning lightweight open-weights models to frontier APIs. A growing number of enterprises are moving from single-model deployments to such portfolios, explicitly optimizing for quality and cost; recent industry surveys report that roughly one third of enterprises already operate five or more LLMs in production~\cite{enterprise-multillm-2025}. Because the optimal model varies across tasks, domains, and individual prompts, \textbf{LLM routing}, which dynamically selects the best model per request, has emerged as a critical lever for managing cost and quality at scale~\cite{ong2024routellm, chen2024routerdc, feng2025graphrouter, panda2025pilot}.

Many existing routers train a static classifier on offline evaluation data~\cite{ong2024routellm, chen2024routerdc, feng2025graphrouter}, but frozen policies risk degradation in production due to three factors. First, \textbf{model preference is heterogeneous}: no single model optimally addresses all tasks~\cite{hu2024routerbench, ong2024routellm}, rendering ``best-on-average'' static policies suboptimal. Second, \textbf{deployment distributions often drift} from offline training data: benchmark prompts are generally not representative of production traffic, and the distribution of interest varies by use case~\cite{ailem2024benchmarkrobustness}. Third, \textbf{model portfolios are non-stationary}: providers silently update APIs in ways that shift downstream performance~\cite{chen2023chatgptdrift, ma2024prompt}, and rapid frontier releases~\cite{thompson2026timeline} require routers to adapt without costly retraining.

These production realities emphasize the need for dynamic routing systems in enterprise applications. With enterprise generative AI spending estimated to hit \$37 billion in 2025~\cite{menlo2025genai}, cost management is a primary concern. The inference costs of frontier models often exceed \$15 per million output tokens, yet mid-tier models offer comparable performance for many workloads at a fraction of the cost; case studies from cloud providers and routing vendors report cost reductions of 60--85\% via intelligent routing while maintaining near-frontier quality~\cite{aws-multillm-routing-2025,swfte-intelligent-routing-2026}. This has fueled a rapidly growing LLM router market, with large multi-model gateways such as OpenRouter mediating over \$100M in annualized inference spend across hundreds of models~\cite{llm-router-market-2026,openrouter-growth-2025,openrouter-stats-2025,state-of-ai-openrouter-2025}. At the same time, real-world monitoring indicates that LLMs undergo daily and weekly periodic performance variability~\cite{johnson2026periodic} and are subject to undocumented API changes that silently degrade specific capabilities~\cite{chen2025silent}, while relying on a single frontier model exposes enterprises to unpredictable API outages and strict rate limits~\cite{reintech2025ratelimits}. The dominant practical challenge is therefore to approach frontier-level average quality at substantially lower cost for heterogeneous, unpredictable prompt streams, while dynamically adapting to non-stationary model behavior and mitigating service interruptions through multi-model traffic distribution. This is precisely the regime where contextual bandit routing is most appropriate.

While recent bandit-based routers~\cite{panda2025pilot, wang2025barp, li2025llmbandit} adapt online to deployment feedback, they lack explicit hedging against misspecified or misleading priors. Under simulated distribution shift, our ablation confirms a single-expert bandit leaves $+1.3$\,pp of statistically significant reward on the table compared to architectures that can safely escape bad priors.

To address these gaps, we present \textbf{banditGPT}, an open-source, cost-aware contextual bandit framework for dynamic LLM routing. \textit{banditGPT} makes three core contributions, each validated across portfolio sizes ($K{\in}\{2, 3\}$) and prior sources:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Context-aware online routing with offline-to-online prior transfer.} To exploit prompt-level preference heterogeneity while continuously adapting to distribution and model drift, \textit{banditGPT} uses a cost-aware contextual LinUCB whose per-prompt embeddings enable model selection to vary with input content. A modular pipeline converts a seed batch of representative workload evaluations (1{,}028 LMSYS prompts) into LinUCB sufficient statistics, substantially reducing the cold-start penalty. A continuous cost penalty traces a Pareto frontier, enabling the system to close \textbf{61--75\%} of the gap to a theoretical oracle using 1{,}543 label-free online interactions, yielding up to a 91\% cost reduction with only 6\% quality loss compared to frontier-only routing \textit{in our tested portfolios}. We validated robustness of the offline-to-online transfer across two prior sources (cross-distribution and same-distribution); the source has no significant effect on steady-state quality ($p{>}0.18$).
    
    \item \textbf{Corralling meta-learning for prior safety.} To address the lack of hedging in existing bandit routers, \textit{banditGPT} employs a Corralling~\cite{agarwal2017corralling} meta-learner that dynamically balances an expert initialized with warmup priors against a \textit{tabula rasa} expert learning from scratch. A controlled factorial ablation \textit{on LMSYS Arena data} confirms that online learning yields a $+7.0$\,pp gain over frozen priors, while the Corralling architecture provides a further $+1.3$\,pp steady-state advantage over a single-expert bandit ($p < 0.0001$, Cohen's $d{=}0.98$), achieving 7--13\% lower cumulative regret than a warmup-only bandit across a prior-quality ablation, while degrading gracefully under prior misspecification as the tabula rasa expert provides a safety floor.
    
    \item \textbf{Multi-model portfolio routing with family parameter sharing.} For larger portfolios ($K \geq 3$), \textit{banditGPT} supports hard per-request budget and latency constraints alongside Hybrid LinUCB, which pools observations across semantically related models via data-driven family assignments.
