\subsection{Corralling: Robust Expert Aggregation with Exploration Floor}
\label{sec:corralling}

\subsubsection{Motivation}
While warmup priors provide strong initial performance, they carry the risk of \textit{negative transfer} if the offline data distribution differs from the deployment domain. To mitigate this, we employ a \textit{Corralling} meta-algorithm \cite{agarwal2017corralling} that adaptively combines two expert policies:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Warmup Expert}: LinUCB initialized with offline priors ($\mathbf{A}_{offline}, \mathbf{b}_{offline}$)
    \item \textbf{Tabula Rasa Expert}: LinUCB with cold initialization ($\mathbf{A} = \lambda \mathbf{I}, \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{0}$)
\end{itemize}

The meta-policy maintains a probability distribution $\mathbf{p}_t \in \Delta^K$ over the $K=2$ experts and selects actions by first sampling an expert $i \sim \mathbf{p}_t$, then querying that expert's policy.

\subsubsection{Exponential Weight Update}
At each time step $t$, the master observes the loss $\ell_{i,t} = 1 - r_t$ for the selected expert $i$ and updates weights via:
\begin{equation}
    w_{i,t+1} \propto w_{i,t} \cdot \exp\left(-\eta \cdot \frac{\ell_{i,t}}{p_{i,t}}\right)
\end{equation}

where $\eta > 0$ is the learning rate and the loss is importance-weighted by $1/p_{i,t}$ to ensure unbiased estimation.

\subsubsection{Preventing Expert Death via Mixing Parameter}
\label{sec:expert_death_prevention}

To ensure robustness in non-stationary environments (e.g., evolving model capabilities), we implement a mixing parameter $\gamma=0.05$. This imposes a uniform exploration floor $p_{min} = \gamma/K$, preventing the \textit{Expert Death} phenomenon where weight decay renders an expert unrecoverable.

The selection distribution is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_t = (1-\gamma) \cdot \frac{\mathbf{w}_t}{\|\mathbf{w}_t\|_1} + \frac{\gamma}{K} \cdot \mathbf{1}
\end{equation}

This ensures that even if the Warmup Expert dominates early on, the Tabula Rasa Expert maintains a minimum $2.5\%$ selection probability, allowing the system to detect and recover from domain shift or the introduction of superior new models.

\paragraph{Practical Properties.} With $\gamma > 0$, every expert $i$ satisfies $p_{i,t} \geq \gamma/K$ for all $t$, ensuring:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bounded Importance Weights}: The loss estimator $\ell_{i,t}/p_{i,t} \leq K/\gamma$, preventing numerical instability.
    \item \textbf{Adaptation to Non-Stationarity}: If an initially poor expert becomes optimal (e.g., due to a model update), the system will sample it $\Omega(\gamma T / K)$ times over horizon $T$, sufficient to detect the shift and reallocate weight.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Implementation}
The Corralling router is implemented as a wrapper around arbitrary expert policies:

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Corralling with Mixing Parameter}
\label{alg:corralling}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Experts $\{\pi_1, \ldots, \pi_K\}$, learning rate $\eta$, mixing parameter $\gamma$
\State Initialize $\mathbf{w}_0 = \mathbf{1} / K$ \Comment{Uniform prior}
\For{$t = 1, 2, \ldots, T$}
    \State Compute mixed distribution: $\mathbf{p}_t = (1-\gamma) \cdot \mathbf{w}_t + (\gamma/K) \cdot \mathbf{1}$
    \State Sample expert: $i_t \sim \text{Categorical}(\mathbf{p}_t)$
    \State Query expert: $a_t \gets \pi_{i_t}(\mathbf{x}_t)$
    \State Observe reward: $r_t$
    \State Compute loss: $\ell_{i_t, t} = (1 - r_t) / p_{i_t, t}$ \Comment{Importance-weighted}
    \State Update weights: $w_{i_t, t+1} = w_{i_t, t} \cdot \exp(-\eta \cdot \ell_{i_t, t})$
    \State Normalize: $\mathbf{w}_{t+1} \gets \mathbf{w}_{t+1} / \|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}\|_1$
    \State Update selected expert: $\pi_{i_t}.\text{update}(\mathbf{x}_t, a_t, r_t)$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Theoretical Motivation.} Under the assumption of bounded losses $\ell_{i,t} \in [0, L]$, the theoretical Corralling algorithm achieves:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Regret}_T \leq \frac{\ln K}{\eta} + \frac{\eta L^2 T}{2} + \gamma T
\end{equation}

The first two terms represent the standard Exp4 regret, while the final $\gamma T$ term is the cost of forced exploration. Setting $\eta = \sqrt{2\ln K / (L^2 T)}$ and $\gamma = O(1/\sqrt{T})$ yields $O(\sqrt{T \ln K})$ regret. Our implementation uses fixed hyperparameters for practical efficiency.

\paragraph{Practical Configuration.} In our experiments, we use $\eta = 0.1$ and $\gamma = 0.05$, prioritizing rapid adaptation over asymptotic optimality. This configuration ensures that if warmup priors are misspecified, the system pivots to the Tabula Rasa expert within $\sim$100 samples, while maintaining safety against transient noise.
