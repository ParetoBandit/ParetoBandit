\section{Experiment 11: BanditGPT vs. RouteLLM}
\label{sec:exp11_comparison}

\subsection{Overview}
In this experiment, we benchmark \texttt{BanditRouter} against the state-of-the-art \texttt{RouteLLM} library. The objective is to evaluate cost-efficiency in a realistic two-model deployment scenario:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Weak Model}: \texttt{gpt-oss-120b} (Cost: $\approx\$0$/1M tokens)
    \item \textbf{Strong Model}: \texttt{gemini-3.0-preview} (Cost: $\approx\$7$/1M tokens)
\end{itemize}

The primary metric is the \textbf{Cost-Performance Pareto Frontier}, which visualizes the trade-off between normalized cost (x-axis) and recovered quality relative to the flagship model (y-axis).

\subsection{Results: The Cost-Performance Pareto Frontier}
Figure \ref{fig:pareto_frontier} presents the main result. The \texttt{BanditRouter} curve (Red Star) dominates the \texttt{RouteLLM} baseline (Blue Curve), achieving significantly lower costs for the same quality targets.

\paragraph{Key Findings:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cost Efficiency at ISO-Quality}: At the 99.5\% quality target, BanditGPT achieves 89.7\% cost savings relative to RouteLLM.
    
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Routing}: RouteLLM requires 42.4\% of flagship cost to reach this target, while BanditGPT reaches it at just 4.4\% cost (effectively identifying the Triviality Floor).
    
    \item \textbf{Data Efficiency}: It is notable that \texttt{RouteLLM} was pre-trained on $\approx$80k examples from Chatbot Arena. In contrast, our \texttt{BanditRouter} was trained on only 1,121 examples from our internal dev set (a 71x reduction in data requirements), yet achieved superior routing efficiency. This highlights the effectiveness of the LinUCB algorithm for rapid adaptation in low-data regimes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Efficiency at the Limit: The 93\% Consensus}

Analysis of our test set revealed a high degree of model convergence: on 92.8\% of prompts, the efficient model ($M_{weak}$) achieved parity with the flagship ($M_{strong}$). We categorize this as the \textbf{Consensus Region}, where the primary optimization objective is not quality maximization (which is bounded), but \textbf{Efficiency Maximization}.

\subsection{Main Result: Discovering the "Triviality Floor"}

Table \ref{tab:iso_quality_cost} demonstrates that \texttt{BanditRouter} identifies the "Triviality Floor"—the minimum cost operating point required to hit the quality ceiling. While RouteLLM remains conservative, our system achieves the 99.5\% quality target at 89.7\% lower cost.

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis: Performance on Discriminating Subset}

To ensure \texttt{BanditRouter} maintains capability when it matters, we isolated the top 7\% of "Hard Prompts" where $|\Delta Q| > 0.1$. On this discriminating subset, our router successfully identified 97\% of the prompts requiring the flagship model, proving it is not merely a "cheap-mode" heuristic but a surgical discriminator.
