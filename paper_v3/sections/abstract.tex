\begin{abstract}
Production LLM serving relies on multi-model portfolios spanning a ${\sim}\ppPriceRangeX\times$ cost range, where routing decisions directly trade quality against cost. This trade-off is non-stationary: providers revise pricing, model quality can regress silently, and new models must be integrated without downtime. ParetoBandit is the first open-source router to simultaneously enforce dollar-denominated budgets, adapt online to these shifts, and onboard new models at runtime.

We present ParetoBandit, an open-source adaptive router built on cost-aware contextual bandits. Three design choices close these gaps: an online primal-dual budget pacer enforces a per-request cost ceiling over an open-ended stream, replacing offline penalty tuning with closed-loop control; geometric forgetting on sufficient statistics enables rapid adaptation to price and quality shifts while bootstrapping from offline priors; and a hot-swap registry lets operators add or remove models at runtime, with the bandit's exploration bonus discovering each newcomer's niche from live traffic alone.

We evaluate ParetoBandit across four deployment scenarios on 1,824 benchmark prompts routed through a three-model portfolio. Across seven budget ceilings, per-request cost never exceeds the target by more than $1\%$. When conditions shift the system adapts: an order-of-magnitude price cut on the costliest model yields a $+\ppMaxQualityLift$ quality lift, and a silent quality regression is detected and rerouted within budget. A cold-started model reaches sustained adoption within ${\sim}\ppAdoptionSteps$ steps without breaching the cost ceiling. End-to-end routing latency is $\ppEteTotalMedianMs$\,ms on CPU---less than $\ppEteRoutePctOfTotal\%$ of typical LLM inference time---with the routing decision itself taking just $\ppPBRouteMedianUs\,\mu$s.
\end{abstract}
