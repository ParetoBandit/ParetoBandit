\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented ParetoBandit, an adaptive LLM routing system that integrates cost-aware contextual bandits with online budget pacing and geometric forgetting. Across four experiments the system keeps per-request cost within ${\sim}5$\% of the target for every binding budget. Under cost drift it autonomously exploits a price drop and recovers compliance when pricing is restored; under silent quality degradation it detects the regression, redistributes traffic, and holds budget---while baselines without closed-loop cost control overshoot by up to $7.0{\times}$. Cold-start model onboarding proceeds without reconfiguration. As multi-provider LLM portfolios become the norm, these results suggest that bandit-driven routing with integrated budget pacing can deliver closed-loop cost control without manual intervention.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Six limitations scope the current contribution.
\emph{(i)~Offline evaluation:} all experiments use a fixed reward matrix with injected non-stationarity; live deployment would add stochastic feedback delays and distribution shift.
\emph{(ii)~Deterministic rewards:} we evaluate with LLM-judge scores; the noise and sparsity of human-in-the-loop feedback remain untested.
\emph{(iii)~Rate vs.\ aggregate budget:} $B$ is a per-request rate, so traffic spikes can exceed an aggregate dollar cap even while per-request compliance holds.
\emph{(iv)~Onboarding exploration cost:} online model discovery exposes a bounded burn-in cost to production traffic.
\emph{(v)~No latency awareness:} the current formulation does not model downstream inference latency or endpoint congestion.
\emph{(vi)~Cost-only constraint:} ParetoBandit constrains cost and maximizes quality, but some deployments require the dual formulation---minimizing cost subject to a quality floor.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Most limitations above are addressable within the existing framework without modifying the core LinUCB update, geometric forgetting, or warmup priors.
Shadow routing and live A/B evaluation~(i) require only engineering atop the cached context-vector architecture.
Sparse human labels~(ii) can be accommodated by adapting $\gamma$ to the label arrival rate.
A token-bucket layer would enforce aggregate budgets~(iii); transfer priors and shadow evaluation could reduce onboarding burn-in~(iv).
Latency awareness~(v) maps directly onto the BwK formulation as a second dual variable $\mu_t$.
The quality-constrained mode~(vi) inverts the pacer to track EMA-smoothed reward against a floor~$\tau$, providing an online counterpart to PROTEUS's offline SLA enforcement~\cite{bhatti2026proteus}.
Validating these extensions under realistic deployment conditions is the most immediate next step.
