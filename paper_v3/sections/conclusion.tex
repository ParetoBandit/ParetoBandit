\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented ParetoBandit, an adaptive LLM routing system that integrates cost-aware contextual bandits with online budget pacing and geometric forgetting. Across four experiments on a three-model portfolio, the system maintains per-request cost compliance within $0.95$--$1.01{\times}$ of the target budget while filling the quality--cost gaps between fixed single-model baselines. Under cost drift, the budget pacer autonomously exploits a price drop and recovers compliance when pricing is restored. Under catastrophic model failure, ParetoBandit detects the degraded model, redistributes traffic, and holds budget compliance throughout---while baselines that learn but lack closed-loop cost control overshoot by up to $5.42{\times}$. Cold-start onboarding of a fourth model proceeds without reconfiguration, with the budget pacer maintaining compliance through the portfolio transition.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
Our evaluation uses a fixed offline reward matrix and simulates non-stationarity by permuting rewards and costs at phase boundaries; live deployment with real-time LLM calls would introduce additional latency variance and stochastic feedback delays. The current context representation uses PCA over TF-IDF features; richer embeddings (e.g., from a small language model) may improve per-prompt routing accuracy. The budget pacer enforces a rate constraint with a single EMA timescale; hierarchical pacing (e.g., per-hour and per-day targets) would better match production SLA contracts. Finally, extending the framework to multi-objective constraints beyond cost---such as latency percentiles or fairness across prompt categories---is a natural next step.
