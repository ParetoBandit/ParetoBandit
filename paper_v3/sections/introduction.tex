\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Production LLM portfolios now span a $530\times$ cost range (e.g., \$0.00003/req for Llama-3.1-8B vs.\ \$0.015 for Gemini-2.5-Pro), and the best model for a given request varies with the prompt~\cite{chen2024routerdc, feng2025graphrouter}. A growing body of work has proposed routers that learn which model to dispatch~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt, ong2024routellm, panda2025pilot}. The question is no longer \emph{whether} to route, but how to do so under the operational realities of deployment: dollar budgets, non-stationary pricing, and evolving model portfolios.

Most existing routers learn a fixed policy from offline data and freeze it at serving time. Cascading approaches query models sequentially from cheapest to most expensive~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt}; supervised classifiers learn from preference data~\cite{ong2024routellm}, contrastive representations~\cite{chen2024routerdc}, or graph-structured interactions~\cite{feng2025graphrouter}. These methods perform well when deployment matches training, but the environment does not hold still. Providers silently update APIs in ways that shift quality~\cite{chen2023chatgptdrift, ma2024prompt}, pricing changes abruptly (OpenAI cut GPT-4o input prices by 50\%~\cite{openai2024gpt4opricing}; Google cut Gemini~1.5~Flash input pricing by ${\sim}78$\%~\cite{google2024flashpricing}), and new models launch regularly. Cost-aware formulations share this limitation: OmniRouter~\cite{ma2025omnirouter} solves an offline constrained optimisation over a fixed prompt set, and PROTEUS~\cite{bhatti2026proteus} trains a Lagrangian reinforcement learning (RL) policy offline, explicitly identifying post-deployment cost drift as an open problem. A frozen router cannot exploit a price drop, detect a degraded model, or incorporate a new endpoint without retraining.

Bandit-based routers address this by learning online from the outcome of each dispatched request~\cite{panda2025pilot, wang2025barp, li2025llmbandit}, and continual-learning frameworks~\cite{wang2025mixllm} further accommodate evolving model pools, though broad benchmarks suggest routing gains over single-model baselines can still be small or inconsistent~\cite{li2026llmrouterbench}. Despite this shift toward adaptive methods, two deployment gaps remain open.

\paragraph{Gap 1: No autonomous budget control.}
Several routers accept budget or SLA targets~\cite{panda2025pilot, ma2025omnirouter, bhatti2026proteus}, but to our knowledge none enforces a per-request dollar target in closed loop over an open-ended stream. Knapsack formulations accept a total dollar budget but require knowing the number of requests in advance, making them unsuitable for open-ended production traffic~\cite{panda2025pilot}. OmniRouter~\cite{ma2025omnirouter} solves a constrained optimisation over a fixed prompt set via Lagrangian dual decomposition and cannot adapt to live traffic. PROTEUS~\cite{bhatti2026proteus} trains a Lagrangian RL policy against SLA constraints offline but freezes it at serving time, so it cannot react to post-deployment price or quality shifts.

\paragraph{Gap 2: No principled non-stationarity handling.}
Non-stationary bandit algorithms with geometric discounting or sliding windows are well studied in the theory literature~\cite{garivier2011switching, russac2019weighted}, yet to our knowledge no existing LLM router incorporates such mechanisms. The bandit-based routers cited above all use infinite memory ($\gamma{=}1.0$), which is optimal when the environment is stationary but causes the router to adapt too slowly when a model degrades or a price drops. More broadly, we are not aware of any LLM routing work that evaluates router behaviour under post-deployment quality degradation or mid-stream price changes. In our quality-degradation experiment (Section~\ref{sec:eval_catastrophic}), a bandit with online learning but no budget pacer detects the degraded model yet overshoots the per-request cost target by up to $6.9\times$, because it shifts traffic to expensive alternatives without a mechanism to constrain spending.

These gaps carry direct financial consequences. A router without closed-loop cost control either wastes quality headroom (under-spend) or triggers cost overruns (over-spend), and one that cannot detect a price drop leaves freed budget idle rather than reinvesting it in quality. Beyond budgets and non-stationarity, production portfolios are not static: new models launch, old ones are deprecated, and operators need to adjust the roster without downtime. A bandit formulation naturally accommodates such changes because its exploration bonus discovers a newcomer's quality--cost niche from live traffic; we demonstrate this experimentally but note it is a practical benefit of the design rather than a limitation unique to prior work.

We address these challenges with \textbf{ParetoBandit}, an open-source adaptive routing system for budget-constrained, non-stationary LLM serving. ParetoBandit is built on cost-aware contextual bandits and introduces three system-level contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Autonomous budget control} (Section~\ref{sec:budget_pacer}). An online primal-dual budget pacer accepts a dollar-denominated per-request cost target and enforces it in closed loop over an open-ended stream with no known horizon. The pacer detects price drops via an EMA-smoothed cost signal and automatically reallocates freed budget to higher-quality models.

  \item \textbf{Non-stationarity resilience} (Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_learning}). Geometric forgetting gives the bandit a bounded memory window that tracks quality and cost shifts. The exploration and forgetting rates are selected via a Pareto knee-point procedure (Appendix~\ref{sec:hparam_sweep}) anchored by a single practitioner-specified adaptation horizon, sidestepping standard cross-validation, which in our experiments consistently selects no forgetting ($\gamma{=}1.0$).

  \item \textbf{Runtime portfolio management} (Section~\ref{sec:production_infra}). A hot-swap model registry supports adding and removing models at runtime. The bandit's exploration bonus discovers a newcomer's quality-cost niche from live traffic, while the budget pacer maintains compliance throughout portfolio changes. Offline warmup priors accelerate convergence but are not required: the router operates correctly from an uninformative cold start, as demonstrated in Section~\ref{sec:eval_onboarding}.
\end{enumerate}

We evaluate ParetoBandit on 1,785 prompts drawn from nine public benchmarks spanning a three-tier portfolio ($K{=}3$, with a $K{=}4$ onboarding extension), scored by an LLM judge under a continuous rubric (Section~\ref{sec:eval_setup}). The system design is presented in Section~\ref{sec:system_design} and evaluated against both deployment gaps in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.
