\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Production LLM portfolios now span a $\ppPriceRangeX\times$ cost range (e.g., \$0.00003/req for Llama-3.1-8B vs.\ \$0.015 for Gemini-2.5-Pro), yet no single model dominates on every input~\cite{chen2024routerdc, feng2025graphrouter}. This complementarity has motivated a growing line of work on learned routers that dispatch each request to the most suitable model~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt, ong2024routellm, panda2025pilot}. The open question is no longer \emph{whether} to route, but how to do so under the operational realities of deployment: dollar budgets, non-stationary pricing, and evolving model portfolios.

Most existing routers learn a fixed policy offline and freeze it at serving time, whether through cascading~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt}, supervised classification on preference or embedding data~\cite{ong2024routellm, chen2024routerdc, feng2025graphrouter}, or constrained optimisation with budget targets~\cite{ma2025omnirouter, bhatti2026proteus}. These methods all navigate the quality--cost tradeoff, yet none has been shown to adapt once deployed. The serving environment, however, is non-stationary: providers silently update APIs in ways that shift quality~\cite{chen2023chatgptdrift, ma2024prompt}, pricing changes abruptly (e.g., OpenAI cut GPT-4o input prices by 50\%~\cite{openai2024gpt4opricing}), and new models launch regularly. A frozen router cannot exploit a price drop, detect a degraded model, or incorporate a new endpoint without retraining---a limitation that PROTEUS~\cite{bhatti2026proteus} explicitly identifies as an open problem.\looseness=-1

Online learning offers a path forward. Bandit-based routers update their policy from the outcome of each dispatched request~\cite{panda2025pilot, wang2025barp, li2025llmbandit}, and continual-learning frameworks further accommodate evolving model pools~\cite{wang2025mixllm}. Yet even among these adaptive methods, two deployment gaps remain open.

\noindent\textbf{Gap 1: No closed-loop enforcement of cost ceilings.}
Several routers accept budget or SLA targets~\cite{panda2025pilot, ma2025omnirouter, bhatti2026proteus}, but to our knowledge none enforces a per-request dollar target in closed loop over an open-ended stream. Existing approaches either require a known finite horizon~\cite{panda2025pilot}, solve a batch optimisation over a fixed prompt set~\cite{ma2025omnirouter}, or freeze dual variables at deployment~\cite{bhatti2026proteus}.

\noindent\textbf{Gap 2: No principled non-stationarity handling.}
Non-stationary bandit algorithms are well studied~\cite{garivier2011switching, russac2019weighted}, yet to our knowledge no existing LLM router incorporates geometric discounting or sliding windows to handle post-deployment shifts, and no published routing work evaluates behaviour under quality degradation or mid-stream price changes. In our quality-degradation experiment (Section~\ref{sec:eval_catastrophic}), a standard bandit without geometric forgetting or a budget pacer detects the degraded model yet overshoots the cost target by up to $\ppMaxOvershootX\times$.

These gaps carry direct financial consequences: a router without closed-loop cost control either wastes quality headroom or triggers cost overruns, and one that cannot detect a price drop leaves freed budget idle. Beyond budgets and non-stationarity, production portfolios are not static; new models must be evaluated and integrated without downtime or offline retraining.

We address these challenges with \textbf{ParetoBandit}, an open-source adaptive routing system for budget-constrained, non-stationary LLM serving. ParetoBandit is built on cost-aware contextual bandits and introduces three system-level contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Autonomous budget control} (Section~\ref{sec:budget_pacer}). An online primal-dual budget pacer accepts an operator-specified per-request cost ceiling and enforces it in closed loop over an open-ended stream with no known horizon, automatically reallocating freed budget when prices drop.

  \item \textbf{Non-stationarity resilience} (Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_learning}). Geometric forgetting gives the bandit a bounded memory window that tracks quality and cost shifts. Exploration and forgetting rates are selected via a Pareto knee-point procedure (Appendix~\ref{sec:hparam_sweep}) anchored by a single practitioner-specified adaptation horizon.

  \item \textbf{Runtime portfolio management} (Section~\ref{sec:production_infra}). A hot-swap model registry supports adding and removing models at runtime; a brief forced-exploration period bootstraps the newcomer's posterior, after which UCB selection discovers its quality--cost niche while the budget pacer maintains compliance. Warmup priors accelerate convergence but are not required (Section~\ref{sec:eval_onboarding}).
\end{enumerate}

We evaluate ParetoBandit on a 1,824-prompt holdout set drawn from nine public benchmarks spanning a three-tier portfolio ($K{=}3$, with a $K{=}4$ onboarding extension), scored by an LLM judge under a continuous rubric (Section~\ref{sec:eval_setup}). The system design is presented in Section~\ref{sec:system_design} and evaluated against both deployment gaps in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.
