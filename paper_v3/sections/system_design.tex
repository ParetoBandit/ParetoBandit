\section{System Design}
\label{sec:system_design}

To our knowledge, no existing algorithm jointly learns reward surfaces from bandit feedback, enforces a long-run budget constraint, and adapts to non-stationarity. ParetoBandit composes four foundational results, each addressing one facet of the problem: LinUCB~\cite{li2010contextual} for contextual reward learning, Lagrangian relaxation of the BwK framework~\cite{badanidiyuru2013bwk, agrawal2016linear} for budget enforcement, geometric discounting~\cite{garivier2011switching, russac2019weighted} for non-stationarity, and offline-to-online warm-start priors~\cite{oetomo2023warmstart} for cold-start elimination. The composition is enabled by LinUCB's sufficient-statistic representation ($A_a$, $b_a$ per arm): forgetting reduces to a scalar multiply, warmup to a matrix addition, and updates to incremental rank-1 operations. Linear Thompson Sampling~\cite{agrawal2013thompson} shares this structure and could support all four ingredients; we chose UCB because its deterministic score interacts more predictably with the Lagrangian penalty, simplifying tuning and debugging. Neural bandits~\cite{zhou2020neural} and learned routing policies such as BaRP~\cite{wang2025barp} lack closed-form sufficient statistics, making this composition considerably more involved.

\paragraph{Why a linear reward model?}
Several recent routers---BaRP~\cite{wang2025barp}, LLM Bandit~\cite{li2025llmbandit}, PROTEUS~\cite{bhatti2026proteus}---parameterize their routing policies with multilayer perceptrons (MLPs).
A linear model is a deliberate architectural choice, not a simplification; its advantages are structural and become most visible under non-stationarity.
%
First, MLP-based policies can be brittle when the cost--performance landscape shifts.
PROTEUS trains its Lagrangian RL policy offline and freezes both the policy weights and the dual variables at deployment; the authors themselves identify post-deployment cost drift as an open problem, since the frozen Lagrange multipliers are calibrated to training-time pricing.
BaRP and LLM Bandit train via policy gradients (BaRP simulating bandit feedback from offline logs; LLM Bandit pretraining on full-information data). In both cases the MLP weights reflect the cumulative training history with no forgetting mechanism, so when a model's quality degrades or a provider cuts prices, stale associations must be slowly overwritten by new gradient steps rather than explicitly discounted.
Moreover, BaRP's calibrated cost scaling normalizes rewards against the observed cost range; if costs shift outside that calibration window, the scalarized reward $r - \lambda c$ becomes unreliable, since neural networks generally extrapolate poorly beyond their training support.
In contrast, ParetoBandit's sufficient statistics $(A_a, b_a)$ are exponentially decayed via geometric forgetting (a single scalar multiply), giving stale evidence an explicit half-life; costs enter as a separate Lagrangian penalty that adapts online through the dual variable $\lambda_t$, requiring no learned cost representation.
Sections~\ref{sec:eval_nonstationary} and~\ref{sec:eval_catastrophic} demonstrate this advantage empirically: the router exploits a mid-stream price drop, recovers compliance when pricing is restored, and detects a silent quality regression---all without operator intervention.
%
Second, every other system-level capability---warm-start priors (matrix addition on $A_a, b_a$), Sherman--Morrison updates ($O(d^2)$), and the deterministic UCB score that interacts predictably with the Lagrangian penalty---likewise depends on the closed-form sufficient-statistic structure. An MLP-based approach would require a separate mechanism for each (e.g., offline fine-tuning for warmup, dropout ensembles for uncertainty), multiplying design complexity and hyperparameter count.
%
Third, LinUCB's confidence bonus $x_t^\top A_a^{-1} x_t$ is the exact posterior variance under a Gaussian linear model, providing \emph{context-dependent} calibrated exploration from the first request.
MLP-based routers typically rely on coarser alternatives---BaRP adds entropy regularization to prevent policy collapse, but this is global rather than prompt-specific and does not concentrate exploration where the reward estimate is most uncertain for a given input.

Although each ingredient is well-established individually, combining them creates cross-component interactions. For example, geometric forgetting shrinks $A_a$ toward singularity over time, inflating LinUCB's confidence bonus. This inflation makes expensive but uncertain arms more attractive, which drives up $\lambda_t$ and tightens the budget penalty. The forgetting rate $\gamma$ and the budget pacer's step size $\eta$ must therefore be calibrated jointly, not in isolation. Section~\ref{sec:hparam_sweep} describes the Pareto knee-point procedure that resolves these interactions.

\subsection{Architecture Overview}
\label{sec:architecture}

ParetoBandit operates as a closed-loop routing system with two data paths.

\paragraph{Synchronous inference path.}
When a user prompt arrives, the feature extractor (Section~\ref{sec:context_repr}) produces $x_t$. The \texttt{BanditRouter} scores every eligible model using the budget-augmented UCB rule (Section~\ref{sec:budget_pacer}) and dispatches the prompt to the highest-scoring model $a_t$.

\paragraph{Asynchronous feedback path.}
After inference, the observed reward $r_t$ and realized cost $c_t$ flow back. The bandit updates its per-arm sufficient statistics with geometric forgetting (Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_learning}), while the budget pacer updates $\lambda_t$ via the EMA-smoothed cost signal (Eqs.~\ref{eq:cost_ema}--\ref{eq:dual_update}). The context vector is cached at route time so that rewards arriving hours later (e.g., human RLHF labels) can still update the bandit without re-encoding the prompt. Algorithm~\ref{alg:paretobandit} consolidates the full per-step loop.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{ParetoBandit: Budget-Paced Non-Stationary Routing}
\label{alg:paretobandit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Arms $\mathcal{A}$; regularization $\lambda_0$; budget $B$; static cost penalty $\lambda_c$; exploration $\alpha$; forgetting $\gamma$; step size $\eta$; EMA $\alpha_{\mathrm{ema}}$; cap $\bar\lambda$
\STATE \textbf{Init:} $\forall\, a$: $A_a \!\leftarrow\! \lambda_0 I$, $b_a \!\leftarrow\! \mathbf{0}$ (or warmup both, \S\ref{sec:warmup}), $\hat\theta_a \!\leftarrow\! A_a^{-1} b_a$; $\lambda_t \!\leftarrow\! 0$; $\bar{c}_0 \!\leftarrow\! B$; $t \!\leftarrow\! 0$
\FOR{each request with context $x_t$}
\STATE \textbf{--- Arm Selection ---}
\IF{$\lambda_t > 0$}
\STATE $\mathcal{A}_t \leftarrow \{a \in \mathcal{A} : c_a \leq c_{\max}^{\mathcal{A}} / (1 {+} \lambda_t)\}$ \label{line:ceiling} \hfill $\triangleright$ Hard ceiling
\ELSE
\STATE $\mathcal{A}_t \leftarrow \mathcal{A}$
\ENDIF
\FOR{each $a \in \mathcal{A}_t$}
\STATE $\mathrm{dt}_a \leftarrow t - \max(\text{last\_upd}_a,\, \text{last\_play}_a)$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Exploration staleness
\STATE $v_a \leftarrow x_t^\top A_a^{-1} x_t / \max(\gamma^{\mathrm{dt}_a}, V_{\max}^{-1})$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Variance inflation
\STATE $s_a \leftarrow \hat\theta_a^\top x_t + \alpha\sqrt{v_a} - (\lambda_c + \lambda_t)\,\tilde{c}_a$ \label{line:ucb}
\ENDFOR
\STATE $a_t \leftarrow \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}_t} s_a$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Random tiebreak
\STATE $t \leftarrow t + 1$;\; $\text{last\_play}_{a_t} \leftarrow t$
\STATE \textbf{--- Observe} reward $r_t$, cost $c_t$ \textbf{---}
\STATE \textbf{--- Reward Update (Geometric Forgetting) ---}
\STATE $\mathrm{dt}' \leftarrow t - \text{last\_upd}_{a_t}$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Decay staleness (statistics age)
\STATE $A_{a_t} \leftarrow \gamma^{\mathrm{dt}'}\, A_{a_t}$;\; $b_{a_t} \leftarrow \gamma^{\mathrm{dt}'}\, b_{a_t}$ \label{line:forget} \hfill $\triangleright$ Decay stale data
\STATE $A_{a_t}^{-1} \leftarrow A_{a_t}^{-1} / \gamma^{\mathrm{dt}'}$ \hfill $\triangleright$ $O(d^2)$ scalar op
\STATE $A_{a_t} \leftarrow A_{a_t} + x_t x_t^\top$;\; $b_{a_t} \leftarrow b_{a_t} + r_t\, x_t$
\STATE Update $A_{a_t}^{-1}$ via Sherman--Morrison \hfill $\triangleright$ $O(d^2)$
\STATE $\hat\theta_{a_t} \leftarrow A_{a_t}^{-1}\, b_{a_t}$;\; $\text{last\_upd}_{a_t} \leftarrow t$
\STATE \textbf{--- Budget Pacer Dual Update ---}
\STATE $\bar{c}_t \leftarrow (1 {-} \alpha_{\mathrm{ema}})\,\bar{c}_{t-1} + \alpha_{\mathrm{ema}}\,c_t$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Eq.~\ref{eq:cost_ema}
\STATE $\lambda_{t+1} \leftarrow [\lambda_t + \eta(\bar{c}_t / B - 1)]_{0}^{\bar\lambda}$ \label{line:dual} \hfill $\triangleright$ Eq.~\ref{eq:dual_update}
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Budget-Paced Arm Selection}
\label{sec:budget_pacer}

At each step $t$, the router selects model $a_t$ by maximizing a budget-augmented utility:
\begin{equation}
    a_t = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}_t} \Bigl[
        \underbrace{\hat\theta_a^\top x_t}_{\text{exploit}}
        +\; \underbrace{\alpha \sqrt{x_t^\top A_a^{-1} x_t}}_{\text{explore}}
        \;-\; \underbrace{(\lambda_c + \lambda_t) \,\tilde{c}_a}_{\text{cost penalty}}
    \Bigr]
    \label{eq:budget_ucb}
\end{equation}
The first term is the router's current best estimate of the expected reward for model $a$ on prompt $x_t$ (\emph{exploitation}). The second term is a confidence bonus proportional to the router's uncertainty about that estimate: models selected rarely or on dissimilar prompts receive larger bonuses, encouraging the router to try them (\emph{exploration}); in practice this base variance is further inflated for stale arms via a staleness divisor (Eq.~\ref{eq:staleness_inflation}, Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_learning}). The exploration rate $\alpha > 0$ controls this tradeoff directly. The cost penalty $(\lambda_c + \lambda_t)\tilde{c}_a$ reduces the effective score of expensive models, constraining exploration to arms the budget can sustain. It has two additive components. The static weight $\lambda_c \geq 0$ (default 0.3) encodes the operator's baseline cost--quality preference and is active from the first request; setting $\lambda_c = 0$ recovers pure quality-only routing. The dynamic dual variable $\lambda_t \geq 0$ is maintained by the budget pacer: it starts at zero and rises only when recent spending exceeds the target $B$, providing closed-loop budget enforcement on top of the static preference. After each request, a smoothed dual-ascent step adjusts $\lambda_t$:
\begin{align}
    \bar{c}_t &= (1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{ema}})\,\bar{c}_{t-1}
                 + \alpha_{\mathrm{ema}}\,c_t
    \label{eq:cost_ema} \\
    \lambda_{t+1} &= \bigl[\lambda_t + \eta\,(\bar{c}_t / B - 1)\bigr]_{0}^{\bar\lambda}
    \label{eq:dual_update}
\end{align}
where $\bar{c}_t$ is an EMA-smoothed cost signal ($\alpha_{\mathrm{ema}}{=}0.05$, half-life ${\approx}14$ requests), $\eta{=}0.05$ is the step size, and $[\cdot]_0^{\bar\lambda}$ projects onto $[0, \bar\lambda{=}5]$. The EMA prevents sawtooth oscillations from single expensive requests; normalizing the gradient by $B$ makes $\eta$ portfolio-independent. When $\bar{c}_t / B > 1$ (overspending), $\lambda_t$ rises, penalizing expensive models; when below, $\lambda_t$ falls, releasing the router to pursue quality.

\paragraph{Reward model and parameter estimation.}
LinUCB assumes a linear reward model: the expected reward for arm $a$ on context $x_t$ is $\mathbb{E}[r_t \mid x_t, a_t{=}a] = \theta_a^{*\top} x_t$, where $\theta_a^* \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is an unknown parameter vector. The estimate $\hat\theta_a = A_a^{-1} b_a$ is the regularized least-squares (ridge regression) solution over all observed context--reward pairs for arm $a$, where
\begin{equation}
    A_a = \lambda_0 I + \textstyle\sum_{\tau:\, a_\tau = a} x_\tau x_\tau^\top, \qquad
    b_a = \textstyle\sum_{\tau:\, a_\tau = a} r_\tau\, x_\tau
    \label{eq:sufficient_stats}
\end{equation}
are the regularized design matrix and the reward-weighted context accumulator, respectively. These two quantities are the per-arm \emph{sufficient statistics}: all learning, forgetting, and warmup operations reduce to manipulations of $(A_a, b_a)$, and the parameter estimate is recovered by a single matrix solve. At cold start ($A_a = \lambda_0 I$, $b_a = \mathbf{0}$), $\hat\theta_a = \mathbf{0}$ and the confidence bonus $x_t^\top A_a^{-1} x_t$ is maximal, driving pure exploration. As observations accumulate, $A_a$ grows, the estimate converges toward $\theta_a^*$, and the confidence set shrinks, shifting the router toward exploitation. Each new observation $(x_t, r_t)$ for arm $a_t$ contributes a rank-1 update $A_{a_t} \leftarrow A_{a_t} + x_t x_t^\top$ and $b_{a_t} \leftarrow b_{a_t} + r_t x_t$; Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_learning} modifies this update with geometric discounting to handle non-stationarity.

\paragraph{Log-normalized cost.}
The term $\tilde{c}_a \in [0,1]$ is a log-normalized unit cost:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{c}_a = \frac{\log c_a - \log c_{\mathrm{floor}}}{\log c_{\mathrm{ceil}} - \log c_{\mathrm{floor}}}
    \label{eq:cost_norm}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\mathrm{floor}}$ and $c_{\mathrm{ceil}}$ are fixed market-floor and market-ceiling blended rates (respectively \$0.0001 and \$0.10 per 1k tokens). The logarithmic scale compresses the $530{\times}$ cost differential into a bounded penalty commensurate with the $[0,1]$ reward scale.

\paragraph{Two-layer enforcement (Adaptive mode).}
The budget pacer combines two mechanisms simultaneously:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Hard ceiling (safety net):} When $\lambda_t > 0$, the candidate set $\mathcal{A}_t$ excludes models whose blended price exceeds a dynamic ceiling $c_{\max}^{\mathcal{A}} / (1 + \lambda_t)$, where $c_{\max}^{\mathcal{A}} = \max_{a \in \mathcal{A}} c_a$ is the portfolio's most expensive rate. This acts as a circuit breaker, preventing catastrophic overspend on any single request.
  \item \textbf{Soft penalty (optimizer):} The dual penalty $\lambda_t \tilde{c}_a$ biases the UCB score toward cheaper models proportionally to their cost, allowing fine-grained, context-dependent routing within the surviving candidate set.
\end{itemize}
The hard ceiling provides per-request worst-case cost bounds independent of $\lambda_t$; the soft penalty provides smooth, quality-maximizing allocation within those bounds.

\paragraph{Theoretical status.}
Eq.~\ref{eq:budget_ucb} is a Lagrangian relaxation of the constrained routing problem (Eq.~\ref{eq:objective}). The cost penalty is structurally necessary to couple the reward learner to the budget, not a heuristic distortion of LinUCB. Standard LinUCB's $O(d\sqrt{T \log T})$ regret bound~\cite{abbasi2011improved} measures regret against the \emph{unconstrained} oracle; the correct comparator for budget-constrained routing is the best \emph{feasible} policy satisfying $\limsup \bar{c}_t \leq B$. Three departures from the classical BwK analysis~\cite{badanidiyuru2013bwk} (the rate constraint, EMA smoothing, and hard ceiling) void the formal $O(\sqrt{T})$ regret and violation bounds. For theoretical analysis of smoothed dual updates in online pacing, see Balseiro and Gur~\cite{balseiro2019learning}. Three concrete properties do hold in the implementation: (a)~$\lambda_t$ is bounded in $[0, \bar\lambda]$; (b)~staleness-based variance inflation (Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_learning}) provides a partial self-correction mechanism for neglected arms; (c)~the hard ceiling provides a per-request worst-case cost bound.

\subsection{Non-Stationary Adaptation via Geometric Forgetting}
\label{sec:adaptive_learning}

A standard LinUCB ($\gamma{=}1.0$) accumulates infinite memory, requiring hundreds or thousands of new observations to override stale estimates after a model degrades. ParetoBandit applies a forgetting factor $\gamma \in (0, 1]$ directly to the per-arm sufficient statistics. Before incorporating the new observation $(x_t, a_t, r_t)$, the design matrix and reward accumulator for the chosen arm $a_t$ are exponentially discounted:
\begin{align}
    A_{a_t} &\leftarrow \gamma^{\mathrm{dt}} \, A_{a_t} + x_t x_t^\top
    \label{eq:forget_A} \\
    b_{a_t} &\leftarrow \gamma^{\mathrm{dt}} \, b_{a_t} + r_t \, x_t
    \label{eq:forget_b}
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{dt} = t - \text{last\_update}_{a_t}$ is the number of intervening requests since $a_t$'s last update. This batched exponentiation avoids redundant per-step multiplications for arms that were not selected. The exponential weighting~\cite{garivier2011switching} gives observations an effective half-life of $\ln 2 / (1 - \gamma)$ steps and an e-folding time of $1 / (1 - \gamma)$. For $\gamma{=}0.997$, the e-folding time is ${\approx}333$ steps: an observation from 333 steps ago retains weight $\gamma^{333} \approx e^{-1} \approx 0.37$, and after ${\sim}1{,}000$ steps the prior's contribution is reduced to $\gamma^{1000} \approx 0.05$.

\paragraph{Staleness-based variance inflation.}
Geometric forgetting shrinks $A_a$ toward singularity for arms that have not been selected recently, which already increases the base variance $x_t^\top A_a^{-1} x_t$. However, this passive growth may be too slow to re-explore an arm whose environment has shifted while it was idle. To accelerate re-exploration, the router explicitly inflates the UCB variance of stale arms. For each candidate arm $a$, define the \emph{exploration staleness} $\mathrm{dt}_a = t - \max(\text{last\_update}_a,\, \text{last\_played}_a)$: the number of steps since $a$ was last updated \emph{or selected}. The inclusion of $\text{last\_played}_a$ is deliberate: when the router selects $a$ but the reward has not yet arrived (e.g., awaiting an asynchronous judge score), the arm should not be re-selected purely due to staleness. This distinguishes $\mathrm{dt}_a$ from the decay exponent $\mathrm{dt}' = t - \text{last\_update}_{a_t}$ in Eqs.~\ref{eq:forget_A}--\ref{eq:forget_b}, which tracks only the age of the sufficient statistics. The inflated variance is:
\begin{equation}
    v_a = \frac{x_t^\top A_a^{-1} x_t}{\max\!\bigl(\gamma^{\mathrm{dt}_a},\; V_{\max}^{-1}\bigr)}
    \label{eq:staleness_inflation}
\end{equation}
Because $\gamma^{\mathrm{dt}_a} < 1$ for any idle arm, the denominator shrinks with staleness, amplifying the exploration bonus. A capacity cap $V_{\max} = 200$ floors the denominator at $V_{\max}^{-1}$, bounding the worst-case inflation to $\sqrt{V_{\max}} \approx 14{\times}$ the uninflated bonus and preventing the exploration term from overwhelming the cost penalty.

\paragraph{Interaction with the budget pacer.}
Geometric forgetting interacts with budget pacing through a feedback loop. As $\gamma$ shrinks $A_a$ over time, the confidence bonus grows, increasing the probability of selecting expensive arms that happen to be uncertain. This drives up $\lambda_t$, which penalizes those arms, partially counteracting the inflation. The EMA smoothing in the dual update (Eq.~\ref{eq:cost_ema}) further dampens the resulting oscillations. The parameter $\gamma$ thus controls a bias-variance tradeoff between \emph{stability} (trusting historical evidence) and \emph{plasticity} (responding quickly to drift). The forgetting rate is selected jointly with $\alpha$ via the Pareto knee-point procedure in Section~\ref{sec:hparam_sweep}.

\subsection{Offline-to-Online Warmup Priors}
\label{sec:warmup}

Without warmup priors, the router begins with uninformative $A_a = \lambda_0 I$, $b_a = \mathbf{0}$, and must explore each model on diverse prompts before learning which model excels where. This cold-start transient wastes quality and budget. ParetoBandit eliminates it by loading offline sufficient statistics $(A_a^{\mathrm{off}}, b_a^{\mathrm{off}})$ fitted on historical prompt-reward data. A tunable prior strength $n_\mathit{eff}$ controls how many pseudo-observations the offline data contribute:
\begin{equation}
    s = \frac{n_\mathit{eff}}{A_a^{\mathrm{off}}[d, d]}
    \label{eq:prior_scale}
\end{equation}
where $A_a^{\mathrm{off}}[d, d]$ is the total precision mass in the bias direction (a proxy for the offline sample size). The scaled prior is then regularized:
\begin{align}
    A_a &\leftarrow s \cdot A_a^{\mathrm{off}} + \lambda_0 I
    \label{eq:prior_A} \\
    b_a &\leftarrow s \cdot b_a^{\mathrm{off}} + \lambda_0\, \hat\theta_a^{\mathrm{off}}
    \label{eq:prior_b}
\end{align}
The term $\lambda_0 \hat\theta_a^{\mathrm{off}}$ in the $b_a$ update is critical: without it, adding $\lambda_0 I$ to $A_a$ would shrink the posterior mean toward zero (Bayesian shrinkage), distorting the reward estimates the offline data encoded. This mean-preserving regularization ensures that $A_a^{-1} b_a \approx \hat\theta_a^{\mathrm{off}}$ at the desired confidence level.

For models absent from the offline data (e.g., a newly launched endpoint), a heuristic prior places $n_\mathit{eff}$ pseudo-observations at isotropic uncertainty with a bias-only reward prediction based on registry metadata. A two-pass calibration checks that no arm's prediction exceeds a safe range, applying theta-reconstruction or global rescaling as needed.

Warmup priors interact naturally with geometric forgetting. After ${\sim}1{,}000$ steps at $\gamma{=}0.997$, the prior's contribution is reduced to $\gamma^{1000} \approx 0.05$ of its original weight; the router's steady-state quality is determined by online evidence regardless of initialization. In the unconstrained regime, priors remain valuable throughout: a burn-in ablation (Appendix~\ref{appendix:val_burnin_ablation}) shows that skipping all online learning before evaluation \emph{reduces} test regret by 6.6\%, because the offline estimate ($n_\mathit{eff}{=}\bdNeff$, trained on 8{,}374 examples) outperforms the ${\sim}333$ effective online observations that geometric forgetting retains. Priors thus serve as a deployment latency optimization: they provide informed routing from request \#1, eliminating the cold-start cost that would otherwise accumulate during initial learning. Crucially, priors are beneficial but not necessary---the system functions correctly without them, as the UCB exploration bonus (Section~\ref{sec:budget_pacer}) drives sufficient exploration from an uninformative cold start; Section~\ref{sec:eval_onboarding} demonstrates this empirically with a no-prior model addition.

The prior strength $n_\mathit{eff}$ acts as a safety valve: small values let online evidence override quickly, while large values trust priors longer; systematically inverted priors cause harm that scales with $n_\mathit{eff}$ (Appendix~\ref{appendix:prior_mismatch}).

\subsection{Computational Efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}

The router sits on the critical path of every LLM request, so per-step latency must be negligible relative to model inference time.

\paragraph{$O(d^2)$ updates via Sherman--Morrison.}
Rather than recomputing $A_a^{-1}$ from scratch after each observation ($O(d^3)$), ParetoBandit maintains a cached inverse and applies the standard Sherman--Morrison rank-1 update in $O(d^2)$. When $\gamma < 1.0$, discounting $A_a \leftarrow \gamma A_a$ requires only a scalar division $A_a^{-1} \leftarrow A_a^{-1} / \gamma$, keeping the total per-update cost at $O(d^2)$. A full $O(d^3)$ recomputation is triggered only as a rare numerical-stability safeguard; in practice fewer than one in every thousand updates.

\paragraph{$O(Kd)$ arm selection.}
The expected reward $\hat\theta_a^\top x_t$ is a single $O(d)$ dot product per arm because $\hat\theta_a = A_a^{-1} b_a$ is cached and refreshed only at update time. The UCB exploration bonus requires one $O(d^2)$ quadratic form $x_t^\top A_a^{-1} x_t$ per arm. With $K{=}3$ arms and $d{=}26$, the full selection pass amounts to ${\sim}2{,}100$ floating-point operations.

\paragraph{Empirical validation.}
A latency microbenchmark (Appendix~\ref{sec:latency_benchmark}) confirms that the production configuration ($d{=}26$, Sherman--Morrison) completes a full route-and-update cycle in $43\,\mu$s (p50) and sustains ${\sim}22{,}000$\,req/s on a single CPU core. Including prompt embedding and PCA projection, the full end-to-end routing pipeline completes in $9.2$\,ms on CPU, adding ${<}\,1\%$ overhead to a typical ${\sim}1$\,s model inference call~\cite{ganglani2026llmlatency}. The combined effect of PCA dimensionality reduction ($d{=}26$ vs.\ $d{=}385$) and Sherman--Morrison inverse caching yields a ${\sim}42{\times}$ throughput improvement over a cached-inversion baseline at raw dimension ($539$ vs.\ $22{,}432$\,req/s; Appendix~\ref{sec:latency_benchmark}).

\subsection{Runtime Portfolio Management}
\label{sec:production_infra}

Production model portfolios are not static. ParetoBandit supports runtime model additions and removals via \texttt{add\_arm()} and \texttt{delete\_arm()} without restarting the router. A newly added model is initialized with either a heuristic prior or uninformative covariance; the bandit's exploration bonus discovers its quality-cost niche from live traffic within approximately $1/(1{-}\gamma)$ steps. The context vector $x_t$ is cached at route time so that feedback arriving later (synchronous judge scores, asynchronous RLHF labels, or batch metrics) can update the bandit without re-encoding the prompt, with both in-memory and SQLite-backed storage backends.
