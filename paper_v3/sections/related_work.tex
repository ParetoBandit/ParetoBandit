\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Supervised and static routers.}
FrugalGPT~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt} pioneered cost-aware LLM routing, demonstrating that sequential cascades---querying models from cheapest to most expensive until a quality threshold is met---can reduce inference costs by up to 98\% while matching the best single-model quality.
A rich line of subsequent work has produced increasingly effective single-dispatch classifiers trained offline.
RouteLLM~\cite{ong2024routellm} achieves strong quality--cost Pareto trade-offs by learning routing policies from pairwise preference data, requiring only two models to match GPT-4 quality at a fraction of the cost.
GraphRouter~\cite{feng2025graphrouter} captures task--query--model interactions through a heterogeneous graph, outperforming baselines on diverse benchmarks.
RouterDC~\cite{chen2024routerdc} learns dual-contrastive embeddings that map queries toward their best-performing model, achieving state-of-the-art routing accuracy.
CSCR~\cite{shirkavand2025cscr} extends contrastive routing with an explicit cost-aware objective, delivering microsecond-scale kNN routing decisions via FAISS.
HybridLLM~\cite{ding2024hybridllm} shows that even a lightweight BERT classifier can achieve effective binary strong/weak triage, while cross-attention architectures~\cite{pulishetty2025onehead} jointly predict quality and cost by modelling prompt--model feature interactions.
OmniRouter~\cite{ma2025omnirouter} formulates budget-aware routing as a batch-level constrained optimisation, solving a Lagrangian dual over a fixed prompt set to achieve strong offline budget--quality trade-offs.
These methods perform well when deployment conditions match training, and several report cost savings of 2--12$\times$ over frontier-model-only baselines.
However, all deploy frozen policies: when model quality drifts or pricing changes mid-stream, they cannot adapt without an offline retraining cycle.
OmniRouter's batch formulation further requires the full workload upfront, precluding open-ended serving.
Several systems also incur onboarding costs---RouteLLM's matrix-factorization variant, for example, must evaluate each new model on ${\sim}20$--$50$ curated benchmark prompts offline before it can participate in routing.
ParetoBandit retains the same goal of quality--cost optimization but replaces the frozen policy with online learning, enabling adaptation to price drops, quality regressions, and portfolio changes without retraining or downtime.

\paragraph{Adaptive and bandit-based routers.}
A growing line of work frames routing as an online learning problem, enabling the router to improve from deployment traffic.
PILOT~\cite{panda2025pilot} provides a principled integration of LinUCB with budget awareness, using preference-prior initialization and an online knapsack algorithm to enforce cumulative spend limits; it demonstrates that contextual bandits can outperform supervised baselines when deployment prompts differ from training data.
BaRP~\cite{wang2025barp} introduces entropy-regularized policy gradients conditioned on a user preference vector, enabling operators to sweep the quality--cost trade-off at inference time without retraining.
LLM Bandit~\cite{li2025llmbandit} generalizes further by conditioning on model identity features, allowing the policy to transfer to unseen models without re-evaluation.
MixLLM~\cite{wang2025mixllm} is the only bandit-based router to explicitly model downstream latency, penalising endpoint congestion via an estimated waiting-time signal and accommodating evolving model pools through continual learning.
xRouter~\cite{qian2025xrouter} fine-tunes a small LM as an RL orchestration agent, achieving competitive routing quality.
PROTEUS~\cite{bhatti2026proteus} is the closest prior work on cost control: it trains a Lagrangian RL policy against SLA constraints and demonstrates strong compliance across large portfolios ($K{=}11$ on Routerbench, $K{=}14$ on SPROUT), explicitly identifying post-deployment cost drift as an open problem.
Despite these advances, two gaps remain.
First, none of the above provides closed-loop online budget pacing over an open-ended request stream: PILOT's knapsack requires a known finite horizon; BaRP and LLM Bandit accept preference weights but do not track realized spend against a dollar target; PROTEUS freezes its dual variables at deployment.
Second, all use infinite memory ($\gamma{=}1.0$), and none evaluates router behaviour under non-stationarity---neither price changes nor quality drift.
ParetoBandit extends this adaptive line of work with two additions: a rate-constrained budget pacer that enforces a per-request dollar target in closed loop, and geometric forgetting that gives the bandit a bounded memory window for tracking environment shifts.

\paragraph{Constrained and non-stationary bandits.}
ParetoBandit draws on three well-established theoretical streams and composes them into a single routing system.
For budget enforcement, we adapt the Lagrangian relaxation of Bandits with Knapsacks~\cite{badanidiyuru2013bwk, agrawal2016linear}---which provides near-optimal regret guarantees under cumulative resource constraints---from a cumulative constraint to a rate constraint suitable for open-ended serving, with smoothed dual updates following the online pacing framework of Balseiro and Gur~\cite{balseiro2019learning}.
For non-stationarity, we apply the geometric discounting of Garivier and Moulines~\cite{garivier2011switching} and the weighted ridge regression of Russac et~al.~\cite{russac2019weighted} to the LinUCB sufficient statistics, giving stale observations an exponentially decaying influence while preserving sublinear regret in piecewise-stationary environments.
For cold-start elimination, we inject offline-fitted priors following the warm-start contextual bandit framework of Kveton et~al.~\cite{kveton2023warmstart}, which shows that even approximate priors substantially reduce initial regret; related work has also explored LLM-generated priors for bandit initialization~\cite{pan2024cbli}.
Each ingredient is individually well-studied; the contribution of ParetoBandit is their joint integration---rate-constrained online pacing, geometric forgetting, and warm-start priors---within a single contextual bandit LLM router, together with empirical evaluation under cost drift, quality degradation, and cold-start onboarding scenarios that, to our knowledge, no prior LLM routing paper has addressed.
