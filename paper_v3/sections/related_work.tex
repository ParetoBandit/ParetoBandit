\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Supervised and static routers.}
FrugalGPT~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt} pioneered cost-aware LLM routing via sequential cascades that query models from cheapest to most expensive until a quality threshold is met, trading latency variance for cost savings.
Subsequent work moved to single-dispatch classifiers trained offline:
RouteLLM~\cite{ong2024routellm} learns from pairwise preferences,
GraphRouter~\cite{feng2025graphrouter} predicts edges in a task--query--LLM heterogeneous graph,
RouterDC~\cite{chen2024routerdc} learns dual-contrastive embeddings that map queries toward their best-performing model,
CSCR~\cite{shirkavand2025cscr} extends contrastive routing with an explicit cost-aware objective,
and HybridLLM~\cite{ding2024hybridllm} trains a BERT classifier for binary strong/weak triage.
Cross-attention architectures~\cite{pulishetty2025onehead} jointly predict quality and cost by modelling prompt--model feature interactions.
OmniRouter~\cite{ma2025omnirouter} formulates budget-aware routing as a batch-level constrained optimisation, solving a Lagrangian dual over a fixed prompt set; this achieves strong offline trade-offs but requires the full workload upfront, precluding open-ended serving.
All of these approaches deploy frozen policies: when model quality drifts or pricing changes mid-stream, they cannot adapt without offline retraining.

\paragraph{Adaptive and bandit-based routers.}
A growing line of work frames routing as an online learning problem.
PILOT~\cite{panda2025pilot} extends LinUCB with preference-prior initialization and enforces a cumulative budget via an online knapsack algorithm, but requires a known finite horizon and uses infinite memory ($\gamma{=}1.0$).
BaRP~\cite{wang2025barp} uses entropy-regularized policy gradients conditioned on a user preference vector;
LLM Bandit~\cite{li2025llmbandit} likewise accepts user-specified quality--cost preferences and further generalizes to unseen models via model identity features.
Both let operators tune the trade-off at inference time via a scalarized reward $r - \lambda c$, but neither tracks realized spend against a budget target.
MixLLM~\cite{wang2025mixllm} accommodates evolving model pools via continual learning,
xRouter~\cite{qian2025xrouter} fine-tunes a small LM as an RL orchestration agent,
and C2MAB-V~\cite{dai2024c2mab} selects model subsets via combinatorial bandits.
PROTEUS~\cite{bhatti2026proteus} is the closest prior work on cost control: it trains a Lagrangian RL policy against SLA constraints across $K{=}11$--$14$ models, but its dual variables are learned offline and frozen at deployment, so the policy cannot react to post-training price or quality shifts.
None of the above evaluates router behaviour under non-stationarity (reward or cost drift), and none provides closed-loop online budget pacing over an open-ended request stream.

\paragraph{Constrained and non-stationary bandits.}
ParetoBandit draws on three theoretical streams.
For budget enforcement, we adapt the Lagrangian relaxation of Bandits with Knapsacks~\cite{badanidiyuru2013bwk, agrawal2016linear} from a cumulative constraint to a rate constraint suitable for open-ended serving, with smoothed dual updates following Balseiro and Gur~\cite{balseiro2019learning}.
For non-stationarity, we apply geometric discounting~\cite{garivier2011switching} and weighted ridge regression~\cite{russac2019weighted} to the LinUCB sufficient statistics, giving stale observations an exponentially decaying influence.
For cold-start elimination, we inject offline-fitted priors following the warm-start contextual bandit framework of Kveton et~al.~\cite{kveton2023warmstart}; related work has also explored LLM-generated priors for bandit initialization~\cite{pan2024cbli}.
To our knowledge, ParetoBandit is the first system to integrate rate-constrained online pacing with geometric forgetting in a contextual bandit LLM router.
