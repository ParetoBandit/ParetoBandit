\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Supervised and static routers.}
Cost-aware cascading~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt} queries models cheapest-first until a quality threshold is met.
Single-dispatch classifiers learn from preference data~\cite{ong2024routellm}, contrastive embeddings~\cite{chen2024routerdc, shirkavand2025cscr}, graph-structured interactions~\cite{feng2025graphrouter}, or lightweight binary triage~\cite{ding2024hybridllm, pulishetty2025onehead}.
OmniRouter~\cite{ma2025omnirouter} formulates budget-aware routing as a batch-level constrained optimisation over a fixed prompt set.
These methods perform well when deployment matches training, but share three structural limitations: cost enters as a manually tuned weight rather than a closed-loop constraint; none adapts autonomously to price or quality shifts; and frozen policies cannot discover where a new model excels.
ParetoBandit replaces the frozen policy with online learning, enabling adaptation without retraining or downtime.

\paragraph{Adaptive and bandit-based routers.}
A growing line of work frames routing as online learning.
PILOT~\cite{panda2025pilot} integrates LinUCB with an online knapsack to enforce cumulative spend limits over a known horizon.
BaRP~\cite{wang2025barp} uses entropy-regularized policy gradients conditioned on a preference vector;
LLM Bandit~\cite{li2025llmbandit} conditions on model identity features to transfer across unseen models;
MixLLM~\cite{wang2025mixllm} models downstream latency and evolving model pools;
xRouter~\cite{qian2025xrouter} fine-tunes a small LM as an RL orchestrator.
PROTEUS~\cite{bhatti2026proteus} trains a Lagrangian RL policy to minimize cost subject to an accuracy floor~$\tau$; ParetoBandit solves the dual: maximize quality given a dollar budget (Eq.~\ref{eq:objective}).
Despite these advances, two gaps remain: no system provides closed-loop budget pacing over an open-ended stream (PILOT requires a known horizon; BaRP and LLM Bandit track preference weights, not dollars; PROTEUS freezes its dual variables at deployment), and to our knowledge all use infinite memory ($\gamma{=}1.0$) with no evaluation under non-stationarity.
ParetoBandit addresses both gaps with an online dual variable for rate-constrained budget pacing and geometric forgetting for bounded-memory adaptation.

\paragraph{Constrained and non-stationary bandits.}
ParetoBandit composes three theoretical streams: Lagrangian relaxation of BwK~\cite{badanidiyuru2013bwk, agrawal2016linear} adapted to a rate constraint with smoothed duals~\cite{balseiro2019learning}; geometric discounting~\cite{garivier2011switching, russac2019weighted} for non-stationarity; and warm-start priors~\cite{oetomo2023warmstart, pan2024cbli}. Each ingredient is well-studied individually; the contribution is their joint integration and empirical evaluation under cost drift, quality degradation, and cold-start onboarding.
