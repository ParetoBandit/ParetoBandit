\caption{\textbf{Multi-model routing at $K{=}5$ and $K{=}10$: cost--quality Pareto frontiers, convergence, and traffic allocation.}
\textbf{(a,b)}~Pareto frontiers sweeping cost penalty $\lambda \in [0, 5]$ ($n{=}20$ seeds per point). Green triangles mark static single-model baselines; the blue curve traces banditGPT's operating envelope. At peak quality, banditGPT achieves 0.954 (K{=}5, $\lambda{=}0$) and 0.940 (K{=}10, $\lambda{=}0.1$). At $\lambda{=}5$ (cost-minimising), it routes nearly all traffic to the cheapest model while maintaining 0.756 (K{=}5) and 0.920 (K{=}10) quality. The orange dashed line (no Corralling) shows the ablated system without the meta-learner.
\textbf{(c)}~Convergence from warmup priors: both portfolios start at ${\sim}0.94$ reward (step~0, no online learning), demonstrating that the 43-model warmup priors provide effective cold-start quality across portfolio sizes. K{=}5 improves to 0.954 by step 533; K{=}10 remains stable at ${\sim}0.93{-}0.94$.
\textbf{(d)}~Traffic allocation on the holdout set ($\lambda{=}0$). At K{=}5, the router concentrates on three high-quality models (GPT-4.1: 37\%, Gemini-Flash: 27\%, Claude-Sonnet: 24\%) while using cheaper models sparingly (6\% each). At K{=}10, traffic distributes across all models (4--16\% each), reflecting contextual per-prompt routing rather than static model selection.
Evaluation protocol: 533 online-learning prompts (three-way split, disjoint from prior training), frozen holdout of 750 prompts (identical to Section~\ref{sec:pareto}).}
\label{fig:multimodel_pareto}
\end{figure*}
