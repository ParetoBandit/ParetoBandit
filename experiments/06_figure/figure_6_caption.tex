\caption{\textbf{Multi-model routing at $K{=}5$ and $K{=}10$: cost--quality Pareto frontiers, convergence, and traffic allocation.}
Reward is mean judge agreement (continuous in $[0,1]$).
\textbf{(a,b)}~Pareto frontiers for cost penalty $\lambda \in [0, 5]$ ($n{=}20$ seeds; log-scaled cost). Green triangles mark Pareto-efficient static baselines; faded ones are dominated. banditGPT (blue curve) peaks at 0.911 ($K{=}5, \lambda{=}0.02$) and 0.898 ($K{=}10, \lambda{=}0.02$). At $\lambda{=}5$ (cost-minimising), it shifts traffic to the cheapest model, maintaining quality of 0.708 ($K{=}5$) and 0.888 ($K{=}10$). Dashed orange lines show ablation without Corralling.
\textbf{(c)}~Convergence: warmup priors yield effective cold-start quality (${\sim}0.90$ reward). $K{=}5$ improves to 0.911 by step 533; $K{=}10$ remains at ${\sim}0.88{-}0.90$.
\textbf{(d)}~Traffic allocation ($\lambda{=}0$). At $K{=}5$, the router favours Claude-Sonnet (32\%), GPT-4.1 (29\%), and Gemini-Flash (28\%). At $K{=}10$, traffic spreads across all models (3--17\%), highlighting per-prompt contextual routing.
Evaluation uses 533 online-learning and 750 holdout prompts.}
\label{fig:multimodel_pareto}
