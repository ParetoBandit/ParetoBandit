\textbf{Catastrophic failure dynamics: Normal $\to$ Mistral failure
$\to$ Recovered ($K{=}3$, 20~seeds, 95\% bootstrap CI).}
Solid coloured lines show ParetoBandit at three budget levels;
dashed grey and orange show the Naive Bandit and Forgetting Bandit
at moderate budget; dash-dot green is unconstrained.
%
\textbf{(a)}~Gemini-Pro selection fraction.  Budget-constrained
conditions show near-zero Gemini adoption throughout: when Mistral
fails, traffic shifts to cheaper Llama-8B rather than expensive
Gemini.  The unconstrained baseline freely shifts ${\sim}80\%$ to
Gemini.
%
\textbf{(b)}~Windowed mean reward (trailing 50~steps).  All adaptive
conditions suffer an initial trough when Mistral fails; the
Forgetting Bandit (orange) recovers fastest due to geometric
forgetting, while the Naive Bandit (grey) recovers comparably
to ParetoBandit---but panel~(c) reveals the cost.
%
\textbf{(c)}~Windowed average cost per request; dotted lines mark
budget targets.  ParetoBandit hugs the target throughout the failure.
The Naive Bandit's cost explodes in Phase~2 and worsens in Phase~3;
the Forgetting Bandit overshoots from Phase~1 onward, confirming
that the BudgetPacer---not forgetting---is responsible for cost
invariance.
See Table~\ref{tab:failure_response} for quantitative comparisons
across all five conditions.
