\subsection{Distribution Shift, Online Learning, and Corralling}
\label{sec:distribution_shift}

The $K{=}2$ evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:pareto}) uses warmup priors constructed from 80,000 RouteLLM battle prompts---a deliberately \emph{cross-distribution} source (Section~\ref{sec:warmup_prior_construction}).
A natural question is: how much does this distribution mismatch hurt, and does it matter once the system adapts online?
More pointedly: does banditGPT's Corralling architecture provide value beyond what any standard bandit router would achieve?

We answer these questions with a \textbf{full factorial design} that crosses three factors---prior source, online adaptation, and the Corralling meta-learner---to cleanly decompose their individual contributions.

\subsubsection{Measuring Feature Distribution Shift}

We embed 8,000 unique RouteLLM battle prompts and 1,871 LMSYS Arena deployment prompts (dev + holdout) using the same PCA projection as the router and measure distributional divergence on the first principal component.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Population Stability Index:} PSI $= 0.763$ [0.688, 0.860] (bootstrap 95\% CI, $B{=}1{,}000$). Under standard industry thresholds (PSI $> 0.25$: ``substantial shift''), this confirms a large distributional gap.
    \item \textbf{Kolmogorov--Smirnov test:} $D = 0.310$, $p < 10^{-129}$, rejecting the null hypothesis of identical distributions.
\end{itemize}

\noindent We verified \textbf{zero prompt overlap} between the two sources, confirming that the distributional gap is not an artifact of duplicate prompts with different labels.

\subsubsection{Experimental Design}

\paragraph{Data splits.}
The 1,121 LMSYS dev prompts are split: 355 prompts form a \emph{prior pool} from which same-distribution priors are built (matching the paper's three-way split), and 766 prompts form the \emph{online pool} used for training.
The 750 holdout prompts are shared across all conditions and are never used for prior construction or training.

\paragraph{Prior sources.}
\emph{Cross-distribution} priors are the standard RouteLLM 80K battle priors (Section~\ref{sec:warmup_prior_construction}), which exhibit $-75\%$ miscalibration on Mixtral.
\emph{Same-distribution} priors are built from the 355-prompt LMSYS prior pool using the same LinUCB accumulation pipeline and plasticity ($\rho{=}0.1$), yielding correctly calibrated but information-sparse priors (${\sim}35$ effective observations per arm).

\paragraph{Six conditions.}
We evaluate a $2 \times 3$ factorial: two prior sources crossed with three adaptation modes.

\noindent
Frozen conditions use Corralling's two-expert architecture but disable \texttt{process\_feedback()}, so neither LinUCB parameters nor expert weights ever update.
``Online (no Corral)'' conditions use a single Hybrid LinUCB expert with online learning---this represents what any standard contextual bandit router would achieve.
``Online (Corralling)'' conditions use the full banditGPT production architecture.
All conditions sweep the same $\lambda$ values (11 points, 20 trials each, 95\% CIs).

\paragraph{Learning curves.}
For all four adaptive conditions (B, D, E, F), we additionally track holdout reward at intermediate checkpoints (50, 100, 200, 400, 766 training steps) at $\lambda{=}0$ to measure convergence speed.

\subsubsection{Statistical Significance (Paired Analysis)}

Because each trial uses the same random seed across conditions, the comparisons are naturally paired.  We increase to 50 trials per condition and apply paired tests at $\lambda{=}0$ (quality-maximising point).  Table~\ref{tab:dist_shift_paired} reports the results.

\noindent The Corralling benefit is \textbf{highly significant}: $p < 0.0001$ under both prior sources and when pooled, with a large effect size (Cohen's $d \approx 1.0$).
Corralling wins in 83 of 96 non-tied trial pairs (86\%).
In contrast, the distribution shift effect is \textbf{not significant}: the 95\% confidence interval includes zero in both comparisons, with negligible effect size ($|d| < 0.2$).
These results were confirmed by Wilcoxon signed-rank tests ($p < 0.0001$ for Corralling; $p > 0.18$ for distribution shift).

\subsubsection{Interpretation}

\paragraph{Online learning is the dominant effect ($+7.0$\,pp).}
Switching from a frozen prior to any online bandit---even a single expert without Corralling---yields a $+7.0$\,pp improvement, consistent across both prior sources.
Any bandit-based router would capture the bulk of this improvement; online adaptation, not matched priors, is the primary mechanism for handling distribution shift.

\paragraph{Corralling provides a significant additional gain ($+1.3$\,pp, $p < 0.0001$).}
Layering Corralling on top of online learning adds $+1.3$\,pp (pooled across prior sources), with a large paired effect size (Cohen's $d = 0.98$).
The learning curves (Figure~\ref{fig:distribution_shift}c) show that Corralling leads at \emph{every} checkpoint from step~50 onward: it does not merely converge faster, but converges to a \emph{higher} steady-state reward.
This separates banditGPT from a standard contextual bandit: the two-expert architecture with adaptive meta-learning consistently outperforms a single-expert bandit regardless of prior quality.

\paragraph{Distribution shift has no significant practical impact ($p > 0.18$).}
Despite PSI$\,{=}\,0.763$ (``substantial'' by industry standards) and $-75\%$ prior miscalibration on Mixtral, the practical performance difference between cross- and same-distribution priors is not statistically significant once online learning is enabled.
The 95\% CI includes zero in both comparisons.
Online learning rapidly overwrites the prior: within 766 interactions, the initial prior is a negligible fraction of the total evidence.

\paragraph{Practical implications.}
These results establish a clear hierarchy of priorities for deployed LLM routing:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Enable online learning} ($+7.0$\,pp). This is the single most impactful decision, available to any bandit-based router.
    \item \textbf{Use Corralling} ($+1.3$\,pp, $p < 0.0001$). The meta-learning architecture provides a statistically significant steady-state advantage over a single-expert bandit. This is banditGPT's primary architectural contribution.
    \item \textbf{Prior source is irrelevant} ($+0.3$\,pp, $p = 0.18$). Practitioners can safely initialise with any available offline data---including cross-distribution sources---without measurable penalty once online learning is enabled.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The case for Corralling is not that it solves distribution shift per se---any online bandit handles distribution shift naturally.
Rather, Corralling provides \emph{architectural robustness with a significant steady-state quality advantage}: the two-expert meta-learner converges to a higher reward plateau ($+1.3$\,pp, $d \approx 1.0$) than a single-expert bandit, by adaptively blending informed and uninformed experts throughout the learning trajectory.
