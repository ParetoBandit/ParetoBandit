\caption{\textbf{Family parameter sharing improves routing stability as portfolio size grows.}
\textbf{Top row:} Rolling online reward (window${}=50$) during training on 888 real prompts.
\textbf{Bottom row:} Greedy holdout reward (750 prompts) evaluated at 25-step intervals.
Each column shows a portfolio of $K{=}5$, $10$, or $20$ models; shaded bands are 95\% CIs ($n{=}20$ seeds).
Family assignments are data-driven: within-provider tetrachoric correlation ($r_{\text{tet}} \geq 0.6$) with connected-components clustering.
At $K{=}5$, Hybrid achieves significantly higher holdout quality ($0.966$ vs.\ $0.937$, $p{=}0.008$) with $3.2\times$ narrower confidence intervals.
At $K{=}20$ (44 observations/model), Hybrid again shows $3.1\times$ tighter CIs ($\pm 0.007$ vs.\ $\pm 0.022$), demonstrating that family sharing stabilises learning when per-model data is scarce.
Neither method reaches the Oracle (1.000) within 888 steps, confirming that larger portfolios require more data---but Hybrid converges more reliably.}
\label{fig:k_scaling}
