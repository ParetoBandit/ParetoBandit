\caption{\textbf{Family parameter sharing improves routing stability as portfolio size grows.}
Greedy holdout reward (750 prompts) at 25-step intervals during training on 888 prompts.
Columns show portfolios of $K \in \{5, 10, 20\}$ models; bands are 95\% CIs ($n{=}20$ seeds).
Family assignments use within-provider tetrachoric correlation ($r_{\text{tet}} \geq 0.6$).
At $K{=}5$, Hybrid yields higher holdout quality ($0.966$ vs.\ $0.937$, $p{=}0.008$) with $3.2\times$ narrower CIs.
At $K{=}20$ (44 obs/model), Hybrid maintains $3.1\times$ tighter CIs ($\pm 0.007$ vs.\ $\pm 0.022$), showing family sharing stabilises learning under data scarcity.
Neither method reaches the Oracle ($1.000$) within 888 steps, though Hybrid converges more reliably.}
