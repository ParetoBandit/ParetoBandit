\subsubsection{Ablation: Sample Efficiency of Family Parameter Sharing}
\label{sec:k_scaling}

We isolate the contribution of Hybrid LinUCB's family sharing by comparing it against Disjoint LinUCB (independent models) at $K{\in}\{5, 10, 20\}$. Both policies start tabula rasa, training on 888 prompts. We use data-driven family assignments via within-provider tetrachoric correlation ($r_{\text{tet}} \geq 0.6$) to correct for binary reward base-rate attenuation, avoiding naive syntactic groupings (e.g., separating uncorrelated \texttt{gpt-4-turbo} and \texttt{gpt-4.1}).

\paragraph{Results \& Implications.}
Table~\ref{tab:k_scaling} and Figure~\ref{fig:k_scaling} show Hybrid LinUCB produces significantly more stable convergence. At $K{=}5$ and $K{=}20$, Hybrid's 95\% CI is $3\times$ narrower. This stability advantage arises because shared $\betavec_F$ parameters pool observations, providing a well-estimated baseline when per-model data is scarce ($44$~obs/model at $K{=}20$). 

For practitioners, this stability gain matters more than the mean gain: compressing the failure tail ensures predictable user experience. Furthermore, data-driven family assignment correctly groups models by empirical reward similarity rather than flawed syntactic naming conventions.
