\subsubsection{RQ3: Ablation---Sample Efficiency of Family Parameter Sharing}
\label{sec:k_scaling}

We isolate the contribution of Hybrid LinUCB's family sharing by comparing it against Disjoint LinUCB (independent models) at $K{\in}\{5, 10\}$, using the \textbf{full production router} (Corralling meta-learner with warmup and tabula rasa experts, exercised via \texttt{route()} / \texttt{process\_feedback()}). Both portfolios include multi-member providers so that shared families form at every $K$, controlling family density across the comparison. Family assignments use within-provider tetrachoric correlation ($r_{\text{tet}} \geq 0.6$) computed on the \textbf{training set only}, preventing target leakage from the holdout evaluation.

\paragraph{Results \& Implications.}
Table~\ref{tab:k_scaling} and Figure~\ref{fig:k_scaling} show that Hybrid LinUCB consistently reduces convergence variance across portfolio sizes. At $K{=}5$, two shared families emerge---OpenAI \{GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-4.1\} and Meta-Llama \{8B, Maverick\}---covering 4 of 5 models, and Hybrid produces $1.8\times$ narrower 95\% CIs ($\pm 0.018$ vs.\ $\pm 0.033$) with a marginally significant mean advantage ($0.862$ vs.\ $0.820$, $p{=}0.078$). At $K{=}10$, a third shared family (Google \{Flash, Gemma\}) appears and the CI compression grows to $2.1\times$ ($\pm 0.017$ vs.\ $\pm 0.035$), though the mean difference is not significant ($p{=}0.160$).

The arm-pull distribution column confirms that per-model data scarcity is more severe than uniform assignment suggests: at $K{=}10$, median observations per model is 47 but the range spans 0--120 under Hybrid exploitation, with some arms receiving no observations in individual trials. Cumulative online reward differences are not significant at either $K$ ($p > 0.05$), confirming that Hybrid's primary advantage is variance reduction rather than mean improvement. For practitioners, this stability gain matters: compressing the failure tail ensures predictable user experience when deploying the production Corralling router with growing model portfolios.
