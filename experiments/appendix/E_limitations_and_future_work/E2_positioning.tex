\subsection{Positioning Among Bandit-Type LLM Routers}
\label{appendix:positioning}

The LLM routing landscape increasingly includes bandit-based approaches.
Table~\ref{tab:bandit_taxonomy} provides a structured comparison across four
families, from simple MABs to banditGPT's integrated architecture.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Key Differentiators
% --------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{1.\ Rich Prior and Registration Machinery.}
banditGPT maintains three prior layers: (a)~warmup priors from 80k RouteLLM
battles, (b)~heuristic priors for models absent from the warmup data, and
(c)~T-shirt sizing with speed-based bias injection (\texttt{fast}/
\texttt{balanced}/\texttt{slow}).
New models are admitted via \texttt{admix\_theta\_from\_neighbors}, which
copies $\theta$ from the nearest neighbor in a ``model DNA'' embedding space
while resetting $A$ to $\neff \cdot I$, inheriting preferences without
inheriting over-confidence.
Progressive registration (Tier~A/B/C) is more elaborate than typical bandit
routers, which usually add a zero-initialized arm with $\lambda I$.
By contrast, PILOT learns a shared embedding space from scratch, and BaRP
trains under bandit feedback---both effective strategies but without
the warm-start advantage that 80k pre-existing battles provide.

\paragraph{2.\ Production Engineering Around LinUCB.}
The \texttt{DisjointLinUCBPolicy} in \texttt{router.py} includes per-model
locks, a global lock, Sherman--Morrison $O(d^2)$ updates with automatic
$O(d^3)$ fallback when the denominator is small, forgetting factors,
staleness-aware variance inflation, and a full numerical-stability pipeline
(trace-based checks, regularization resets, proactive regularization floor).
State save/load includes dimension validation and matrix-shape checks.
Thread-safe cloning uses \texttt{deepcopy} with atomic arm add/remove.
These engineering safeguards are absent from typical research-grade
implementations and are essential for production deployments where numerical
instability or race conditions would degrade routing quality silently.

\paragraph{3.\ Corralling Meta-Learner (Bandits over Bandits).}
Unlike PILOT, BaRP, and LLM Bandit---each of which uses a single contextual
bandit---banditGPT runs an Exp4-style Corralling
layer~\cite{agarwal2017corralling} that maintains trust weights over two
expert bandits: one initialized from warmup priors and one starting from
scratch.
The meta-learner receives an optional cost weight $\lambda$ so it operates on
$\text{quality} - \lambda \cdot \text{cost}$ rewards, enabling global
cost--quality trade-offs on top of per-expert learning.
This ``bandits over bandits'' structure provides the safety guarantee
formalized in Appendix~\ref{appendix:mathematical_foundations}: even under
severe prior mismatch, total regret is bounded by tabula-rasa performance
plus $O(\sqrt{T})$ meta-overhead (Corollary~\ref{cor:neg_transfer}).
No concurrent bandit-based router provides an analogous meta-ensemble or
formal safety bound.

\paragraph{4.\ Explicit Constraint Handling and Market Anchoring.}
\texttt{RouterConfig} carries \texttt{market\_cost\_floor/ceiling} and
\texttt{latency\_floor/ceiling}, plus pessimistic defaults when registry
metadata is missing (unknown models are treated as expensive and slow rather
than blowing up the budget).
Hard constraints (\texttt{max\_cost}, \texttt{max\_latency},
\texttt{quality\_floor}) are enforced via Dynamic Pareto Filtering
\emph{before} the bandit selects, so the bandit operates only over the
feasible set.
PILOT handles budget via a knapsack policy that selects a cost tier per
query; BaRP scalarizes cost into the reward.
banditGPT's approach guarantees constraint satisfaction per-request rather
than in expectation over a horizon.

\paragraph{5.\ Feature Architecture and Observability.}
banditGPT separates concerns into a pluggable \texttt{FeatureService}
(``Eyes''), the bandit policy (``Brain''), and a \texttt{ContextStore}
(``Memory''), a more modular architecture than most academic routers which
bake feature extraction into the model.
Two observability features are uncommon in simpler routers:
\texttt{explain\_decision} decomposes $\theta^\top x$ into per-feature
contributions, and \texttt{get\_probabilities} approximates the probability
each model is optimal via posterior sampling---useful for monitoring,
debugging, and A/B testing in production.

\paragraph{Advantages of Concurrent Work.}
We note areas where PILOT and BaRP advance the state of the art beyond
banditGPT's current design:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Learned embedding spaces.}
    PILOT jointly optimizes query and LLM embeddings aligned by affinity,
    potentially capturing non-linear structure that our fixed
    SentenceTransformer + PCA pipeline cannot.
    Integrating a learned embedding layer is a natural extension.
  \item \textbf{Preference-conditioned inference.}
    BaRP allows operators to specify a performance--cost preference vector at
    inference time without retraining, providing per-request flexibility.
    banditGPT's $\lambda$ parameter achieves a similar effect but requires
    setting the value before the evaluation episode rather than per-query.
  \item \textbf{Budget-aware cost policies.}
    PILOT's knapsack formulation optimizes cumulative cost over a horizon,
    while banditGPT enforces per-request constraints.
    For deployments with strict aggregate budgets (e.g., monthly spend caps),
    a horizon-aware cost policy could complement banditGPT's per-request
    filtering.
\end{itemize}

These complementary strengths suggest fruitful directions for future
integration: a Corralling-based meta-learner with a learned embedding space
and horizon-aware cost policy would combine the safety and prior machinery
of banditGPT with the adaptive representations and budget optimization of
PILOT and BaRP.
