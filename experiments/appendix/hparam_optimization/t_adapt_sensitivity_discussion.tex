\paragraph{$T_{\text{adapt}}$ sensitivity.}
A natural concern is whether the selected configuration is sensitive
to the anchor value $T_{\text{adapt}}{=}\hpTAdapt$, which was set to
approximately match the catastrophic-failure phase length.
To test robustness, we repeat the full Pareto knee-point selection
for $T_{\text{adapt}} \in \{250, 500, 1000\}$---a $4{\times}$ range
that spans reaction times from rapid detection to conservative
adaptation (Table~\ref{tab:t_adapt_sensitivity}).

Three observations support robustness:
%
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{$\alpha$ is perfectly stable.}
    The exploration coefficient is $\hpSensMidAlpha$ at every
    $T_{\text{adapt}}$.  This is expected: $\alpha$ governs the
    exploration--exploitation balance, which is orthogonal to the
    adaptation horizon.  The coupling formula only links
    $n_{\text{eff}}$ and $\gamma$; $\alpha$ is selected independently
    on the Pareto surface.

  \item \textbf{$\gamma$ varies by at most two grid steps and
    stabilises.}
    The selected forgetting factors---$\hpSensLoGamma$ ($T{=}250$),
    $\hpSensMidGamma$ ($T{=}500$), $\hpSensHiGamma$
    ($T{=}1000$)---form a monotonically non-increasing sequence.
    The largest shift occurs between $T{=}250$ and $T{=}500$ (two grid
    steps), after which $\gamma$ is unchanged.
    This follows from the coupling formula:
    a larger $T_{\text{adapt}}$ derives a larger $n_{\text{eff}}$ for
    the same $\gamma$, creating a heavier prior that needs more
    aggressive forgetting (lower $\gamma$) to maintain the same
    balance between stationary AUC and failure resilience on the
    Pareto frontier.
    The total range of $\gamma$ across a $4{\times}$ range of
    $T_{\text{adapt}}$ is only two grid steps, and the selection
    converges for the larger horizons.

  \item \textbf{Budget-paced Pareto AUC is effectively flat.}
    The AUC varies by less than $0.15\%$ across the three horizons
    ($\hpSensMidAUC$ to $\hpSensHiAUC$), well within the standard
    error of the mean across seeds.
    This means the stationary routing quality of the selected
    configuration is insensitive to $T_{\text{adapt}}$ within a
    $4{\times}$ range.
    Phase-2 reward is similarly stable, spanning
    $\hpSensLoPTwo$--$\hpSensMidPTwo$ with no monotonic trend,
    indicating that failure-recovery quality is not meaningfully
    affected by the choice of $T_{\text{adapt}}$.
\end{enumerate}
%
In summary, the Pareto knee-point selection identifies essentially
the same operating region of the $(\alpha, \gamma)$ space regardless
of the exact $T_{\text{adapt}}$ anchor.
The variation in $\gamma$ is confined to two grid steps and converges
for the larger horizons, while the routing quality (AUC) and
failure resilience (Phase-2 reward) are effectively invariant.
A practitioner can set $T_{\text{adapt}}$ based on their operational
requirement---shortest acceptable reaction time to a model
degradation---without risk of materially altering the selected
configuration.
