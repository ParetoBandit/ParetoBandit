\section{Hyperparameter Optimization for Non-Stationary Routing}
\label{sec:hparam_sweep}

\paragraph{The challenge.}
ParetoBandit's core hyperparameters---exploration coefficient
$\alpha$, prior strength $n_{\text{eff}}$, and forgetting factor
$\gamma$---interact non-trivially with stationarity.
Standard cross-validation optimises a single stationary metric,
which selects $\gamma \geq 0.999$ because forgetting always hurts
stationary performance.  However, $\gamma{=}1.0$ fails under
non-stationarity (Section~\ref{sec:eval_nonstationary}: $+34\%$
regret after a mid-stream price change), while optimising solely for adaptability
yields excessive forgetting that destabilises the stationary policy.
\emph{A good hyperparameter configuration must balance stationary
efficiency against non-stationary recovery.}

Among bandit-based LLM routers we surveyed, none addresses this tension directly:
PILOT~\cite{panda2025pilot} grid-searches $\alpha$ on a single
stationary metric; BaRP~\cite{wang2025barp} and LLM
Bandit~\cite{li2025llmbandit} omit forgetting entirely;
MixLLM~\cite{wang2025mixllm} delegates adaptation to neural-network
retraining.  In the general bandit literature,
CDT~\cite{kang2024cdt} tunes $\alpha$ online but remains
single-objective and does not tune $\gamma$, while
Garivier and Moulines~\cite{garivier2011switching} derive $\gamma$
from the number of breakpoints---unknown in production.
To our knowledge, no prior work proposes a multi-objective protocol
for jointly selecting $(\alpha, n_{\text{eff}}, \gamma)$ to balance
stationary efficiency against non-stationary recovery.

\paragraph{Adaptation-horizon coupling.}
Rather than treating $\alpha$, $n_{\text{eff}}$, and $\gamma$ as three
independent hyperparameters, we observe that two of
them---$n_{\text{eff}}$ and $\gamma$---are linked through a single
interpretable quantity: the \emph{adaptation horizon} $T_{\text{adapt}}$.
Under the Discounted LinUCB update~\cite{russac2019weighted}, the
effective weight of online evidence relative to the prior reaches
parity after
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t_adapt}
  T_{\text{adapt}} = \frac{-\log\bigl(n_{\text{eff}}\,(1{-}\gamma) + 1\bigr)}{\log \gamma}
\end{equation}
%
queries.  Inverting for $n_{\text{eff}}$ gives
$n_{\text{eff}} = (\gamma^{-T_{\text{adapt}}} - 1) / (1 - \gamma)$,
which for $\gamma{=}1.0$ reduces to $n_{\text{eff}} = T_{\text{adapt}}$
by L'H\^opital's rule.
Fixing $T_{\text{adapt}}$ collapses the 3D grid
$(\alpha, n_{\text{eff}}, \gamma)$ to a 2D search over
$(\alpha, \gamma)$ with $n_{\text{eff}}$ derived, ensuring that every
configuration in the sweep adapts to non-stationarity at
the same rate---only the exploration--exploitation balance
and the granularity of forgetting differ.

We set $T_{\text{adapt}} = \hpTAdapt$ by anchoring it to the
catastrophic-failure phase length ($N_{\text{phase2}} \approx 595$
prompts in the validation split), so the router can fully override
its prior within the experimental measurement window.
In practice, a deployment engineer would set $T_{\text{adapt}}$ to the
shortest acceptable reaction time for their service (e.g., the
time to detect and reallocate away from a degraded model).

\paragraph{Multi-objective formulation.}
With $n_{\text{eff}}$ derived, we score each $(\alpha, \gamma)$
configuration on two objectives:
%
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Budget-Paced Pareto AUC} (stationary efficiency):
    sweep log-spaced budget targets on the validation split with the
    BudgetPacer active, build per-seed Pareto frontiers, average AUC
    across $\hpGridAlpha \times \hpGridGamma = 42$ configs $\times$ 20~seeds.

  \item \textbf{Catastrophic-failure Phase-2 reward} (non-stationary
    resilience):
    two-phase simulation on the validation split---Phase~1 (first
    half) with normal rewards, Phase~2 (second half) with the failed
    arm's reward degraded to $\cfFailureReward$ (${\sim}\ppCfDegPct$\% below normal) while cost remains unchanged.
    Mean Phase-2 reward captures how quickly the router detects the
    failure and reallocates traffic.
    We tune on Mistral failure, the most operationally stressful
    scenario (Mistral is the dominant arm across the broadest range
    of budget targets), and validate generalization to all $K$ arms
    post-hoc.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Pareto knee-point selection.}

We select a single configuration from the scored grid by
constructing the \emph{Pareto frontier} of non-dominated
$(\text{AUC},\;\text{Phase-2 reward})$ pairs and identifying
the \textbf{knee point}---the configuration whose perpendicular
distance to the line connecting the frontier's two extreme
endpoints is maximal, after min--max normalisation for scale
invariance.
Geometrically, the knee marks the inflection beyond which
improving either objective demands a disproportionate sacrifice
of the other; unlike scalarisation weights or
$\epsilon$-constraints, it requires no user-specified trade-off
parameter.
Seed-level bootstrap resampling (\hpBootNIter{} iterations)
confirms the selection is stable: the knee configuration is
chosen in \hpBootKneeFreq\% of resamples, and a configuration
within one $\gamma$-grid step in \hpBootNeighborFreq\%
(only \hpBootNUnique{} unique configurations appear across all
resamples).

AUC-only optimisation selects $\gamma{=}1.0$ (no forgetting) for
both variants---confirming that single-objective tuning converges
on the stationary optimum, which
Section~\ref{sec:eval_nonstationary} shows is catastrophic under drift.
The Pareto knee-point method introduces moderate forgetting for
\emph{both} variants: $\gamma{=}\hpParetoBanditGamma$ (ParetoBandit)
and $\gamma{=}\hpTabulaGamma$ (Tabula Rasa), trading a small amount
of stationary AUC (${\sim}\hpAUCSacrifice\%$ for ParetoBandit) for
substantially improved failure resilience.

On the held-out test split, the selected configurations achieve
Pareto AUC of $\hpParetoBanditTestAUC$ (ParetoBandit) and
$\hpTabulaTestAUC$ (Tabula Rasa) versus $\hpFixedTestAUC$ for the
fixed-model envelope---deltas of $\hpParetoBanditTestDelta\%$ and
$\hpTabulaTestDelta\%$, both within one standard deviation of
seed variance ($\pm\hpParetoBanditTestStd$ and
$\pm\hpTabulaTestStd$).

tuning arm (Mistral), we evaluate it against catastrophic failure

Three findings merit attention:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{The adaptation-horizon coupling eliminates a
    hyperparameter.}
    The 3D search collapses to 2D $(\alpha, \gamma)$ with
    $n_{\text{eff}}$ derived, so the practitioner tunes a single
    deployment-meaningful parameter ($T_{\text{adapt}}$) rather than
    two abstract statistical quantities.

  \item \textbf{The knee-point method selects forgetting for both
    variants.}
    A hard-threshold approach ($\epsilon$-best AUC) would lock
    Tabula Rasa to $\gamma{=}1.0$; the Pareto knee-point instead
    identifies the inflection where the marginal AUC cost of
    additional forgetting accelerates, selecting
    $\gamma{=}\hpTabulaGamma$ for Tabula Rasa despite its steeper
    AUC price.

  \item \textbf{Warmup priors remain the structural enabler.}
    ParetoBandit is strictly superior on both objectives
    ($\hpParetoBanditAUC$ vs.\ $\hpTabulaAUC$ AUC;
    $\hpParetoBanditPTwoReward$ vs.\ $\hpTabulaPTwoReward$ P2 reward).
    Under exponential discounting, the estimate shrinks toward its
    prior: for Tabula Rasa, toward ignorance
    $(\lambda I, \mathbf{0})$; for ParetoBandit, toward
    $\hpParetoBanditNeff$ informative pseudo-observations per arm.
    The cross-arm validation
    (Table~\ref{tab:cross_arm_validation}) confirms that the
    forgetting mechanism generalises across all arms.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Practical implications.}
The protocol requires the practitioner to specify $T_{\text{adapt}}$
(shortest acceptable reaction time) and a grid of
$(\alpha, \gamma)$ candidates; it outputs a single configuration
that is near-optimal for budget-paced routing and maximally
adaptive, with no weight or tolerance parameter.
Because the secondary objective uses a synthetic failure simulation,
the protocol runs entirely on a static validation split---safe,
offline, and repeatable.
The empirical result that single-objective tuning selects
$\gamma{=}1.0$ for \emph{all} variants is itself a cautionary
finding: standard cross-validation converges on no forgetting,
offering zero protection against distribution shift.
