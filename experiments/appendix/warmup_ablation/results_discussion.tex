\subsection{Cold-Start vs Warmup Priors (Figure~\ref{fig:warmup_ablation})}
\label{sec:warmup_ablation}

We isolate the contribution of warmup priors with two complementary
comparisons on the $K{=}3$ stationary portfolio under four budget
regimes: unconstrained (no pacer), tight
($B{=}\$3.0{\times}10^{-4}$/req), moderate
($B{=}\$6.6{\times}10^{-4}$/req), and loose
($B{=}\$1.87{\times}10^{-3}$/req).

\paragraph{Q1: Deployment comparison (``best vs best'').}
ParetoBandit (warmup) uses its own Pareto-knee hyperparameters
($\alpha{=}0.01$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1{,}163.9$,
$\gamma{=}0.997$); Tabula Rasa uses its own
($\alpha{=}0.01$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1$, $\gamma{=}0.995$).
This answers the deployment-relevant question: should a practitioner
enable warmup priors?

\paragraph{Q2: Mechanistic control (matched-$\gamma$).}
Because the Pareto-knee sweep selects a different forgetting factor
for each condition ($\gamma{=}0.997$ for warmup vs.\ $0.995$ for
Tabula Rasa), the ``best vs best'' comparison confounds the prior
contribution with the effect of longer memory (${\sim}333$ vs.\
${\sim}200$ effective-memory steps).
A matched-$\gamma$ control runs Tabula Rasa at warmup's
$\gamma{=}0.997$ (cold start, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1$), isolating
the prior contribution at matched memory length.

\paragraph{Statistical methodology.}
Two complementary tests formalise each comparison:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item The \textbf{sign test} (exact binomial) asks whether warmup
    has lower regret seed-by-seed (location shift).
    H$_0$: P(warmup wins)${}= 0.5$.
  \item The \textbf{Fisher exact test} on a $2{\times}2$
    catastrophic-failure table asks whether warmup has fewer
    catastrophic failures (tail risk).
    H$_0$: equal failure rates.
\end{itemize}
The catastrophic threshold for each budget regime is
$2{\times}$ the \emph{pooled} median across all conditions in
that regime (warmup, Tabula Rasa, and matched-$\gamma$), ensuring
no single condition anchors the threshold.
All $p$-values are corrected for multiplicity via the
Holm--Bonferroni procedure across the full family of tests
(8 sign tests and 8 Fisher tests, corrected separately per family).

\paragraph{Deployment comparison (Q1).}
Warmup priors consistently reduce mean regret across all budget
regimes: by 52\% unconstrained ($55.0$ vs.\ $113.5$;
$p_\text{sign}^* < 10^{-5}$), 36\% under tight budget ($161.2$
vs.\ $252.0$; $p_\text{sign}^* {=} 0.001$), 25\% at moderate
budget ($133.9$ vs.\ $178.8$; $p_\text{sign}^* {=} 0.59$, n.s.),
and 47\% at loose budget ($83.6$ vs.\ $159.0$;
$p_\text{sign}^* {=} 0.083$, n.s.).
After Holm correction, only the unconstrained and tight
comparisons remain significant at $\alpha{=}0.05$ for the
location shift.
The non-significance at moderate and loose budgets reflects Tabula
Rasa's bimodal distribution: ``lucky'' cold-start seeds that avoid
early expensive-arm exploration achieve comparable or lower regret
than warmup, while ``unlucky'' seeds catastrophically fail.
The mean comparison averages over these two modes and is neither
a reliable summary of typical Tabula Rasa performance nor evidence
that warmup is unnecessary.

\paragraph{Mechanistic control (Q2).}
The matched-$\gamma$ control performs nearly identically to the
original Tabula Rasa across all regimes (unconstrained: $117.7$
vs.\ $113.5$; tight: $255.6$ vs.\ $252.0$; moderate: $191.4$
vs.\ $178.8$; loose: $162.9$ vs.\ $159.0$), and in fact slightly
\emph{worse} in every case.
This rules out the forgetting-factor confound: running a cold
start at warmup's $\gamma{=}0.997$ (longer memory of an
uninformative prior) is counterproductive rather than helpful.
Warmup's advantage is attributable to the \emph{priors themselves},
not to the higher $\gamma$ that the Pareto-knee sweep selects
when priors are available.
The sign tests confirm this: warmup beats the matched-$\gamma$
control with comparable significance to the original Tabula Rasa
(unconstrained: $p_\text{sign}^* < 10^{-3}$; tight:
$p_\text{sign}^* {=} 0.006$).

\paragraph{Cold-start bimodality.}
The budget-stratified results reveal a qualitative finding beyond
mean regret reduction: Tabula Rasa's per-seed regret is
\emph{bimodal} under every budget constraint tested
(Figure~\ref{fig:warmup_ablation}).
The matched-$\gamma$ control exhibits the same bimodal pattern
with similar catastrophic failure rates (e.g.\ moderate budget:
8/20 vs.\ 6/20), confirming that the bimodality arises from
\emph{missing priors}, not from the different forgetting rate.
The mechanism is a deterministic consequence of the interaction
between UCB exploration and the budget pacer: the cold-start router
has high initial uncertainty, so UCB occasionally selects the
expensive frontier arm (Gemini-Pro) during early exploration.
This triggers a dual-variable spike in the primal-dual pacer,
which then penalises all future expensive-arm selections,
permanently locking out the expensive arm before its quality is
learned.

Under tight budgets, the pooled-median catastrophic threshold
($360.6$) exceeds all individual seed regrets, so no seeds are
formally classified as catastrophic despite clear bimodality in
the Tabula Rasa distribution (median $297.7$,
range~${\sim}170$--$310$).
This is a consequence of the conservative pooled threshold:
warmup's low-regret seeds pull the pooled median down, and
$2{\times}$ that value exceeds the Tabula Rasa ceiling.
The bimodality is nevertheless visible in
Figure~\ref{fig:warmup_ablation} and in the $14{\times}$ standard
deviation gap ($4.6$ vs.\ $62.6$).

\paragraph{Discussion.}
Warmup priors are best understood as a \emph{deployment
reliability mechanism}: they eliminate both the cold-start
transient and the catastrophic pacer-interaction failure mode.
The matched-$\gamma$ control sharpens this conclusion by ruling
out the alternative hypothesis that the forgetting-factor difference
drives the advantage.
The warmup benefit is transient by design---geometric forgetting
($\gamma{=}0.997$) replaces priors within ${\sim}333$
effective-memory steps; after sufficient learning, warmup and
tabula-rasa conditions converge
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:val_burnin_ablation}).
Under budget constraints, however, catastrophic seeds never
recover because the budget pacer's dual variable permanently
locks out the expensive arm.

Importantly, these gains assume the prior is directionally correct.
A dedicated prior-mismatch sensitivity analysis
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:prior_mismatch}) quantifies trade-offs
across five quality levels---from well-calibrated through
domain-restricted to actively inverted priors.

\paragraph{Business perspective.}
Intelligent LLM routing delivers simultaneous quality and cost
gains because no single model dominates all queries.
In our $K{=}3$ portfolio, oracle routing achieves 3.3\% higher
quality than routing all traffic to the frontier model while
reducing per-request cost by 66\%.
Warmup priors protect routing quality during the critical but
transient cold-start window: under tight budgets, the 36\% regret
reduction ($161.2$ vs.\ $252.0$; $p_\text{sign}^* {=} 0.001$)
represents a substantial fraction of the router's total
quality--cost value recovered solely by starting with informed
priors.
These projections are illustrative: the per-request costs are
specific to our three-model portfolio and March 2026 pricing,
and the traffic volumes are drawn from public platform statistics
rather than a single organisation's logs.
Nevertheless, warmup priors are easy to justify when calibrated
on representative data: computing them from a labelled offline
dataset takes minutes and requires no ongoing
maintenance~\cite{kveton2023warmstart}.
