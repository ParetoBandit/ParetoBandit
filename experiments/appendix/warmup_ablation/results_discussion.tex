\section{Cold-Start vs Warmup Priors}
\label{sec:warmup_ablation}

Section~\ref{sec:warmup} introduces offline-to-online warmup priors,
which let the router begin with informed beliefs about each model's
strengths by loading sufficient statistics fitted on historical data.
Two natural questions arise when deciding whether to enable this
feature.

\paragraph{Q1: Do warmup priors reduce cold-start regret in practice?}
A practitioner choosing between enabling warmup priors or deploying
with a blank slate needs an apples-to-apples comparison where
\emph{each} condition uses its own best hyperparameters---the
configuration the practitioner would actually run.
We therefore compare ParetoBandit with warmup priors
($\alpha{=}\waAlpha$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}\waNeff$,
$\gamma{=}\waGamma$) against Tabula Rasa
($\alpha{=}\waTRAlpha$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1$,
$\gamma{=}\waTRGamma$), each tuned independently via the
Pareto-knee procedure (Appendix~\ref{sec:hparam_sweep}).

\paragraph{Q2: Is the improvement actually from the priors, or from
a confounded hyperparameter?}
The ``best vs best'' comparison above has a subtle confound.
Because the Pareto-knee sweep selects different forgetting factors
for each condition ($\gamma{=}\waGamma$ for warmup vs.\
$\waTRGamma$ for Tabula Rasa), a skeptic could attribute the
improvement to the different memory lengths (${\sim}\waWarmupEffMem$ vs.\
${\sim}\waTREffMem$ effective-memory steps) rather than to the priors
themselves.
To isolate the prior contribution, we add a \emph{matched-$\gamma$
control}: Tabula Rasa run at warmup's $\gamma{=}\waGamma$
(cold start, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1$), so the only remaining
difference is prior strength.
The three conditions and their key parameters are:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Warmup}: $\alpha{=}\waAlpha$,
    $n_\mathit{eff}{=}\waNeff$, $\gamma{=}\waGamma$.
  \item \textbf{Tabula Rasa} (independently tuned):
    $\alpha{=}\waTRAlpha$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1$,
    $\gamma{=}\waTRGamma$.
  \item \textbf{Tabula Rasa} (matched-$\gamma$):
    $\alpha{=}\waTRAlpha$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1$,
    $\gamma{=}\waGamma$.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent
Each condition uses the hyperparameters selected by its own
Pareto-knee sweep (Appendix~\ref{sec:hparam_sweep}).
Conditions~1 and~3 share the same $\gamma$ but differ in both
prior strength and $\alpha$; however, the $\alpha$ difference
is conservative for warmup: a higher $\alpha$ gives the cold-start
condition \emph{more} exploration, which should accelerate its
convergence rather than hinder it.
Conditions~2 and~3 share the same $\alpha$ and differ only
in $\gamma$, so comparing them cleanly reveals whether the
forgetting factor matters independently of the prior.

\medskip\noindent
Both questions are evaluated on the $K{=}\waK$ stationary portfolio
under four budget regimes: unconstrained (no pacer), tight
($B{=}\$\waTightBudget$/req), moderate
($B{=}\$\waModBudget$/req), and loose
($B{=}\$\waLooseBudget$/req).

\paragraph{Statistical methodology.}
We assess each comparison along two dimensions.
The first is \emph{typical-case} regret: does warmup
tend to outperform seed-by-seed?
The second is \emph{tail risk}: does warmup avoid
outlier runs where regret is disproportionately high?
We call a seed a \emph{catastrophic failure} when its
cumulative regret exceeds $\waCatMultiplier{\times}$ the pooled median across
all conditions in that budget regime (warmup, Tabula Rasa,
and matched-$\gamma$); pooling ensures no single condition
anchors the threshold.
These two dimensions require different tests because a method
can improve the median while still leaving heavy tails, or
eliminate outliers without shifting the centre.
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Sign test} (exact binomial): tests whether warmup
    has lower regret seed-by-seed, capturing the typical-case
    comparison.
    H$_0$: P(warmup wins)${}= 0.5$.
  \item \textbf{Fisher exact test} on a $2{\times}2$ table of
    catastrophic vs.\ non-catastrophic seeds: tests whether warmup
    has a lower catastrophic-failure rate, capturing the tail-risk
    comparison.
    H$_0$: equal failure rates.
\end{itemize}
All $p$-values are corrected for multiplicity via the
Holm--Bonferroni procedure across the full family of tests
(\waNTests{} sign tests and \waNTests{} Fisher tests, corrected separately per family).

Below we highlight the key contrasts that answer Q1 and Q2.

\paragraph{Q1 result: warmup priors reduce cold-start regret.}
Relative to Tabula Rasa, warmup priors reduce mean cumulative regret
across all budget regimes.
We report the paired difference $\Delta{=}\text{Tabula Rasa} -
\text{Warmup}$ (positive favours warmup) with \waCILevel\% bootstrap CIs:
unconstrained $\Delta{=}\waUncVsTRDiffMean$\,[\waUncVsTRDiffCILo,
\waUncVsTRDiffCIHi],
tight $\Delta{=}\waTightVsTRDiffMean$\,[\waTightVsTRDiffCILo,
\waTightVsTRDiffCIHi],
moderate $\Delta{=}\waModVsTRDiffMean$\,[\waModVsTRDiffCILo,
\waModVsTRDiffCIHi],
and loose $\Delta{=}\waLooseVsTRDiffMean$\,[\waLooseVsTRDiffCILo,
\waLooseVsTRDiffCIHi].
The CI excludes zero for unconstrained, tight, and loose regimes;
the moderate regime is inconclusive
($\Delta{=}\waModVsTRDiffMean$, CI spans zero).
The reductions correspond to
\waTightRegretReductionPct--\waUncRegretReductionPct\% of Tabula
Rasa's total regret, reflecting that the cold-start baseline
converges within the ${\sim}\waTREffMem$ effective-memory window afforded by
its $\gamma{=}\waTRGamma$.
Much of this advantage is concentrated in the early learning phase.
The R@\waEarlyStep{} paired differences
($\Delta{=}\text{TR} - \text{Warmup}$, same sign convention)
are significant in every regime:
unconstrained $\Delta{=}\waUncVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffMean$\,[\waUncVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffCILo,
\waUncVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffCIHi],
tight $\Delta{=}\waTightVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffMean$\,[\waTightVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffCILo,
\waTightVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffCIHi],
moderate $\Delta{=}\waModVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffMean$\,[\waModVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffCILo,
\waModVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffCIHi],
and loose $\Delta{=}\waLooseVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffMean$\,[\waLooseVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffCILo,
\waLooseVsTRRAtTwoHundredDiffCIHi]
(see also per-condition R@\waEarlyStep{} CIs in
Table~\ref{tab:warmup_ablation_results}).
The advantage narrows as online evidence accumulates
past the effective-memory window.

\paragraph{Q2 result: the improvement comes from the priors, not the
forgetting factor.}
We compare the two Tabula Rasa conditions directly.
If adopting warmup's higher $\gamma$ helped a cold start, the
matched-$\gamma$ Tabula Rasa should outperform the independently
tuned Tabula Rasa.
The opposite is true: the matched-$\gamma$ variant has
\emph{higher} regret in every regime
($\Delta{=}\text{matched-}\gamma - \text{Tabula Rasa}$,
positive means matched-$\gamma$ is worse):
unconstrained $\Delta{=}\waUncTRMatchedVsTRDiffMean$\,[\waUncTRMatchedVsTRDiffCILo,
\waUncTRMatchedVsTRDiffCIHi],
tight $\Delta{=}\waTightTRMatchedVsTRDiffMean$\,[\waTightTRMatchedVsTRDiffCILo,
\waTightTRMatchedVsTRDiffCIHi],
moderate $\Delta{=}\waModTRMatchedVsTRDiffMean$\,[\waModTRMatchedVsTRDiffCILo,
\waModTRMatchedVsTRDiffCIHi],
and loose $\Delta{=}\waLooseTRMatchedVsTRDiffMean$\,[\waLooseTRMatchedVsTRDiffCILo,
\waLooseTRMatchedVsTRDiffCIHi].
Running a cold start at warmup's $\gamma{=}\waGamma$ (longer
memory of an uninformative prior) is counterproductive rather than
helpful.
Since changing $\gamma$ alone does not improve a cold start,
warmup's advantage over Tabula Rasa (Q1) is attributable to
the \emph{informative priors themselves}.

\paragraph{Cold-start variability.}
Warmup priors produce tighter per-seed regret distributions
(Figure~\ref{fig:warmup_ablation}).
Under tight budgets, the per-seed regret standard deviation
for warmup is $\waTightWarmupRegretStd$ compared to
$\waTightTRRegretStd$ for Tabula Rasa
(${\sim}\waTightStdRatio{\times}$ lower).
Warmup records zero catastrophic failures
(regret $> \waCatMultiplier{\times}$ pooled median) across all
regimes.
Tabula Rasa also records zero catastrophic failures
(\waUncVsTRBaselineCat/\waNseeds{} unconstrained,
\waTightVsTRBaselineCat/\waNseeds{} tight,
\waModVsTRBaselineCat/\waNseeds{} moderate,
\waLooseVsTRBaselineCat/\waNseeds{} loose), yielding
non-significant Fisher tests ($p_\text{Fisher}^* {=}
\waUncVsTRFisherP$ across all regimes).
The matched-$\gamma$ control is the only condition to produce
catastrophic failures: \waUncVsTRMatchedBaselineCat/\waNseeds{}
in the unconstrained regime
(see Table~\ref{tab:warmup_ablation_results} for all
Holm-corrected $p$-values).
This confirms that adopting warmup's longer memory
($\gamma{=}\waGamma$) without informative priors can
increase tail risk rather than reduce it.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
Warmup priors are best understood as a \emph{deployment
reliability mechanism}: they reduce cold-start regret and
tighten the per-seed distribution.
The matched-$\gamma$ Tabula Rasa sharpens this conclusion: the
data provide no evidence that the forgetting-factor difference
drives the advantage, since the matched-$\gamma$ condition
performs no better than---and in fact slightly worse than---the
independently tuned Tabula Rasa.
Warmup priors are optional---ParetoBandit is fully functional
without them, and the Tabula Rasa results in
Table~\ref{tab:warmup_ablation_results} confirm that the bandit
converges to comparable steady-state quality once sufficient online
evidence accumulates.
The warmup benefit is transient by design: geometric forgetting
($\gamma{=}\waGamma$) replaces priors within ${\sim}\waWarmupEffMem$
effective-memory steps, after which warmup and tabula-rasa
conditions converge.
Enabling warmup priors reduces regret during this early learning
window and tightens run-to-run variability, but practitioners who
lack historical data or prefer a simpler deployment can omit them
without sacrificing long-run performance.

When warmup priors are used, these gains assume the prior is
directionally correct.
A dedicated prior-mismatch sensitivity analysis
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:prior_mismatch}) quantifies trade-offs
across five quality levels---from well-calibrated through
domain-restricted to actively inverted priors.
