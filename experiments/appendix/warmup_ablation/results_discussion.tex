\section{Cold-Start vs Warmup Priors}
\label{sec:warmup_ablation}

We isolate the contribution of warmup priors with two complementary
comparisons on the $K{=}3$ stationary portfolio under four budget
regimes: unconstrained (no pacer), tight
($B{=}\$3.0{\times}10^{-4}$/req), moderate
($B{=}\$6.6{\times}10^{-4}$/req), and loose
($B{=}\$1.87{\times}10^{-3}$/req).

\paragraph{Q1: Deployment comparison (``best vs best'').}
ParetoBandit (warmup) uses its own Pareto-knee hyperparameters
($\alpha{=}\waAlpha$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}\waNeff$,
$\gamma{=}\waGamma$); Tabula Rasa uses its own
($\alpha{=}\waAlpha$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1$, $\gamma{=}\waTRGamma$).
This answers the deployment-relevant question: should a practitioner
enable warmup priors?

\paragraph{Q2: Mechanistic control (matched-$\gamma$).}
Because the Pareto-knee sweep selects a different forgetting factor
for each condition ($\gamma{=}\waGamma$ for warmup vs.\ $\waTRGamma$ for
Tabula Rasa), the ``best vs best'' comparison confounds the prior
contribution with the effect of different memory lengths (${\sim}250$ vs.\
${\sim}333$ effective-memory steps).
A matched-$\gamma$ control runs Tabula Rasa at warmup's
$\gamma{=}\waGamma$ (cold start, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1$), isolating
the prior contribution at matched (shorter) memory length.

\paragraph{Statistical methodology.}
Two complementary tests formalise each comparison:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item The \textbf{sign test} (exact binomial) asks whether warmup
    has lower regret seed-by-seed (location shift).
    H$_0$: P(warmup wins)${}= 0.5$.
  \item The \textbf{Fisher exact test} on a $2{\times}2$
    catastrophic-failure table asks whether warmup has fewer
    catastrophic failures (tail risk).
    H$_0$: equal failure rates.
\end{itemize}
The catastrophic threshold for each budget regime is
$2{\times}$ the \emph{pooled} median across all conditions in
that regime (warmup, Tabula Rasa, and matched-$\gamma$), ensuring
no single condition anchors the threshold.
All $p$-values are corrected for multiplicity via the
Holm--Bonferroni procedure across the full family of tests
(8 sign tests and 8 Fisher tests, corrected separately per family).

\paragraph{Deployment comparison (Q1).}
Warmup priors consistently reduce mean regret across all budget
regimes: by \waUncRegretReductionPct\% unconstrained ($\waUncWarmupRegret$ vs.\ $\waUncTRRegret$;
$p_\text{sign}^*{=}\waUncVsTRSignP$), \waTightRegretReductionPct\% under tight budget ($\waTightWarmupRegret$
vs.\ $\waTightTRRegret$; $p_\text{sign}^* {=} \waTightVsTRSignP$), \waModRegretReductionPct\% at moderate
budget ($\waModWarmupRegret$ vs.\ $\waModTRRegret$; $p_\text{sign}^* {=} \waModVsTRSignP$, n.s.),
and \waLooseRegretReductionPct\% at loose budget ($\waLooseWarmupRegret$ vs.\ $\waLooseTRRegret$;
$p_\text{sign}^* {=} \waLooseVsTRSignP$).
After Holm correction, the unconstrained, tight, and loose
comparisons are significant at $\alpha{=}0.05$; the moderate
comparison is borderline ($p{=}\waModVsTRSignP$).
The reductions are modest in magnitude (\waModRegretReductionPct--\waUncRegretReductionPct\%),
reflecting that the Tabula Rasa baseline converges within the
${\sim}250$ effective-memory window afforded by its
$\gamma{=}\waTRGamma$; warmup's advantage is concentrated in the
early learning phase (R@200) and narrows as online evidence
accumulates.

\paragraph{Mechanistic control (Q2).}
The matched-$\gamma$ control performs nearly identically to the
original Tabula Rasa across all regimes (unconstrained: $\waUncTRMatchedRegret$
vs.\ $\waUncTRRegret$; tight: $\waTightTRMatchedRegret$ vs.\ $\waTightTRRegret$; moderate: $\waModTRMatchedRegret$
vs.\ $\waModTRRegret$; loose: $\waLooseTRMatchedRegret$ vs.\ $\waLooseTRRegret$), and in fact slightly
\emph{worse} in every case.
This rules out the forgetting-factor confound: running a cold
start at warmup's $\gamma{=}\waGamma$ (longer memory of an
uninformative prior) is counterproductive rather than helpful.
Warmup's advantage is attributable to the \emph{priors themselves},
not to the higher $\gamma$ that the Pareto-knee sweep selects
when priors are available.
The sign tests confirm this: warmup beats the matched-$\gamma$
control with comparable significance to the original Tabula Rasa
(unconstrained: $p_\text{sign}^*{=}\waUncVsTRMatchedSignP$; tight:
$p_\text{sign}^* {=} \waTightVsTRMatchedSignP$).

\paragraph{Cold-start variability.}
Warmup priors produce tighter per-seed regret distributions
(Figure~\ref{fig:warmup_ablation}).
Under tight budgets, the standard deviation gap is
$\waTightWarmupRegretStd$ vs.\ $\waTightTRRegretStd$
(${\sim}14{\times}$).
No catastrophic failures (regret $> 2{\times}$ pooled median)
are observed for any condition in the current runs
(\waUncVsTRBaselineCat/\waNseeds unconstrained,
\waTightVsTRBaselineCat/\waNseeds tight,
\waModVsTRBaselineCat/\waNseeds moderate,
\waLooseVsTRBaselineCat/\waNseeds loose),
reflected in the non-significant Fisher tests
($p_\text{Fisher}^* {=} \waUncVsTRFisherP$ across all regimes).
The matched-$\gamma$ control exhibits similar variance
(\waModVsTRMatchedBaselineCat/\waNseeds catastrophic at moderate),
confirming that the variability reduction is attributable to
informative priors, not to the different forgetting rate.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
Warmup priors are best understood as a \emph{deployment
reliability mechanism}: they reduce cold-start regret and
tighten the per-seed distribution.
The matched-$\gamma$ control sharpens this conclusion by ruling
out the alternative hypothesis that the forgetting-factor difference
drives the advantage.
The warmup benefit is transient by design---geometric forgetting
($\gamma{=}\waGamma$) replaces priors within ${\sim}333$
effective-memory steps; after sufficient learning, warmup and
tabula-rasa conditions converge
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:val_burnin_ablation}).

These gains assume the prior is directionally correct.
A dedicated prior-mismatch sensitivity analysis
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:prior_mismatch}) quantifies trade-offs
across five quality levels---from well-calibrated through
domain-restricted to actively inverted priors.
