\subsection{Motivation}
\label{appendix:alpha_neff_motivation}

Hyperparameter selection is critical for contextual bandit routers: the
exploration coefficient~$\alpha$, the prior effective sample
size~$\neff$, and the forgetting factor~$\gamma$ jointly control how
aggressively the router explores alternative models, how strongly it
relies on warmup priors, and how quickly it discounts stale observations.
Prior sweeps (Appendices~\ref{appendix:hp_sensitivity}
and~\ref{appendix:pca_neff}) varied each parameter in isolation,
potentially missing interactions.  This appendix performs a full 3D grid
search over $(\alpha, \neff, \gamma)$.

\paragraph{Why Pareto AUC, not single-point reward.}
\label{appendix:alpha_neff_objective}
A na\"ive selection criterion is to maximise mean reward at
$\lambda{=}0$ (quality-only, no cost penalty).  However, when one model
dominates on average --- as GPT-4.1 does for $K{=}2$ --- the
reward-maximising policy converges to a \emph{degenerate static policy}
that routes ${\sim}96\%$ of traffic to the strongest model regardless of
prompt context or cost.  The resulting hyperparameters
($\alpha{\approx}0.1$, $\gamma{\approx}1.0$) suppress exploration so
severely that the router never discovers cost-effective alternatives.

This defeats the core value proposition of a contextual bandit router:
finding the \emph{best quality model at every cost level}.  A router
tuned for quality-only has no incentive to learn when a cheaper model
would suffice, and therefore cannot trace a meaningful cost--quality
Pareto frontier.

We instead select hyperparameters by \textbf{normalized AUCPC} --- the
area under the cost--reward Pareto frontier after \emph{normalizing both
axes} to the portfolio endpoints.  For each $(\alpha, \neff, \gamma)$
configuration, we sweep the cost penalty~$\lambda$ over a representative
set of values, train a separate router at each~$\lambda$, freeze it, and
evaluate on the dev-val split.  The resulting (cost, reward) points form
an empirical cloud; we filter it to the \emph{non-dominated} Pareto
frontier (removing any configuration that costs more while yielding worse
quality than another).  The AUCPC of this frontier is the selection metric:
\[
    \text{AUCPC}
    = \int_{0}^{1} \tilde{R}^{*}(\tilde{c})\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{c},
\]
where $\tilde{c} = (c - c_{\text{cheap}}) / (c_{\text{frontier}} - c_{\text{cheap}})$
maps the cheapest model cost to 0 and the frontier model cost to 1, and
$\tilde{R} = (R - R_{\text{cheap}}) / (R_{\text{frontier}} - R_{\text{cheap}})$
maps the cheapest and frontier models' \emph{static baseline rewards} on
the same evaluation split to 0 and 1, respectively.  Under this
normalization, a diagonal baseline yields $\text{AUCPC}\approx 0.5$,
while a near-oracle router yields $\text{AUCPC}\approx 1.0$.

This criterion directly rewards hyperparameters that enable diverse,
intelligent routing across the cost spectrum:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item At low~$\lambda$, the router should achieve near-oracle
          quality by routing to the strongest model.
    \item At moderate~$\lambda$, it should save cost by routing
          easy prompts to cheaper models without sacrificing quality.
    \item At high~$\lambda$, it should converge to the cheapest
          model while maintaining reasonable quality.
\end{itemize}
Hyperparameters that produce a degenerate static policy --- regardless
of~$\lambda$ --- will have a lower Pareto AUC than those that adapt
routing to the cost signal.

\subsection{Design}
\label{appendix:alpha_neff_design}

We evaluate a $5 \times 4 \times 4$ grid:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$
    \item $\neff \in \{10, 100, 1{,}000, 5{,}000\}$
    \item $\gamma \in \{0.995, 0.999, 0.9999, 1.0\}$
\end{itemize}
At each grid point, we sweep $\lambda$ over 7~values:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item $K{=}2$: $\lambda \in \{0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0\}$
    \item $K{=}3$: $\lambda \in \{0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$
\end{itemize}
yielding $80 \times 7 = 560$~configurations per variant, each evaluated
with 20~seeds using a train-then-freeze protocol.  The best
configuration is selected by dev-val Pareto AUC, then its full
$\lambda$~sweep is repeated on a held-out test set.

Both BanditGPT (Corralling + warmup priors) and Tabula Rasa (single
LinUCB, no priors) are tuned independently for $K{=}2$ and $K{=}3$,
producing four best-hyperparameter files consumed by downstream
experiments.

\subsection{Findings}
\label{appendix:alpha_neff_findings}

Results will be populated after running the grid with the Pareto AUC
selection criterion.  Key hypotheses:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item Pareto AUC selection should favour higher $\alpha$
          (0.5--2.0) than quality-only selection ($\alpha{=}0.1$),
          because exploring cheaper models pays off across the
          $\lambda$~sweep.
    \item Moderate $\neff$ (100--1000) should emerge as optimal for
          BanditGPT, providing prior guidance without overwhelming
          online evidence at different cost levels.
    \item Routing fractions at the median-cost $\lambda$ should show
          meaningful diversity (e.g.\ 60--75\% to the strong model for
          $K{=}2$) rather than the degenerate 96\% observed under
          quality-only tuning.
    \item The Pareto-AUC-selected hyperparameters should yield stronger
          cost--quality frontiers in downstream experiments (Figure~3)
          than the old quality-only hyperparameters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Practical Recommendations}
\label{appendix:alpha_neff_recommendations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Selection criterion:} Use Pareto AUC rather than
          single-point reward for hyperparameter selection.  This
          produces routers that are simultaneously quality-competitive
          and cost-aware.
    \item \textbf{With warmup priors:} Start with moderate exploration
          ($\alpha \in [0.5, 1.0]$) and let the prior guide early
          routing; the Pareto criterion ensures sufficient exploration
          of cost-effective alternatives.
    \item \textbf{Without priors (tabula rasa):} Higher exploration
          ($\alpha \geq 1.0$) compensates for the lack of prior
          information and ensures sufficient arm coverage across all
          cost levels.
    \item \textbf{Non-stationary deployments:} Use a heterogeneous
          forgetting strategy --- the warmup expert retains its tuned
          $\gamma$ (long memory, stability), while the tabula rasa expert
          uses $\gamma{\approx}0.999$ (short memory, adaptability).
          Corralling automatically shifts weight to the expert best suited
          to the current regime (see \S\ref{sec:catastrophic_failure}).
\end{itemize}
