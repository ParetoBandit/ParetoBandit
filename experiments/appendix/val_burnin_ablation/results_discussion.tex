\section{Validation Burn-In Ablation}
\label{appendix:val_burnin_ablation}

The validation split ($n{=}1{,}785$) serves two roles in the
experimental pipeline: (i)~hyperparameter selection---$\alpha$,
$\gamma$, and $n_\mathit{eff}$ are tuned on val via the
T\textsubscript{adapt}-constrained Pareto knee-point method
(Appendix~\ref{sec:hparam_sweep}), and (ii)~online learning
---the bandit processes val prompts before the held-out test split
($n{=}1{,}824$), providing reward-model burn-in and BudgetPacer
calibration.  The first role is essential for preventing test-set
contamination; the second is a consequence of the evaluation protocol,
not its motivation.  A reviewer may nonetheless ask whether this
incidental burn-in artificially inflates test-set performance.
To answer, we ablate the amount of validation data used for online
learning before test evaluation \emph{across three budget regimes}:
unconstrained, tight ($B{=}\$3.0{\times}10^{-4}$/req), and moderate
($B{=}\$6.6{\times}10^{-4}$/req).

\paragraph{Setup.}
Five warmup conditions share identical priors (from train) and the
same hyperparameters ($\alpha{=}\vbAlpha$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}\vbNeff$,
$\gamma{=}\vbGamma$); only the fraction of val used for burn-in varies
(0\%, 25\%, 50\%, 75\%, 100\%).  Two tabula-rasa baselines (0\% and
100\% burn-in) use their own independently tuned hyperparameters
($\alpha{=}\vbAlpha$, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1$, $\gamma{=}\vbTRGamma$), selected
via the same T\textsubscript{adapt}-constrained Pareto knee-point
protocol.
\textbf{Cross-system caveat:} because warmup and tabula-rasa systems
use independently tuned $\gamma$ (\vbGamma{} vs.\ \vbTRGamma, yielding effective
memory of ${\sim}333$ vs.\ ${\sim}200$ steps), comparisons between
the two reflect the difference between two jointly optimised
configurations---not solely the presence of priors.
Within-warmup comparisons (varying only burn-in fraction) are cleanly
single-factor and constitute the primary evidence.
All conditions are evaluated on the same held-out test
split across 20 random seeds with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests
against the 100\% burn-in warmup reference; $p$-values are
Bonferroni-corrected within each budget regime.%
\footnote{The oracle in this ablation is the per-step quality-only
oracle $\max_a r_a$ (i.e., it ignores cost).  Absolute regret values
are therefore \emph{not} comparable to the cost-adjusted oracle used in
the main paper's tables; only relative differences between conditions
should be compared.}

\paragraph{Unconstrained regime (Table~\ref{tab:val_burnin}).}
Less burn-in yields \emph{lower} test regret for warmup conditions:
0\% burn-in achieves $\vbWarmupZeroRegret$ cumulative regret, significantly below the
100\% reference ($\vbWarmupHundredRegret$; $\Delta{=}\vbStatWarmupZeroDeltaPct\%$,
$p_\mathrm{adj}$\vbStatWarmupZeroPAdj).  The 25\% and 50\% conditions
are also significantly lower ($\vbWarmupTwentyFiveRegret$, $p_\mathrm{adj}$\vbStatWarmupTwentyFivePAdj;
$\vbWarmupFiftyRegret$, $p_\mathrm{adj}{=}\vbStatWarmupFiftyPAdj$), with only 75\% approaching the
reference ($\vbWarmupSeventyFiveRegret$, $p_\mathrm{adj}{=}\vbStatWarmupSeventyFivePAdj$).  This monotonic trend
reflects the forgetting--burn-in interaction: with $\gamma{=}\vbGamma$,
effective memory is ${\sim}333$ steps.  At 0\% burn-in the
priors---trained on $8{,}374$ examples with
$n_\mathit{eff}{=}1{,}163.9$---are fully intact, providing a stronger
starting point than the ${\sim}333$ effective online observations
available after full burn-in.
After 1{,}785 val steps, priors are decayed by $\gamma^{1785} \approx
4.7{\times}10^{-3}$---heavily attenuated but not fully erased.

\paragraph{Prior value at cold start.}
Tabula rasa without burn-in incurs $\vbTRZeroRegret$ cumulative regret, $+\vbStatTRZeroDeltaPct\%$
above the warmup reference ($p_\mathrm{adj}{=}\vbStatTRZeroPAdj$), with the
penalty concentrated in the first ${\sim}200$ steps (R@200: $\vbTRZeroRAtTwoHundred$
vs.\ $\vbWarmupHundredRAtTwoHundred$).  After 100\% burn-in, tabula rasa narrows the gap
($\vbTRHundredRegret$, $+\vbTRHundredGroupDeltaPct\%$) but this difference does not survive Bonferroni
correction ($p_\mathrm{adj}$~\vbStatTRHundredPAdj), consistent with partial
convergence: both systems operate predominantly on recent online
evidence, though the different effective memory lengths
(${\sim}333$ vs.\ ${\sim}200$ steps) and residual prior contribution
at $\gamma{=}\vbGamma$ sustain a nominal gap.

\paragraph{Budget-constrained regimes (Table~\ref{tab:val_burnin_budget}).}
To verify that these findings generalise under budget pressure, we
repeat the $2{\times}2$ factorial (priors~$\times$~burn-in at
0\%/100\%) with the Primal-Dual BudgetPacer at tight and moderate
targets.  The BudgetPacer is \emph{pre-calibrated} on the full
validation set: all prompts are routed under the initial policy and
costs are fed to the pacer, but the bandit is \emph{not} updated.  All
burn-in fractions therefore start with an identically calibrated
$\lambda_t$.  During the burn-in phase itself, however, the pacer
continues to co-adapt alongside the bandit; differences between 0\%
and 100\% burn-in at the start of test therefore reflect both
reward-model learning \emph{and} pacer adaptation.

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{Tight budget: burn-in is a wash.}
    Warmup~0\% vs.\ Warmup~100\% is non-significant
    ($\vbWarmupZeroTightRegret$ vs.\ $\vbWarmupHundredTightRegret$, $\Delta{=}\vbStatWarmupZeroTightDeltaPct\%$, $p{=}\vbStatWarmupZeroTightPAdj$).
    Under tight constraints the pacer dominates routing decisions,
    making the reward model's initialisation less consequential.
  \item \emph{Moderate budget: burn-in helps.}
    Warmup~0\% is significantly worse than Warmup~100\%
    ($\vbWarmupZeroModRegret$ vs.\ $\vbWarmupHundredModRegret$, $\Delta{=}{+}\vbStatWarmupZeroModDeltaPct\%$,
    $p_\mathrm{adj}{=}\vbStatWarmupZeroModPAdj$).
    Under moderate constraints the bandit and pacer must co-adapt: the
    bandit learns which arms offer the best quality-per-dollar, while
    the pacer adjusts $\lambda_t$ in response.  Burn-in provides the
    online experience needed for this co-adaptation; without it, the
    first ${\sim}333$ test steps involve simultaneous calibration of
    both systems.
  \item \emph{Tabula rasa fails under budget constraints.}
    Without priors, the cold-start bandit cannot route effectively and
    the BudgetPacer cannot calibrate: cost/target ratios fall to
    $\vbTRZeroTightCostRatio$ (tight) and $\vbTRZeroModCostRatio$ (moderate) for tabula rasa without
    burn-in, indicating massive underspending.  Even after 100\%
    burn-in, tabula rasa remains far from budget compliance ($\vbTRHundredTightCostRatio$
    tight, $\vbTRHundredModCostRatio$ moderate) with regret $+\vbTRHundredTightGroupDeltaPct\%$--$+\vbTRZeroModGroupDeltaPct\%$ above warmup.
    This failure mode underscores that informative priors
    are critical for budget-paced routing, not merely a convenience.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implication.}
The burn-in ablation reveals a \emph{regime-dependent} interaction:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Unconstrained}: intact priors outperform online
    estimates, so burn-in \emph{increases} test regret.
  \item \textbf{Tight budget}: the pacer dominates, making burn-in
    irrelevant.
  \item \textbf{Moderate budget}: bandit--pacer co-adaptation requires
    burn-in, so skipping it \emph{increases} test regret.
\end{enumerate}
In no regime does burn-in artificially inflate test performance.
The standard evaluation protocol (100\% val burn-in) is conservative
for the unconstrained case and beneficial for the budget-paced cases
that are the paper's main contribution.  The validation split thus
earns its place in the pipeline through hyperparameter selection and
pacer calibration; the reward-model burn-in it provides is incidental
and, in the unconstrained regime, mildly harmful.

Together with the cold-start analysis
(Appendix~\ref{sec:warmup_ablation}) and prior-mismatch sensitivity
study (Appendix~\ref{appendix:prior_mismatch}), these results
establish three distinct roles for warmup priors.
First, priors provide \emph{superior initialisation}: in the
unconstrained regime, the offline estimate (trained on $8{,}374$
examples, $n_\mathit{eff}{=}1{,}163.9$) outperforms the ${\sim}333$
effective online observations that replace it under geometric
forgetting---every step of burn-in degrades the router's starting
point.
Second, priors are a \emph{structural prerequisite for budget
compliance}: without them, the BudgetPacer achieves only
$\vbTRZeroTightCostRatio$--$\vbTRZeroModCostRatio$ of target spend because the cold-start bandit cannot provide the arm
diversity the pacer needs to regulate costs.  This quantifies the
mechanism behind the catastrophic pacer--exploration failures
documented in Appendix~\ref{sec:warmup_ablation}, where 25--45\% of
tabula-rasa seeds enter a permanent failure mode under budget
constraints.
Third, priors are \emph{robust to miscalibration}
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:prior_mismatch}): even domain-mismatched
priors reduce regret relative to a cold start, and $n_\mathit{eff}$
serves as a safety knob that limits worst-case exposure when prior
quality is uncertain.
The forgetting factor $\gamma{=}\vbGamma$ unifies these roles: it
preserves prior influence long enough to matter during the cold-start
window (${\sim}333$ effective-memory steps), then hands control to the
online learner.  The burn-in ablation shows that this handoff is lossy
in unconstrained routing, neutral under tight budget pressure, and
beneficial when the bandit must co-adapt with the pacer---a
regime-dependent interaction that underscores the importance of
evaluating routing systems under realistic budget constraints.
