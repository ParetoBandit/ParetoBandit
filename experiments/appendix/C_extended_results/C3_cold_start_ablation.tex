\subsection{Cold-Start Ablation: Value of Dev-Set Training}
\label{appendix:cold_start}

A potential fairness concern with the Figure~4 comparison is that banditGPT
trains on 1,121 dev prompts (Phase~1 burn-in) before evaluation, while
RouteLLM-MF uses only pre-trained weights with no access to the dev set.
This ablation quantifies the value of dev-set training by comparing:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Warm-start:} Standard protocol --- burn-in on dev set,
          then evaluate on holdout (same as Figure~4).
    \item \textbf{Cold-start:} Skip burn-in entirely --- evaluate on holdout
          using 80K-battle warmup priors only, with no online adaptation.
\end{itemize}

Both conditions use the same Corralling architecture ($\eta{=}1.0$,
$\gamma{=}0.05$), prior normalization ($\neff{=}10$), and $\alpha$ decay
($2.0 \to 0.1$).  The only difference is whether Phase~1 (burn-in on
$N{=}1{,}121$ dev prompts) is executed.

\subsubsection{Key Findings}

\paragraph{1.\ Online adaptation adds substantial value.}
Warm-start banditGPT (0.914) outperforms cold-start (0.839) by 7.5
percentage points at $\lambda{=}0$.  This demonstrates that the online
learning machinery --- contextual LinUCB with UCB exploration, Corralling
meta-learning, and bandit feedback --- extracts substantial routing signal
from only 1,121 dev prompts.  The learning curve analysis
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:learning_curves}) shows that this improvement
occurs rapidly and smoothly: by 200 online samples, holdout reward reaches
0.893 and surpasses RouteLLM-MF's peak (0.883).

\paragraph{2.\ The gap narrows monotonically with $\lambda$.}
As the cost penalty increases and routing shifts toward the cheap model
(Mixtral), both conditions converge.  At $\lambda \geq 0.5$, both
route nearly 100\% to Mixtral and achieve identical reward (0.823).  The
training advantage is concentrated in the quality-sensitive regime where the
router must make non-trivial contextual decisions.

\paragraph{3.\ Cross-domain priors provide a viable starting point.}
Cold-start reward (0.839 at $\lambda{=}0$) exceeds both random routing
(0.814) and static-Mixtral (0.823), demonstrating that the warmup priors
from 80K RouteLLM battles transfer useful signal even cross-domain.  This
is a significant improvement over earlier router implementations where
cold-start fell below random, and confirms that the production router's
prior normalization ($\neff{=}10$) and T-Shirt Sizing initialization
produce a useful starting configuration.

The priors are nonetheless imperfect: cold-start (0.839) falls well below
RouteLLM-MF's peak (0.883), which uses 100K prompt-specific pairs.
This is consistent with Figure~3's prior degradation sweep, where
Corralling with priors achieves 30--40\% lower regret than tabula rasa
at low corruption but regret rises as priors become adversarial.
The practical implication is that \textbf{deploying the library with
warmup priors and no online data already beats naive baselines} ---
a critical property for real-world adoption where collecting labeled
data before deployment may not be feasible.

\paragraph{4.\ Online learning closes the gap to optimality.}
Warm-start banditGPT reaches 0.914 --- closing 70\% of the gap between
random (0.814) and oracle (0.953).  The Corralling mechanism detects that
online data outperforms the cross-domain priors and shifts $94.4\%$ of
routing weight to the tabula rasa expert
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:learning_curves}), while LinUCB's online updates
rapidly calibrate model estimates within ${\sim}200$ steps.

\paragraph{5.\ Fair comparison with RouteLLM.}
RouteLLM-MF achieves peak reward of 0.883 on the holdout set (from the
Figure~4 Pareto sweep).  banditGPT cold-start (0.839) is below this,
confirming that banditGPT's advantage at moderate-to-high budgets
in Figure~4 stems from online adaptation on the dev set, not from unfair
access to additional data.  The cold-start gap ($0.839$ vs.\ $0.883$)
quantifies the value of RouteLLM's 100K prompt-specific training pairs
in the absence of online learning.  At low budgets, RouteLLM-MF's
prompt-level discrimination makes it competitive even against the fully
trained banditGPT (Section~\ref{sec:nonmonotone}).

\subsubsection{Deployment Guidance}

This ablation clarifies when and how to deploy banditGPT under different
data availability conditions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{With burn-in data ($\geq 200$ labeled prompts):}
          Use the standard warm-start protocol.  By 200 prompts the system
          surpasses RouteLLM-MF's peak (0.893 vs.\ 0.883), and by 750
          steps reaches $0.911$ with tight CI ($\pm 0.003$).
    \item \textbf{With limited data ($50$--$200$ prompts):}
          Use warm-start with warmup priors ($\neff{=}10$).  Priors
          accelerate early learning (Figure~3) and the cold-start floor
          (0.839) already exceeds static baselines.  Expect holdout
          reward in the $0.858$--$0.893$ range depending on data quantity.
    \item \textbf{Without any deployment data (true cold start):}
          Cross-domain priors alone yield 0.839 --- above random (0.814)
          and static Mixtral (0.823).  The library is \emph{deployable
          out-of-box} with warmup priors, providing useful routing from
          the first request while online data accumulates in the
          background.  Quality will improve automatically as online
          learning kicks in.
    \item \textbf{Fairness vs.\ baselines:}
          banditGPT's advantage over RouteLLM-MF (0.914 vs.\ 0.883) stems
          from online adaptation.  When comparing to pre-trained baselines,
          report both warm-start and cold-start conditions to separate
          the algorithm's contribution from the data advantage.
\end{itemize}
