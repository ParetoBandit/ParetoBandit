\subsection{Architectural Ablation: Component Contributions}
\label{appendix:architectural_ablation}

To quantify the marginal contribution of each architectural component, we
evaluate seven routing strategies that progressively add one capability at a
time.  All methods share the same evaluation protocol as the main Pareto
frontier experiment (Figure~4): training on the dev set ($N{=}1{,}121$) with
bandit feedback, evaluation on the holdout set ($N{=}750$) with pure
exploitation, 20 independent seeds per configuration, and the same 10-value
$\lambda$ sweep.  Hyperparameters ($\alpha$: $2.0 \to 0.1$, $\neff{=}10$,
reward normalization bounds) are identical across all contextual methods.

\subsubsection{Methods}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Random:} Uniform random model selection (no learning).
    \item \textbf{EMA Tracker:} Non-contextual exponential moving average
          ($\alpha_{\text{ema}}{=}0.15$) with $\varepsilon$-greedy exploration
          ($\varepsilon{=}0.1$). Learns per-model reward averages but ignores
          prompt content.
    \item \textbf{$\varepsilon$-greedy (no priors):} Contextual ridge
          regression with $\varepsilon{=}0.1$ random exploration.  No warmup
          priors (learns from scratch).
    \item \textbf{$\varepsilon$-greedy (w/ priors):} Same as above but
          initialized with 80K-battle warmup priors ($\neff{=}10$).
    \item \textbf{LinUCB (no priors):} Contextual UCB exploration (no
          $\varepsilon$-random component). Learns from scratch.
    \item \textbf{LinUCB (w/ priors):} LinUCB initialized with warmup priors.
          Equivalent to a single warmup expert without Corralling.
    \item \textbf{banditGPT-Hybrid:} Full system --- Corralling ($\eta{=}0.1$)
          over warmup + tabula rasa LinUCB experts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Key Findings}

\paragraph{1.\ Context features are the dominant contributor ($+$10.4\%).}
The largest single gain comes from adding contextual features:
$\varepsilon$-greedy with context (0.916) outperforms the non-contextual EMA
tracker (0.812) by 10.4 percentage points.  This validates the PCA feature
pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:cross_domain_transfer}): prompt embeddings contain
strong routing signal that non-contextual methods cannot exploit.

\paragraph{2.\ UCB exploration outperforms $\varepsilon$-greedy.}
LinUCB (no priors) achieves 0.923 vs.\ $\varepsilon$-greedy (no priors) at
0.916 --- a meaningful advantage ($+0.7$\%).  UCB's optimism-based exploration
targets regions of high uncertainty rather than exploring uniformly,
improving sample efficiency during burn-in.

\paragraph{3.\ banditGPT-Hybrid trades marginal peak quality for robustness.}
The full system (0.914) is slightly below LinUCB (no priors) at 0.923
($\Delta{=}-0.009$) at $\lambda{=}0$.  This gap is the cost of maintaining
two experts plus meta-learning overhead.  However, banditGPT-Hybrid provides
a property that simpler methods lack: \emph{dominant performance when priors
are useful and adaptive cost--quality control}.  Figure~3 demonstrates
this directly: with good-to-moderate priors ($\alpha \leq 0.5$), Corralling
achieves 29--39\% lower regret than both warmup-only and tabula rasa
(regret 32--38 vs.\ 52+), by exploiting complementary alpha schedules
across its two experts.  At higher $\lambda$ values
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation_lambda_sweep}), banditGPT-Hybrid maintains
higher quality than LinUCB --- e.g., 0.886 vs.\ 0.861 at $\lambda{=}0.2$
--- because Corralling's adaptive expert weighting navigates the
cost--quality trade-off more smoothly.

The Corralling mechanism decisively identifies the tabula rasa expert
as superior for this cross-domain dataset, allocating $0.944 \pm 0.069$
weight to it (Appendix~\ref{appendix:learning_curves}).  The low variance
($\pm 0.069$) across seeds confirms consistent convergence.

\paragraph{4.\ Non-contextual learning adds no value over random.}
The EMA tracker (0.812) performs comparably to random routing (0.814), and
in fact defaults to a single model (GPT-4-Turbo at $\lambda{=}0$, Mixtral at
$\lambda{\geq}0.5$). With only two models whose global win rates are similar,
non-contextual tracking cannot distinguish them without prompt-level features.
EMA Tracker, LinUCB (no priors), and LinUCB (w/ priors) all exhibit zero
variance across 20 seeds ($\dagger$ in Table~\ref{tab:architectural_ablation}).
EMA Tracker is deterministic because with $K{=}2$ and $\varepsilon{=}0.1$,
the EMA converges to the same single-model policy regardless of seed.
LinUCB variants are deterministic because, after 1,121 burn-in steps, the
well-conditioned $A$ matrices make the UCB bonus negligibly small,
producing identical greedy policies regardless of seed or prior state.

\paragraph{5.\ Warmup priors are neutral after full burn-in.}
Adding warmup priors has no measurable effect after 1,121 burn-in steps:
$\varepsilon$-greedy achieves 0.916 with or without priors, and LinUCB
achieves 0.923 with or without priors ($\Delta{=}0.000$ in both cases).
This confirms that 1,121 burn-in samples ($100\times$ the prior's
$\neff{=}10$ effective samples) completely overwhelm the prior
contribution.

The $\neff$ ablation (Appendix~\ref{appendix:pca_neff}) corroborates this:
$\neff{=}1$ through $\neff{=}100$ all yield ${\sim}0.912$--$0.915$,
because the burn-in data dominates regardless of prior strength.  Warmup
priors therefore provide the most value in \textbf{low-data online
deployment} (routing live traffic from step~1): Figure~3 shows Corralling
with warmup priors achieves 29--39\% lower regret than tabula rasa
(37 vs.\ 52 at $\alpha{=}0$).  The cold-start ablation
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:cold_start}) further demonstrates that priors
alone yield 0.839 quality --- above static baselines --- providing a
useful starting point before online learning takes over.

\subsubsection{Practical Guidance}

These findings translate into clear guidance for deploying the library:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Always use contextual features.}  The PCA embedding pipeline
          accounts for 10.4\% of routing quality --- more than all other
          components combined.  Non-contextual methods (EMA, static routing)
          cannot distinguish models whose global win rates are similar.
    \item \textbf{Use the full banditGPT-Hybrid (the library default).}
          Corralling dominates both warmup-only and tabula rasa for
          good-to-moderate priors (29--39\% lower regret), and the 53\%
          breakeven threshold means it has better expected regret than tabula
          rasa for most practical deployments (Figure~3).
    \item \textbf{Use LinUCB (no priors) when priors are unavailable.}
          If no warmup data is available, LinUCB without priors achieves
          0.923 --- the highest single-expert reward --- and is the simplest
          contextual option.  Corralling over two tabula rasa experts
          provides no additional benefit in this case.
    \item \textbf{Warmup priors matter most in low-data regimes.}
          If deploying live without burn-in data (Figure~3's scenario),
          warmup priors reduce early regret by 39\%.  If burn-in data is
          plentiful ($\geq 500$ prompts; Appendix~\ref{appendix:learning_curves}),
          priors are washed out and can be safely omitted.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Cost-Quality Trade-Off Across $\lambda$ Sweep}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation_lambda_sweep} shows representative operating points
across the full $\lambda$ sweep.  All methods converge to Mixtral-only
routing (reward ${\approx}0.823$, cost ${\approx}\$0.0003$) at high $\lambda$.
At moderate cost penalties ($\lambda{=}0.2$--$0.5$), banditGPT-Hybrid
\emph{outperforms} all baselines: 0.886 vs.\ 0.861 (LinUCB) at
$\lambda{=}0.2$, and 0.860 vs.\ 0.823 at $\lambda{=}0.5$.  This advantage
arises because Corralling's adaptive expert weighting navigates the
cost--quality trade-off more smoothly than single-expert strategies,
mirroring the Pareto frontier analysis in Figure~4.
