\subsubsection{Key Findings}

\paragraph{1.\ Rapid initial improvement.}
The online learning machinery is remarkably data-efficient: the first 50
steps account for the majority of the cold-start gap, lifting holdout reward
from $0.839$ to $0.893$ ($+5.4$ percentage points).  Notably, the
production router's cold-start quality ($0.839$) already exceeds both random
routing ($0.814$) and static Mixtral ($0.823$), confirming that the
cross-domain warmup priors provide genuine value before any online
observation.  A small number of online samples then rapidly close the
remaining gap toward convergence.

\paragraph{2.\ Convergence after ${\sim}750$ steps.}
By step~750 (67\% of the full burn-in), reward reaches 0.905 with CI
($\pm$0.005).  The final 371 steps add negligible improvement.
This suggests the burn-in could be shortened to ${\sim}750$ steps with no
measurable performance loss, reducing cold-start latency in production
deployments.

\paragraph{3.\ Decisive expert weighting.}
The Corralling meta-learner decisively identifies the tabula rasa expert
as superior for this cross-domain setting.  After full burn-in, the mean
expert weights are: tabula rasa $= 0.891 \pm 0.163$,
warmup $= 0.109 \pm 0.163$.  This strong delegation to the
learning-from-scratch expert demonstrates that Corralling correctly detects
cross-domain prior misspecification and resolves the exploration-exploitation
dilemma.  The moderate variance across seeds ($\pm 0.163$) reflects
stochastic expert selection dynamics, though the majority weight
consistently favors the tabula rasa expert.

\paragraph{4.\ Smooth convergence with modest intermediate variance.}
Between steps 50--500, holdout reward generally improves from $0.893$
to $0.896$, with confidence intervals of $\pm 0.005$--$0.014$.  A modest
dip at step~100 ($0.875 \pm 0.014$) reflects stochastic exploration
effects that resolve with more data.

By step~750, the CI contracts to $\pm 0.005$, and the final three
checkpoints (750, 1{,}000, 1{,}121) all fall within $0.903$--$0.906$,
confirming stable convergence.  The system is not sensitive to the exact
burn-in length beyond ${\sim}500$ steps.

\subsubsection{Burn-In Length Recommendations}

The learning curve data suggests concrete guidelines for choosing burn-in
length in production:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Immediate deployment is viable.}  Cold-start quality
          ($0.839$) already exceeds static baselines and random routing.
          A user can deploy the library out-of-box with warmup priors and
          begin collecting online data while serving useful routing from
          the first request.
    \item \textbf{Minimum viable burn-in: ${\sim}300$ prompts.}  By step
          300, mean holdout reward reaches ${\sim}0.884$
          ($\pm$0.013), surpassing RouteLLM-MF's peak (0.883).
    \item \textbf{Recommended burn-in: 500--750 prompts.}  Achieves
          0.896--0.905 with tightening variance.  Captures most of the
          improvement over cold-start.
    \item \textbf{Diminishing returns beyond 500.}  The final 621 steps
          (500 $\to$ 1,121) add only ${\sim}0.007$.  Allocating
          additional burn-in data yields negligible benefit.
    \item \textbf{Monitor the CI, not just the mean.}  When the CI
          contracts below $\pm$0.005, the router has converged and can
          be switched to pure exploitation.  This typically occurs by
          step~750.
\end{itemize}
