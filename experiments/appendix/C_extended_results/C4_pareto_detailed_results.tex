\paragraph{Key metrics.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{banditGPT peak quality:} 0.905 at cost \$0.00883
          ($\lambda{=}0.02$, 20 seeds, 95\% CI $\pm$0.007;
          consistent with learning-curve convergence at ${\sim}0.904$ by
          step~900 in Appendix~\ref{appendix:learning_curves}).
    \item \textbf{RouteLLM-MF peak quality:} 0.883 at cost \$0.00688
          (deterministic, at $\tau{\approx}0.12$).
    \item \textbf{Oracle:} 0.953 at cost \$0.00195 (perfect per-prompt routing).
    \item \textbf{Gap closure:} banditGPT closes
          $\frac{0.905 - 0.823}{0.953 - 0.823} = 63.1\%$ of the
          Oracle--Mixtral gap, vs.\ RouteLLM-MF's
          $\frac{0.883 - 0.823}{0.953 - 0.823} = 45.9\%$.
    \item \textbf{Two-regime structure:}
          RouteLLM-MF's dense threshold sweep
          (Table~\ref{tab:pareto_baselines}) reveals non-monotonic
          quality---peak reward 0.883 occurs at $\tau{\approx}0.12$--$0.14$,
          then \emph{declines} at higher thresholds---because GPT-4-Turbo
          underperforms Mixtral on a subset of prompts in this benchmark.
          At low budgets (cost $< \$0.005$), RouteLLM-MF's prompt-level
          discrimination from 100K pre-trained pairs makes it competitive
          with or superior to banditGPT; at moderate-to-high budgets,
          banditGPT's online aggregate calibration dominates
          (Section~\ref{sec:nonmonotone}).
\end{itemize}
