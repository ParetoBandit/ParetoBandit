\subsection{Adaptive Drift Detection (Figure~\ref{fig:adaptive_drift})}
\label{sec:adaptive_drift}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Warmup priors encode model-quality rankings observed on benchmark data.
When the deployed prompt distribution shifts---e.g., from curated
benchmarks to real-world user traffic---these rankings may become
miscalibrated, causing the router to over-route to a now-suboptimal
arm.  A production router must detect such shifts and adapt
\emph{without manual intervention}.

\paragraph{Setup.}
We construct a two-phase online stream over the $K{=}2$ portfolio
(Llama-3.1-8B vs.\ Gemini-2.5-Pro, $\lambda{=}0.2$):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Phase~1 (steps 0--500):}
        In-distribution prompts drawn from the Pareto benchmark
        train split.  All warm-started conditions perform well because
        their priors match the data.
  \item \textbf{Phase~2 (steps 501--3{,}306):}
        Prompts from the K4 real-world corpus with a controlled
        $2\sigma$ synthetic perturbation: the leading~8 PCA embedding
        components are shifted by $2\sigma$ of their training-set
        standard deviation, and Llama rewards are boosted by
        $+0.04$ per~$\sigma$ ($+0.08$ net).  This couples covariate
        shift with a reward-structure change that makes Llama
        competitive on a larger fraction of prompts (cost-adjusted
        oracle: 60\% Llama).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
The synthetic shift is calibrated so that (i)~the covariate shift is
detectable by the embedding-based drift detector, and (ii)~the reward
perturbation is large enough to make warmup priors demonstrably
suboptimal but small enough to remain plausible
(Section~\ref{sec:drift_calibration}).

Four conditions are compared (20~seeds, 95\% $t$-CIs):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Warmup-only:}
        Strong priors ($n_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}5{,}000$, $\alpha{=}0.5$),
        no adaptation mechanism.  Represents the failure mode: priors
        persist and resist correction from online feedback.
  \item \textbf{Oracle ff$=$0.999:}
        Same priors but with a pre-configured exponential forgetting
        factor (half-life ${\approx}693$ steps) that continuously
        down-weights old sufficient statistics.  Represents a
        practitioner who correctly anticipates nonstationarity but
        must pre-commit to a decay rate.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive (Reset):}
        Same priors with the BanditGPT covariate shift detector
        enabled.  When the detector fires, the router performs a
        \emph{tabula rasa reset}: it discards its accumulated
        sufficient statistics and re-learns from scratch on the new
        distribution.  The detector itself re-enters burn-in after
        each reset.
  \item \textbf{Tabula Rasa:}
        No priors, no adaptation.  A lower bound on what online
        learning alone can achieve.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Regret (panel~a).}
Panel~(a) plots cumulative \emph{cost-adjusted regret}, the primary
metric of online routing quality.  At each request, regret measures the
gap between the router's chosen model and the best model for that
prompt, accounting for both response quality and API cost.
In operational terms, each unit of regret is one request where the
router made a suboptimal cost--quality trade-off: either paying for an
expensive model when a cheaper one would have delivered comparable
quality, or accepting unnecessarily low quality when a better model
was affordable within the budget.  Lower cumulative regret therefore
translates directly to better allocation of API spend.

During Phase~1 all warm-started conditions track identically,
confirming that the priors are well-calibrated in-distribution.
Tabula Rasa incurs 16\% higher Phase~1 regret (36.4 vs.\ 31.3),
consistent with the cold-start penalty documented in
Section~\ref{sec:warmup_ablation_k2}.

After the shift, trajectories diverge sharply.
\emph{Warmup-only} accumulates the highest regret (226.6) because its
strong priors resist online correction: the $n_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}5{,}000$
pseudo-observations dominate the ${\sim}2{,}800$ Phase~2 real
observations, keeping the posterior anchored to the stale ranking.
In practice, this means the router continues sending requests to the
expensive Gemini model even when the shifted distribution has made
Llama a cost-effective alternative for a majority of prompts.
\emph{Oracle~ff} mitigates this by exponentially discounting priors,
reaching 211.9.
\emph{Adaptive (Reset)} achieves the lowest final regret
(\textbf{206.5}), an \textbf{8.9\%} reduction vs.\ Warmup-only
and a 2.5\% improvement over Oracle~ff.
By fully discarding stale statistics at the detected change-point,
the adaptive condition avoids the ``ghost prior'' inertia that even
the oracle forgetting factor must slowly dissipate.

\paragraph{Drift detection (panel~b).}
The BanditGPT drift detector monitors the mean squared $z$-score
of incoming PCA embeddings relative to a running baseline established
during a 50-step burn-in.  An exponentially weighted moving average
(EMA, $\alpha_d{=}0.05$) smooths the score.  Drift is confirmed when
the EMA exceeds $\mu_{\mathrm{base}} + k \cdot \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}$
for 20 consecutive steps, where $k{=}2\sigma$ is calibrated via a
regret-grounded sweep (Section~\ref{sec:drift_calibration}).

Panel~(b) shows the detector's EMA chi-squared score (solid green) rising
sharply after the Phase~2 boundary and crossing the $2\sigma$ threshold
(dotted red) at step~${\approx}550$.  The prior reset fires, after which
the detector re-enters burn-in on the shifted distribution and
stabilises without re-triggering---a desirable property confirming
that the threshold is not over-sensitive.

\paragraph{Cheap-arm discovery (panel~c).}
Panel~(c) plots each condition's Llama routing fraction over time.
The \emph{cost-adjusted optimum} (dashed red at 60\%) is the Llama
fraction a clairvoyant router would choose on the shifted distribution
at $\lambda{=}0.2$: on each prompt it selects
$\arg\max_a [r_a - \lambda \cdot c_a]$ using ground-truth rewards,
and 60\% of those optimal decisions route to Llama.
This line is a ceiling---no online learner can reach it without full
information---but the gap between each condition and this ceiling
reveals how much cost-saving opportunity remains unexploited.

\emph{Warmup-only} routes only 42\% to Llama by the end of the
experiment---well below the 60\% optimum---because its priors anchor
the posterior toward Gemini.
After prior reset at step~${\approx}550$, \emph{Adaptive (Reset)}
ramps up Llama routing at the same rate as \emph{Oracle~ff},
both converging to ${\sim}47\%$ by step~3{,}306.
\emph{Tabula Rasa}, lacking priors entirely, starts at 100\% Llama
at step~0: absent any learned quality signal, the cost penalty
alone determines routing, directing all traffic to the cheapest arm.
This is the \emph{cold-start failure mode} that warmup priors
eliminate---within Phase~1 the router learns the correct
in-distribution ranking and Llama drops below 5\%.
After the shift, Tabula Rasa must re-learn from scratch without the
benefit of a clean break-point, ultimately matching Adaptive's final
Llama fraction but at higher cumulative regret.

\paragraph{Production implications.}
The experiment models a concrete deployment scenario: a team trains
an LLM router on curated benchmark data, deploys it, and user traffic
gradually diverges from the evaluation distribution---through seasonal
topic shifts, new product features, or upstream model updates that
alter relative quality rankings.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Cost of inaction.}
        Without adaptation (\emph{Warmup-only}), the 20-unit regret
        gap vs.\ Adaptive means that over the 2{,}800-request shifted
        window, the router makes a worse cost--quality decision on
        ${\sim}500$ additional prompts---typically sending them to
        the $500{\times}$-more-expensive Gemini (\$0.015/req) when
        Llama (\$0.00003/req) would have sufficed.
        The 18-percentage-point gap in Llama routing fraction
        (42\% vs.\ the 60\% cost-adjusted optimum) quantifies this
        misallocation: at 10{,}000 requests/day, each percentage
        point of unnecessary Gemini routing costs an additional
        ${\sim}\$1.50$/day in API fees with no quality benefit.

  \item \textbf{No manual intervention required.}
        The embedding-based covariate shift detector fires within
        ${\sim}50$ steps of the true change-point, using only the
        input embeddings---it requires no reward labels, no human
        monitoring dashboards, and no retraining pipeline.
        This is critical for 24/7 production systems where shift may
        occur overnight or during weekend traffic.

  \item \textbf{Better than pre-configured decay.}
        A practitioner who anticipates nonstationarity might deploy a
        forgetting factor (\emph{Oracle~ff$=$0.999}).  This helps, but
        it continuously discounts \emph{all} history---including
        in-distribution experience that remains valid---imposing a
        small but persistent exploration cost even when the
        distribution is stable.
        The adaptive reset is surgically targeted: it preserves all
        Phase~1 learning until the detector confirms a genuine shift,
        then cleanly restarts.  The resulting 2.5\% regret improvement
        (5.4 fewer suboptimal routing decisions over the horizon)
        comes despite requiring no advance knowledge of the shift's
        existence or timing.

  \item \textbf{Stability after adaptation.}
        After resetting, the detector re-calibrates on the new
        distribution without spurious re-triggers, and the router
        converges to near-optimal routing within the experiment
        horizon.  In production, this means the system does not
        oscillate between reset states, and operators can trust
        that a single reset suffices per genuine distributional event.

  \item \textbf{Cold-start risk without priors.}
        Tabula Rasa illustrates the alternative: forgo priors entirely
        to avoid the miscalibration problem.  But this trades one
        failure mode for another---at deployment time, every request
        is routed to the cheapest model (100\% Llama at step~0),
        sacrificing quality until enough feedback accumulates.
        BanditGPT with adaptive reset combines the best of both
        worlds: warmup priors eliminate the cold start, and the drift
        detector prevents those priors from becoming a liability.
\end{enumerate}
