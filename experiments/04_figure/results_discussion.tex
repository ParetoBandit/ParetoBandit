\subsection{$K{=}10$: Multi-Model Pareto Frontier (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_k10})}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
Real-world deployments typically involve more than two models.  As the
portfolio grows, the routing problem becomes combinatorially harder:
which model to select depends on prompt characteristics, cost constraints,
and the relative strengths of each model.  RouteLLM does not natively
support $K > 2$, so this experiment evaluates whether BanditGPT's
architecture---Corralling over heterogeneous LinUCB experts with
family-based parameter sharing---scales to larger portfolios.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The $K{=}10$ portfolio spans four cost tiers:\ cheap
(\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B}, \texttt{Mixtral-8x7B}, \texttt{Gemma-3-27B}),
mid (\texttt{Claude-Haiku-4.5}, \texttt{DeepSeek-V3},
\texttt{Gemini-2.5-Flash}, \texttt{Llama-4-Maverick}), and expensive
(\texttt{Claude-Sonnet-4}, \texttt{GPT-4-Turbo}, \texttt{GPT-4.1}).
BanditGPT trains on the dev-train portion of the online-learn pool
(${\sim}426$ prompts, 80\% of $n{=}533$) from the three-way split;
the remaining ${\sim}107$ prompts form the dev-val set used exclusively
for hyperparameter selection.  The canonical holdout ($n{=}750$) is
reserved for final evaluation.
The cost penalty $\lambda$ is swept over 33 values in $[0,\,1]$,
with fine spacing in the transition zone ($0.18$--$0.22$) where routing
behavior shifts sharply between cost tiers.  All results are averaged
over 20 seeds.

\paragraph{Results.}
BanditGPT's dev-selected Pareto frontier dominates tabula rasa (plain
LinUCB, no priors, no Corralling) across the full cost range.
Dev-selected Pareto AUC: BanditGPT $0.652$ vs.\ tabula rasa $0.332$
(advantage $+0.320$; paired bootstrap 95\% CI
$[+0.309,\, +0.331]$, $p{=}0.0$, 1{,}000 holdout resamples with joint
cost-reward resampling).
At the lowest cost point (\$0.00005/req), BanditGPT achieves $0.861$
reward---within $5\%$ of the \emph{best static model} (GPT-4.1 at
$0.910$)---while using $68\!\times$ lower cost.

At the highest budgets ($\lambda{=}0$) the two frontiers converge:
BanditGPT $0.893$ vs.\ tabula rasa $0.905$.  This is expected: with a
large budget and no cost pressure, the optimal policy is pure
exploitation, and tabula rasa's decaying $\alpha$ ($0.25 \to 0.01$)
converges to near-greedy selection.  BanditGPT's warmup expert
maintains constant exploration ($\alpha{=}0.25$) for distribution-shift
robustness, which imposes a small quality tax in stationary,
budget-unconstrained regimes.

\paragraph{Single-point baselines: why they cannot produce a frontier.}
Each non-BanditGPT baseline in Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_k10} appears as a
\emph{single point} rather than a Pareto frontier.  This is not a
plotting choice---it reflects a fundamental limitation: none of these
methods can continuously trade cost for quality.

\emph{Oracle} ($R{=}0.968$, \$0.0014/req) selects the per-prompt best
model with clairvoyant access to rewards.  It is a single point because
it defines one unique optimal assignment; there is no knob to turn.  It
establishes the ceiling for any contextual routing policy.

\emph{Best static} (GPT-4.1; $R{=}0.910$, \$0.0034/req) always routes
every prompt to a single pre-chosen model.  It is a single point because
a static policy has no mechanism to vary cost: every request incurs the
same model price.  BanditGPT matches this reward at comparable cost
($\lambda{=}0.03$: $R{=}0.896$, \$0.0034/req) but can also operate at
$60\!\times$ lower cost ($\lambda{=}0.5$: $R{=}0.861$, \$0.00005/req)
by routing easy prompts to cheap models---a trade-off unavailable to any
static policy.

\emph{Best-static+noise} ($\varepsilon$-greedy perturbation of the
training-set best model; \texttt{Claude-Sonnet-4}, $R{=}0.886$,
\$0.0059/req) is the most expensive baseline---and does not deliver the
highest reward.  It is a single point because $\varepsilon$-greedy
exploration is not cost-aware: it explores uniformly, driving up cost
without targeted benefit.  BanditGPT achieves higher reward at
$42\%$ lower cost ($\lambda{=}0.03$: $R{=}0.896$, \$0.0034/req).

\emph{UCB1} (non-contextual; \texttt{Gemini-2.5-Flash}, $R{=}0.886$,
\$0.0030/req) converges to a single arm because, without prompt
features, a bandit can only identify the globally best model---it cannot
differentiate easy from hard prompts.  Its final reward ($0.886$) is
nearly identical to best-static+noise ($0.886$), confirming that a
non-contextual bandit collapses to static best-arm selection.
At comparable cost, BanditGPT achieves $+0.011$ higher reward
($\lambda{=}0.03$: $R{=}0.896$, \$0.0034/req); at comparable reward,
BanditGPT operates at $36\%$ lower cost ($\lambda{=}0.05$: $R{=}0.884$,
\$0.0019/req).  The value of context in $K{=}10$ thus manifests
primarily as \emph{cost efficiency}---contextual features enable the
router to serve easy prompts cheaply, producing a full Pareto frontier
rather than a single operating point.

\emph{Random} (uniform model selection; $R{=}0.823$, \$0.0026/req) is a
single point because it has no parameters to tune.  BanditGPT exceeds
Random's reward at $50\!\times$ lower cost ($\lambda{=}0.5$: $R{=}0.861$,
\$0.00005/req).

\paragraph{Why this matters.}
BanditGPT is the only method that produces a deployable Pareto
frontier---a continuum of cost-quality operating points---rather than a
single take-it-or-leave-it configuration.  This flexibility is enabled
by two properties unique to the contextual bandit formulation: (i)~the
cost penalty~$\lambda$ provides an explicit knob to trade quality for
cost, and (ii)~contextual features allow the router to \emph{vary its
routing per prompt}, sending easy requests to cheap models and hard
requests to expensive ones.  Without context (UCB1), the ``frontier''
collapses to a single arm; without the cost knob (best static), the
operator is locked to one model's price.

The $K{=}10$ results show that BanditGPT's hybrid architecture
(Corralling + warmup priors + family sharing) achieves a statistically
significant dev-selected Pareto AUC advantage over na\"{i}ve online
learning ($+0.320$; $p{=}0.0$).  That such a severely
data-constrained setting (fewer than 43 interactions per arm on average)
yields Pareto dominance underscores the effectiveness of the warmup
prior architecture: the priors from 43 pre-trained models provide a
strong initialisation that the online learner refines rather than learns
from scratch, making every interaction count.
Corralling's meta-learning prevents over-commitment to either the
informed or uninformed expert.  This architecture is
\emph{the only tested method that simultaneously handles cost
constraints, large model portfolios, and cold-start initialization}---a
combination required by real-world routing deployments.
