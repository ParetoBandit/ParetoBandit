\subsection{The Value of Warmup Priors (Figure~\ref{fig:warmup_ablation})}
\label{sec:warmup_ablation}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
BanditGPT's architecture has two distinctive ingredients: (i)~warmup
priors---LinUCB sufficient statistics derived from offline model evaluations---and
(ii)~Corralling~\citep{agarwal2017corralling}, a meta-learner that
hedges between a prior-informed expert and a tabula rasa expert.
Figure~\ref{fig:prequential} demonstrated that BanditGPT outperforms
supervised baselines; this experiment isolates \emph{how much of that
advantage comes from warmup priors} by comparing the full system against
a tabula rasa ablation that removes both priors and Corralling.

\paragraph{Setup.}
We evaluate on the same $K{=}3$ portfolio and train-then-freeze protocol
as Section~\ref{sec:prequential_results}.  Both variants share identical
data (dev-train $n \approx 426$; holdout $n{=}750$), seeds ($20$), and
cost penalty sweep ($33$~$\lambda$ values).  Per-expert hyperparameters
($\alpha$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $\gamma$) are loaded from
Appendix~\ref{sec:alpha_neff_ablation} (dev-val grid search).
Supervised baselines (KNN, SVM, MLP) are retrained fresh on the same
dev-train split to provide reference anchors.

\paragraph{Pareto frontier (panel a).}
Warmup priors shift the entire cost--quality frontier upward.

The BanditGPT frontier dominates tabula rasa across the full cost range,
with a statistically significant Pareto AUC advantage
(paired bootstrap 95\% CI, 1{,}000 holdout resamples).

At low $\lambda$ (high-budget regime), the gap between the two variants
narrows, as expected: given enough budget and no cost pressure, the
routing problem reduces to pure exploitation, where the tabula rasa
expert can eventually converge.  The priors' advantage is largest in
the \emph{cost-constrained} regime ($\lambda \gtrsim 0.1$), where the
router must make intelligent cost--quality trade-offs from limited
interactions---precisely the regime relevant to production deployments.

\paragraph{Learning curve (panel b).}
The learning curve reveals the mechanism: warmup priors provide
\emph{immediate quality at step~0}---before any online interaction---because
the prior sufficient statistics encode quality signals from the 43-model
offline evaluation pool.  Tabula rasa starts with no information and must
accumulate reward observations before making informed routing decisions.

This ``cold start'' advantage is especially valuable in production,
where the first few hundred interactions determine user experience and
retention.  With warmup priors, BanditGPT can deploy at
supervised-baseline quality from the first request, then \emph{improve}
as online data arrives.

\paragraph{What warmup priors contribute.}
The ablation demonstrates that warmup priors serve three complementary
roles:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Cold-start quality.}
    At step~0, the warm-started system routes at competitive quality
    without any online data, eliminating the ``exploration tax'' that
    tabula rasa pays during early interactions.

    \item \textbf{Sample efficiency.}
    The priors provide a strong initialisation that the online learner
    refines rather than learns from scratch.  With fewer than 43 online
    interactions per arm on average, every observation contributes to
    fine-tuning the prior rather than building a model from zero.

    \item \textbf{Cost-constrained routing.}
    The Pareto frontier advantage is concentrated in the cost-constrained
    regime, where informed priors enable the router to distinguish
    easy-for-cheap-models prompts from hard-need-expensive-models
    prompts \emph{without} having to explore each cost tier.  This is
    the regime most relevant to real-world budgets.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relationship to Corralling.}
Corralling is necessary but not sufficient: it provides the mechanism
for the prior-informed and tabula rasa experts to collaborate, but the
\emph{quality} of that collaboration depends on the priors themselves.
The tabula rasa ablation removes both Corralling and the priors
simultaneously, representing the worst-case ``learn from scratch'' scenario.
Appendix~\ref{sec:alpha_neff_ablation} shows how the prior strength
($n_{\mathrm{eff}}$) affects the balance: too-strong priors can
over-regularize, while too-weak priors revert to the tabula rasa regime.
