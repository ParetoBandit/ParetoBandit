    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../experiments/04_figure/results/figure4_adaptive_drift.pdf}
    \caption{Adaptive drift detection and prior reset under a controlled
    $2\sigma$ synthetic distribution shift ($K{=}2$: \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B}
    vs.\ \texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro}, $\lambda{=}0.2$).
    %
    Phase~1 (steps 0--500) draws from the in-distribution Pareto
    benchmark; Phase~2 (steps 501--3{,}306) draws from a K4 real-world
    distribution whose embeddings are perturbed by $2\sigma$ on the
    leading~8 PCA components and whose Llama rewards are boosted
    ($+0.08$ net), simulating prior miscalibration.
    %
    \textbf{(a)}~Cumulative cost-adjusted regret for four conditions
    (20~seeds, 95\% $t$-CI).
    Each unit of regret corresponds to one request where the router
    chose a suboptimal cost--quality trade-off compared to the
    best available model for that prompt---concretely, either
    paying for an expensive model when a cheap one sufficed, or
    accepting lower quality when a better model was affordable.
    \emph{Adaptive (Reset)} detects the shift and resets its sufficient
    statistics, achieving the lowest final regret (206.5), a
    \textbf{8.9\%} reduction vs.\ \emph{Warmup-only} (226.6)
    and a 2.5\% improvement over the oracle forgetting factor.
    %
    \textbf{(b)}~Covariate shift detector for the Adaptive condition.
    The EMA chi-squared score (solid green) spikes above the
    $2\sigma$ threshold (dotted red) after the Phase~2 boundary,
    triggering a prior reset at step~${\approx}550$.  After reset the
    detector re-enters burn-in on the new distribution and stabilises.
    %
    \textbf{(c)}~Llama routing fraction over time.
    The \emph{cost-adjusted optimum} (dashed red, 60\%) is the Llama
    fraction a clairvoyant router would select on the shifted
    distribution at $\lambda{=}0.2$---i.e., the per-prompt
    $\arg\max_a [r_a - \lambda \cdot c_a]$ evaluated with ground-truth
    rewards.
    After prior reset, Adaptive discovers the newly-competitive
    cheap arm at the same rate as Oracle~ff,
    both converging toward this optimum.
    %
    \emph{Tabula Rasa} (dotted gray) starts at 100\% Llama because,
    absent any learned quality signal, the cost penalty alone determines
    routing---the cold-start failure mode that warmup priors eliminate.
    It then learns the in-distribution ranking (Gemini dominant) within
    ${\sim}100$ steps, before slowly re-discovering Llama after the shift.
    %
    Setup: priors trained offline on Pareto train ($n{=}8{,}374$),
    $n_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}5{,}000$,
    $\alpha{=}0.5$ (re-tuned at production $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$),
    disjoint LinUCB, no Corralling.
    Drift detector: burn-in~50 steps, EMA~$\alpha_d{=}0.05$,
    confirmation window~20 steps.
    }
    \label{fig:adaptive_drift}
\end{figure}
