\subsection{Distribution Shift, Online Learning, and Corralling}
\label{sec:distribution_shift}
\label{app:distribution_shift}

We construct a $2 \times 3$ factorial design to measure how online learning and the Corralling meta-learner isolate prior distribution shift (PSI$\,{=}\,0.763$). The conditions are prior source (cross-distribution 80K battle priors vs. same-distribution 355-prompt priors) crossed with adaptation mode (Frozen vs. Online single expert vs. Online Corralling).

\subsubsection{Interpretation and Practical Implications}

\paragraph{Online learning is the dominant effect ($+7.0$\,pp).}
Switching from a frozen prior to any online bandit---even a single expert without Corralling---yields a $+7.0$\,pp improvement, consistent across both prior sources. Any bandit-based router captures this; online adaptation is the primary mechanism for handling distribution shift.

\paragraph{Corralling provides a significant steady-state gain ($+1.3$\,pp).}
Layering Corralling adds a highly significant $+1.3$\,pp ($p < 0.0001$, Cohen's $d = 0.98$). Learning curve analysis confirms Corralling leads at \emph{every} checkpoint from step~50 onward, converging to a higher reward plateau than a single-expert bandit regardless of prior quality. This separates banditGPT from a standard contextual bandit: the two-expert architecture adaptively blends informed and uninformed experts.

\paragraph{Distribution shift has no significant practical impact.}
Despite PSI$\,{=}\,0.763$ and severe prior miscalibration, the practical performance difference between cross- and same-distribution priors is not statistically significant once online learning is enabled ($p > 0.18$). Online learning rapidly overwrites the prior.

For practitioners, this establishes a clear hierarchy: (1) enable online learning, (2) use Corralling for a significant steady-state advantage, and (3) safely initialize with any available offline data---including cross-distribution sources---without measurable penalty.
