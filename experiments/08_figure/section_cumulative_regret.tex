\subsubsection{Cumulative Regret Analysis}

While the preceding sections demonstrate banditGPT's practical utility across various deployment scenarios, cumulative regret provides the standard theoretical measure for its learning efficiency. For a contextual bandit operating over $T$ rounds, the
cumulative regret is
\[
  R_T = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \bigl[\max_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \mu_a(x_t) - \mu_{a_t}(x_t)\bigr],
\]
where $x_t$ is the context (prompt embedding), $\mathcal{A}$ is the arm
(model) set, $\mu_a(x_t)$ is the expected reward of arm $a$ in context $x_t$,
and $a_t$ is the arm selected by the policy.  An algorithm with sub-linear
regret ($R_T = o(T)$) provably converges to the oracle policy; the tighter
$O(\sqrt{T})$ rate is the minimax-optimal bound for linear contextual
bandits~\cite{abbasi2011improved}.

\paragraph{Experimental setup.}
We track per-step regret during the 533-prompt online learning phase
($\lambda = 0$, no cost penalty) for five methods:
banditGPT, LinTS (warmup), LinTS (tabula rasa), $\varepsilon$-greedy ($\varepsilon{=}0.1$),
and uniform random.
Each method runs 20 independent trials with different random seeds.

\subsubsection{Understanding the $\varepsilon$-Greedy Result}
\label{sec:egreedy_analysis}

Surprisingly, $\varepsilon$-greedy---a non-contextual baseline---may achieve lower cumulative regret than contextual methods at $\lambda{=}0$ \textbf{TODO: update with K=3 numbers}. This is a predictable consequence of extreme model dominance in this specific portfolio: GPT-4.1's average reward ($0.983$) is so high that the \emph{contextual improvement ceiling} (the gap to the $1.000$ per-prompt oracle) is only $1.7\%$. 

Standard regret theory predicts this: with fixed $\varepsilon{=}0.1$, the per-step exploration cost is $\varepsilon \bar{\Delta}$, where $\bar{\Delta}$ is the average sub-optimality gap across arms. When one arm dominates ($\bar{\Delta}$ small), cumulative regret grows slowly despite the linear $O(\varepsilon T)$ worst-case rate. By contrast, LinUCB's regret scales as $O\!\bigl(d\sqrt{KT\log T}\bigr)$~\cite{abbasi2011improved}, paying a mandatory feature-space exploration tax regardless of $\bar{\Delta}$. In this ``easy instance'' regime, simpler algorithms are more efficient.

However, contextual routing's value emerges under production constraints:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \textbf{Cost-sensitive operation ($\lambda > 0$):} At realistic cost penalties, the oracle routes $74.5\%$ of traffic to Llama-3.1-8B ($136\times$ cheaper than GPT-4.1). banditGPT discovers this Pareto-optimal allocation, achieving a $96.3\%$ cost reduction with near-oracle quality. $\varepsilon$-greedy, locked onto the expensive model, cannot make this trade-off.
  \item \textbf{Heterogeneous portfolios:} When no single model dominates (as in our $K{=}2$ evaluation), contextual methods easily outperform static baselines.
  \item \textbf{Non-stationary environments:} banditGPT's continuous UCB exploration and Corralling meta-learner automatically detect model degradation or API shifts, whereas $\varepsilon$-greedy adapts slowly ($O(1/\varepsilon)$ steps) to a changed best arm.
\end{enumerate}
