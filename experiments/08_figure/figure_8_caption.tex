  \caption{
    \textbf{Cumulative Regret During Online Learning ($K{=}5$, $K{=}10$).}
    Regret at step $t$: $r_t = \max_{a \in \mathcal{A}} \mu_a(x_t) - \mu_{a_t}(x_t)$.
    \emph{Left:} $K{=}5$; \emph{Right:} $K{=}10$.
    Among contextual bandits, banditGPT achieves the lowest regret at $K{=}5$
    (27.1 vs.\ 31.9 for LinTS, $-15\%$) and is competitive at $K{=}10$ (28.9 vs.\ 29.9).
    Both contextual methods track below the $O(\sqrt{T})$ reference (grey dashed),
    confirming sub-linear regret consistent with linear bandit theory.
    $\varepsilon$-greedy achieves the lowest overall regret (14.5 at $K{=}5$)
    by exploiting the single best-on-average model (GPT-4.1).
    This is expected: when one model dominates (contextual ceiling $=1.7\%$),
    non-contextual methods benefit from a favourable $O(K/\Delta \cdot \log T)$
    bound, while contextual methods pay $O(d\sqrt{KT})$ exploration cost
    regardless of gap size.
    The value of contextual routing emerges at nonzero cost penalties
    (Section~\ref{sec:egreedy_analysis}), where the oracle routes $74.5\%$
    of traffic to the $136\times$ cheaper Llama-3.1-8B.
    Shaded bands: $\pm 1\sigma$ across 20 trials.
