\subsection{The Value of Warmup Priors ($K{=}2$)}
\label{sec:warmup_ablation_k2}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The Pareto frontier in Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_k2} shows that all
contextual methods achieve comparable AUC for $K{=}2$.  This section
asks a sharper question: when the router operates at a \emph{fixed}
cost penalty, do warmup priors reduce cumulative regret---the canonical
measure of online learning efficiency?  We compare two conditions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{BanditGPT (warm priors):}  Disjoint LinUCB initialised
        with offline priors from the training split
        (ridge-regression sufficient statistics, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$
        controlling prior strength).
  \item \textbf{Tabula Rasa (no priors):}  The same Disjoint LinUCB
        architecture but starting from scratch
        ($A = \lambda I$, $b = \mathbf{0}$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Both conditions use independently tuned hyperparameters
($\alpha$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$) selected on the validation split at each
$\lambda_c$ to maximise mean reward, following the operating-point
tuning methodology of PILOT~\cite{panda2025pilot}.  This ensures a
fair comparison: Tabula Rasa receives its own best exploration rate,
not the rate tuned for warm priors.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The router trains online on $n{=}8{,}374$ prompts (train split, partial
feedback), then continues learning on the held-out test split
($n{=}1{,}824$).  All metrics are computed on the test split.
The validation split ($n{=}1{,}785$) is used exclusively for
hyperparameter selection and is not evaluated.
Results are averaged over 20 independent seeds with matched shuffle
order between conditions.

\paragraph{Regret reduction across cost penalties.}
Table~\ref{tab:regret_reduction} reports cumulative test-set regret
for both conditions across six cost penalties.
Crucially, the test split is reached \emph{after} both conditions have
processed $n{=}8{,}374$ online training prompts.  Tabula Rasa is
therefore no longer ``cold'' at evaluation time; it has had ample
opportunity to learn from online feedback.  The comparison measures
whether offline-informed initialisation provides a
\textbf{lasting parameter-quality advantage} that extensive online
learning alone does not fully recover---a stronger claim than
transient cold-start acceleration.

Three regimes emerge:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Moderate cost sensitivity
        ($\lambda_c \in [0.05,\, 0.20]$):}
        Warmup priors consistently reduce regret by 9--20\%
        \emph{even after 8{,}374 online steps}.
        The prior encodes quality signals from the offline evaluation,
        and the resulting parameter estimates remain superior in
        regions of the context space that receive sparse online
        observations---the ``long tail'' of prompt types where
        partial feedback alone is insufficient to fully calibrate
        arm estimates.
        At $\lambda_c{=}0.15$, where the quality--cost trade-off
        is most active, the reduction is \textbf{20.0\%}
        ($49.3 \pm 2.0$ vs.\ $61.6 \pm 0.9$).
  \item \textbf{High cost sensitivity ($\lambda_c \geq 0.30$):}
        Both conditions converge to high exploration ($\alpha{=}2.0$)
        and near-identical regret.  When cost dominates the UCB score,
        the router routes almost exclusively to the cheap model
        regardless of context, and quality priors offer no guidance.
  \item \textbf{Variance reduction.}
        Beyond mean regret, warmup priors stabilise learning across
        seeds.  At $\lambda_c{=}0.10$, Tabula Rasa exhibits an order
        of magnitude higher seed-level variance
        (SE $16.6$ vs.\ $0.9$), indicating that some uninformed
        seeds fall into suboptimal exploration trajectories that
        priors prevent.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
This pattern mirrors the $K{=}3$ ablation
(Appendix~\ref{appendix:cold_start}), where the warm-start advantage
similarly concentrates in the quality-sensitive regime and vanishes
as $\lambda$ pushes routing toward the cheapest model.

\paragraph{Learning dynamics (Figure~\ref{fig:warmup_ablation_k2}).}
Both panels show exclusively \textbf{held-out test data}
($n{=}1{,}824$) to avoid conflating the warm-start benefit with the
data overlap between priors and training (priors are built offline
from the same train split that both conditions process online).

Panel~(a) shows the cumulative regret accrued during the test phase.
The gap between conditions is present from the first test prompts
and widens monotonically, reflecting better-calibrated arm
estimates that warm priors carry through from the training phase.
Since Tabula Rasa has already processed $8{,}374$ online training
prompts at this point, the persistent gap demonstrates that
offline priors encode information that online partial feedback
alone does not fully recover.
Panel~(b) shows the windowed average reward on the test split.
BanditGPT maintains a ${\sim}1$~pp advantage throughout,
consistent with the regret gap in panel~(a).

\paragraph{Relationship to prior work.}
The warm-start bandit literature provides theoretical support for
these findings.  \citet{kveton2023warmstart} prove that Linear
Thompson Sampling with warm-start priors achieves tighter regret
bounds than cold-start under standard assumptions, and demonstrate
empirical gains on recommendation and database tuning tasks.
\citet{pan2024cbli} show that even approximate priors (generated by
an LLM rather than from offline data) reduce online regret
in contextual bandits.
In the LLM routing setting specifically, PILOT~\cite{panda2025pilot}
uses offline human preference data to initialise a shared embedding
space before online learning.  Their ablation (Table~6) shows that
combining the preference prior with 10\% online data approaches
the performance of fully trained PILOT\@.
Our results complement these findings: we provide the first systematic
characterisation of how warm-start regret reduction varies with the
cost operating point, showing that priors are most valuable when the
routing decision involves a genuine quality--cost trade-off rather
than a cost-dominated one.

Direct numerical comparison of absolute regret values across papers
is not meaningful, as cumulative regret depends on the model panel,
reward definition, prompt distribution, and horizon
length~\cite{lattimore2020bandit}.
The operationally meaningful comparison is the \emph{relative}
reduction, and the 9--20\% range we observe at moderate
$\lambda_c$ is consistent with the warm-start literature.

\paragraph{Information budget and fairness.}
The warmup priors are constructed from \emph{full-information}
rewards---observing all arms' quality on every training prompt---whereas
Tabula Rasa learns only from partial (bandit) feedback during the
online phase.  BanditGPT therefore has a strictly larger information
budget.  We note three points in defence of this comparison:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Standard evaluation assumption.}
        The same full-information data is available to all supervised
        baselines in Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_k2} (logistic regression,
        KNN, SVM, MLP) and is the standard evaluation setting in
        LLM routing benchmarks~\cite{ong2024routellm, panda2025pilot}.
        The warmup priors use this data no differently than a
        supervised classifier would.
  \item \textbf{Realistic production analogue.}
        In deployment, the full-information phase corresponds to an
        initial A/B testing or uniform exploration period where all
        candidate models are served on a traffic sample and their
        rewards observed.  This is standard practice before engaging
        a contextual bandit~\cite{kveton2023warmstart} and
        constitutes a bounded, one-time data-collection cost.
  \item \textbf{The regret reduction reflects both prior quality
        and the warm-start mechanism.}
        The 9--20\% reduction at moderate $\lambda_c$ is not purely
        an information advantage: it also reflects the mechanism of
        compressing full-information data into LinUCB sufficient
        statistics that initialise online learning in the right
        region of parameter space.  A na\"ive full-information
        classifier (e.g., logistic regression in
        Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_k2}) cannot continue improving from
        bandit feedback after deployment; the warm-started bandit can.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
Constructing warmup priors from partial-feedback production logs---via
inverse propensity scoring~\cite{dudik2014doubly} or offline policy
evaluation---is an important extension for settings where a full A/B
test is infeasible.  We leave this to future work.

\paragraph{Fixed exploration rate.}
Both conditions use a single exploration rate~$\alpha$ throughout
training, following the standard LinUCB
formulation~\cite{li2010contextual, panda2025pilot}.
The tuning procedure naturally selects higher~$\alpha$ for Tabula Rasa
than for warmup (e.g., $0.30$ vs.\ $0.01$ at $\lambda_c{=}0.15$),
reflecting the uninformed condition's greater exploration need.
Nevertheless, an $\alpha$-decay schedule---high exploration early,
low exploitation late---could disproportionately benefit Tabula Rasa,
whose uncertainty is highest in its first few hundred interactions.
We note that the constraint is symmetric (neither condition receives
decay) and that the test-set regret is measured after
$n{=}8{,}374$ training steps, well past the phase where scheduling
would have the largest effect.
Exploring $\alpha$-decay or Bayesian exploration schedules for
cold-start bandits is a natural extension.

\paragraph{Practical implications.}
The table provides actionable deployment guidance:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,noitemsep]
  \item When operating at moderate cost sensitivity
        ($\lambda_c \leq 0.20$), investing in offline prior
        construction yields measurable regret reduction and
        lower seed-level variance.
  \item When cost budgets are severely constrained
        ($\lambda_c \geq 0.30$), the router's decisions are
        dominated by cost and warmup priors offer diminishing
        returns.  In this regime, a simpler tabula rasa deployment
        suffices.
  \item The operating-point-specific tuning of $\alpha$
        (Table~\ref{tab:regret_reduction}) is critical:
        hyperparameters tuned for Pareto AUC can be suboptimal
        at a fixed $\lambda_c$, echoing the tuning recommendations
        of PILOT~\cite{panda2025pilot}.
\end{itemize}
