\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../experiments/03_figure/results/figure3_warmup_ablation.pdf}
    \caption{Warmup prior ablation for the $K{=}2$ portfolio
    (\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B} vs.\ \texttt{Gemini-2.5-Pro}) at
    cost penalty $\lambda_c{=}0.15$, averaged over 20~seeds
    ($\pm 1$~SE shaded).
    \textbf{Both panels show exclusively held-out test data}
    ($n{=}1{,}824$); the test split was not used for prior
    construction or hyperparameter selection.
    %
    \textbf{(a)}~Cumulative regret
    $\sum_{t=1}^{T}[\max_a \mu_a(x_t) - \mu_{a_t}(x_t)]$
    on the test split.
    BanditGPT (warm priors, solid blue) accumulates
    $49.3 \pm 2.0$ regret versus
    $61.6 \pm 0.9$ for Tabula Rasa (no priors, dashed gray),
    a \textbf{20.0\% reduction}.
    The gap opens within the first few hundred test prompts
    and widens monotonically.
    %
    \textbf{(b)}~Windowed average reward (window${=}200$)
    on the test split.
    Warm priors yield ${\sim}1$~pp higher reward throughout
    the evaluation phase, reflecting better-calibrated arm
    estimates carried over from the training phase.
    %
    Both conditions train on the same train split
    ($n{=}8{,}374$, online partial feedback) before evaluation.
    Warmup priors are built offline from full-information
    rewards on the train split---the same data available to
    supervised baselines (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto_k2}).
    Hyperparameters are independently tuned per condition on
    the validation split at $\lambda_c{=}0.15$
    (Table~\ref{tab:regret_reduction}).}
    \label{fig:warmup_ablation_k2}
\end{figure}
