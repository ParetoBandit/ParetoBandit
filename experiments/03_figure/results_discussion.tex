\subsection{Experimental Protocol}

\paragraph{Ground-truth multi-judge rewards.}
Rewards are computed via \texttt{extract\_reward()}: mean of (vote $\times$ confidence)
across the multi-judge panel.  Under binary \texttt{raw\_score}, 58--66\% of prompts
yield identical rewards across all $K$ models; under mean(vote $\times$ confidence),
this drops to $<1\%$, providing the fine-grained signal needed to distinguish
routing strategies.

\paragraph{Train-then-freeze protocol.}
All methods train on the dev set and are frozen for holdout evaluation.
BanditGPT trains online (route $\to$ observe reward $\to$ update);
supervised baselines (KNN, SVM, MLP) train offline via scikit-learn on
the same dev-train split.  Both classes of router are frozen during
evaluation, making the comparison directly interpretable: observed
differences reflect learned routing policies, not online learning
artefacts.

\paragraph{Supervised baselines (LLMRouter-style).}
We compare against three supervised static routers following the
LLMRouter protocol~\cite{hu2024routerbench, li2026llmrouterbench,
pulishetty2025onehead}, implemented via
scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011scikit}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{KNN} ($k$-nearest neighbors): deterministic; uses the same
          BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bgem3} + PCA embeddings as BanditGPT.
    \item \textbf{SVM} (support vector machine): maps embeddings to best-model
          labels via a tuned kernel classifier.
    \item \textbf{MLP} (multi-layer perceptron): two hidden layers with early
          stopping on a validation split, mirroring BanditGPT's convergence
          strategy.
\end{itemize}
These three classifiers are standard supervised routing baselines in the
LLM routing literature~\cite{hu2024routerbench, pulishetty2025onehead,
li2026llmrouterbench}.
All baselines are hyperparameter-tuned via grid search on the dev-val split,
using the same held-out data that BanditGPT uses for lambda selection.
This ensures symmetric tuning budgets.  KNN is deterministic
(std$\,{=}\,$0 reflects zero initialisation variance, not zero statistical
uncertainty); SVM and MLP use multi-seed trials.

\paragraph{Isocost and iso-quality comparison.}
BanditGPT offers a continuous cost--quality Pareto frontier (via $\lambda$),
whereas supervised baselines produce a single operating point.  Following
the unified evaluation framework of
LLMRouterBench~\cite{li2026llmrouterbench} and the cost-aware metrics
of RouterBench~\cite{hu2024routerbench}, we compute three comparison
metrics that are standard in the LLM routing
literature~\cite{li2026llmrouterbench, pulishetty2025onehead,
ong2024routellm, chen2023frugalgpt}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PerfGain}: BanditGPT's interpolated reward at the baseline's
          cost minus the baseline's reward (isocost comparison; positive means
          BanditGPT achieves higher quality at the same
          budget)~\cite{li2026llmrouterbench}.
    \item \textbf{CostSave}: percentage reduction in cost for BanditGPT to
          match the baseline's quality (iso-quality comparison; positive means
          BanditGPT is cheaper at the same reward
          level)~\cite{li2026llmrouterbench, chen2023frugalgpt}.
    \item \textbf{Gap@Oracle}: remaining reward gap to instance-wise optimal
          routing, reported as $(R_{\text{oracle}} - R_{\text{method}}) /
          R_{\text{oracle}} \times 100\%$~\cite{hu2024routerbench,
          li2026llmrouterbench}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Dev-selected deployable frontier.}
The dev set is split into a \emph{train} portion (80\%) and a held-out
\emph{val} portion (20\%).  BanditGPT trains on dev-train; supervised
baselines tune hyperparameters on the same dev-train/val split.  Dev
metrics ($c_{\text{dev}}, r_{\text{dev}}$) used for Pareto frontier
selection come exclusively from dev-val.  The Pareto hull is built from
$(c_{\text{dev-val}}, r_{\text{dev-val}})$ pairs, and for those
dev-optimal settings, the corresponding holdout cost and holdout reward
are extracted.  No holdout information enters the selection step.

\paragraph{Greedy frozen evaluation.}
When evaluating a frozen BanditRouter, the LinUCB~\cite{li2010contextual}
exploration bonus is disabled ($\alpha{=}0$) and the
Corralling~\cite{agarwal2017corralling} meta-learner selects the
highest-weight expert deterministically, so holdout scores reflect the
\emph{learned policy} under pure exploitation.

\paragraph{Early stopping symmetry.}
BanditGPT training uses early stopping on dev-val reward (patience$\,{=}\,$5
evaluations at 50-step intervals, with best-checkpoint restoration via
\texttt{deepcopy}).  MLP uses analogous early stopping
(\texttt{early\_stopping=True}, \texttt{validation\_fraction=0.2},
\texttt{n\_iter\_no\_change=10}).  KNN and SVM are non-iterative and
require no convergence control.

\paragraph{Statistical reporting.}
\emph{Primary per-prompt test:} paired $t$-tests comparing BanditGPT's
per-prompt holdout rewards against each supervised baseline's per-prompt
rewards (df$\,{=}\,n_{\text{holdout}}{-}1$), with Holm-Bonferroni
correction~\cite{holm1979simple} across the three baselines (KNN, SVM,
MLP).
\emph{Across-seed stability test:} for deterministic baselines (KNN,
SVM), one-sample $t$-tests of BanditGPT's per-seed mean rewards against
the fixed baseline value; for stochastic baselines (MLP), Welch's
two-sample $t$-test~\cite{welch1947generalization} with
Satterthwaite-approximated degrees of freedom.  All across-seed tests
report Cohen's~$d$ effect sizes~\cite{cohen1988statistical}.
\emph{Pareto sweep test:} for each $\lambda$, a $t$-test across
seeds against the best supervised baseline.

\paragraph{Warmup prior provenance.}
BanditGPT is initialised with portfolio-specific warmup priors extracted
from a broader 43-model offline prior file.  For $K{=}2$, the priors
are filtered to the 2 models under test; for $K{=}10$, to the 10 models
under test.  This is an architectural feature of BanditGPT (transfer
learning from a broader registry), not an unfair data advantage---the
supervised baselines have no analogous pre-training component, but they
also require no such component since they are trained directly on the
dev set.

\paragraph{Addressing potential confounders.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Symmetric data access:} all methods train/tune on the
          same dev-train split with hyperparameter selection on the same
          dev-val split.
    \item \textbf{Symmetric convergence control:} BanditGPT and MLP both
          use early stopping; KNN and SVM are non-iterative.
    \item \textbf{Symmetric feature access:} all methods (BanditGPT, KNN,
          SVM, MLP) use the same BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bgem3} sentence
          embeddings projected via the same 6-component PCA.
    \item \textbf{Expanded tuning grids:} supervised baseline hyperparameter
          grids were expanded (KNN: 16 configs; SVM: 12 configs; MLP: 18
          configs) to reduce any tuning-budget asymmetry.
\end{enumerate}

warmup priors already capture the routing signal (GPT-4.1 dominates),
so online adaptation provides minimal lift---but also causes no
degradation, confirming the safety of the Corralling meta-learner.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The $K{=}2$ results establish that BanditGPT \emph{matches} the best
supervised baseline at quality-maximising settings while offering a
continuous Pareto frontier that static classifiers cannot provide.
With only two models, routing reduces to a binary decision, limiting
the scope for contextual routing to add value.  The $K{=}10$ experiment
(below) tests BanditGPT in the setting where contextual, cost-aware
routing matters most.

that a static SVM classifier cannot capture.

\paragraph{Iso-quality comparison.}
To match SVM's quality ($R{=}0.888$), BanditGPT's Pareto frontier
achieves the same reward at \$0.00094/req---a CostSave of 54.1\%
relative to SVM's \$0.00205/req.  Against MLP ($R{=}0.855$), the
savings are even larger: BanditGPT matches MLP quality at 80.8\% lower
cost.  In practical terms, a deployment switching from an SVM-based
static router to BanditGPT (at matched quality) would cut per-request
LLM inference costs by more than half.

\paragraph{Gap@Oracle analysis.}
BanditGPT at $\lambda{=}0$ closes 7.2\% of the gap to the oracle
ceiling ($R{=}0.972$), compared to 8.6\% for SVM, 12.1\% for MLP, and
13.0\% for KNN.  While the absolute gap of 7.2\% may seem modest,
it represents the most difficult portion of the quality ladder:
the oracle routes each prompt to its \emph{individually optimal} model,
a policy that requires perfect knowledge of the counterfactual reward
matrix---information no practical router possesses.

\paragraph{UCB1 ablation: value of contextual features.}
A non-contextual UCB1 baseline~\cite{abbasi2011improved}
($R{=}0.909$) slightly outperforms BanditGPT at $\lambda{=}0$
($R{=}0.902$) and nearly matches the best static model (GPT-4.1 at
$R{=}0.911$).  This is expected: without a cost penalty, UCB1 converges
to the globally best arm (GPT-4.1), achieving high reward at the expense
of always paying the premium price
(\$0.0034/req).  BanditGPT's advantage materialises when cost matters:
at any budget below \$0.003/req, BanditGPT's Pareto frontier dominates
UCB1's single operating point, demonstrating that contextual features
enable cost-efficient prompt-specific routing that a non-contextual
policy cannot achieve.

\paragraph{BanditGPT's Pareto sweep in detail.}
The 33-point Pareto sweep reveals a smooth cost--quality tradeoff:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\lambda{=}0$: $R{=}0.902$, \$0.00265/req (quality-maximising).
    \item $\lambda{=}0.14$: $R{=}0.892$, \$0.00102/req (62\% cheaper
          than $\lambda{=}0$, 1pp quality loss).
    \item $\lambda{=}0.25$: $R{=}0.890$, \$0.00098/req (matches SVM
          quality at half the cost).
    \item $\lambda{=}1.0$: $R{=}0.850$, \$0.00025/req (90\% cheaper,
          still above KNN quality).
\end{itemize}
This granularity allows practitioners to select any operating point along
the frontier based on their budget constraints---a capability that
single-point supervised baselines fundamentally cannot offer.

\subsection{Practical Implications}

The results from both $K{=}2$ and $K{=}10$ yield several practical
insights for LLM routing deployments:

\paragraph{1. BanditGPT matches or exceeds supervised baselines.}
At the quality-maximising operating point ($\lambda{=}0$), BanditGPT
matches SVM in $K{=}2$ (within 0.03pp) and significantly outperforms all
supervised baselines in $K{=}10$ ($+1.3$pp over SVM, $p{<}10^{-5}$).
This establishes that online contextual
bandits~\cite{li2010contextual, abbasi2011improved} are competitive
with---and often superior to---the supervised static routers studied in
the LLM routing
literature~\cite{hu2024routerbench, li2026llmrouterbench,
pulishetty2025onehead}, despite requiring no pre-collected preference
labels.

\paragraph{2. Cost savings at matched quality are substantial.}
In the $K{=}10$ portfolio, BanditGPT can deliver the same routing
quality as SVM at 54\% lower cost, or match MLP at 81\% lower
cost---savings consistent with cost-reduction findings in the LLM
efficiency literature~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt, ong2024routellm}.
For a deployment serving 1M requests/day at SVM's operating cost of
\$0.00205/req (\$2{,}050/day), switching to BanditGPT at matched quality
would save approximately \$1{,}100/day (\$400k/year)---a substantial
operational saving with no quality degradation.

\paragraph{3. The Pareto frontier is a deployment advantage.}
Supervised baselines produce a single operating point: one fixed
cost--quality pair.  BanditGPT produces an entire Pareto
frontier~\cite{wang2025barp, li2025llmbandit}, allowing deployment
teams to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Adjust the cost--quality tradeoff in real time via $\lambda$
          (e.g., tighter budgets during peak traffic).
    \item Negotiate SLAs at specific quality floors while minimising cost.
    \item A/B test different operating points without retraining.
\end{itemize}
This flexibility is particularly valuable in production environments
where budget constraints fluctuate and model pricing changes over time.

\paragraph{4. Online adaptation provides a structural advantage at scale.}
The $K{=}2$ results show that when the portfolio is trivially small, all
methods converge to the same solution.  The $K{=}10$ results reveal
BanditGPT's structural advantage: as the portfolio grows, the supervised
baselines' single-label classification struggles to capture the
combinatorial structure of prompt-model
interactions~\cite{hu2024routerbench}, while BanditGPT's contextual
bandit framework~\cite{li2010contextual, agarwal2017corralling}
naturally exploits prompt-specific model strengths.  The 7 of 33 $\lambda$ values that significantly outperform
SVM---all in the quality-maximising regime---confirm that BanditGPT
extracts routing signal that a static classifier misses.

\paragraph{5. Warmup priors accelerate deployment.}
BanditGPT's step-0 performance in $K{=}2$ ($R{=}0.910$) already matches
the converged supervised baselines, demonstrating that warmup priors from
a broader model registry provide immediate routing quality without any
online training.  This zero-shot capability enables instant deployment:
a new BanditGPT router can serve production traffic from the first
request, with quality improving as online feedback accumulates.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Selection (Appendix~H)}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The exploration coefficient $\alpha$, prior effective sample size
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and forgetting factor $\gamma$ are the key
hyperparameters of the Corralling
router~\cite{agarwal2017corralling, pacchiano2020model}.  A natural reviewer question
is:\ \emph{how sensitive are the results to these choices?}

\paragraph{Approach.}
Hyperparameters are selected via a 3D grid ablation (Appendix~H) over
$\alpha \times n_{\mathrm{eff}} \times \gamma$, independently for the
warmup and tabula-rasa experts.  This per-expert tuning allows the
two Corralling experts to operate at their individually optimal settings.
The selected values are automatically loaded by
\texttt{run\_prequential.py}; hardcoded hyperparameters are forbidden
(a \texttt{FileNotFoundError} is raised if the Appendix~H result files
are missing).
