\paragraph{Setup.}
We evaluate \textit{banditGPT}'s model onboarding capability by
adding Mistral-Large-2512 to a pre-trained $K{=}2$ portfolio
(Llama-3.1-8B + Gemini-2.5-Pro).
Mistral is an ideal onboarding candidate: it achieves 99\% of
Gemini's reward ($0.923$ vs.\ $0.932$) at $1/29$th the cost
(\$0.00052/req vs.\ \$0.015/req).
Crucially, it comes from a different provider family (Mistral AI),
so any knowledge transfer must work across provider boundaries.

The router is first trained on the canonical training split
($n{=}8{,}374$) with $K{=}2$ arms using the best corralling
configuration ($\alpha{=}0.05$, $\neff{=}50$, Hybrid LinUCB with
Corralling).
After training, Mistral is added via \texttt{register\_model()},
and the router processes the test split ($n{=}1{,}824$) with all
three arms.  We compare two conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Hybrid LinUCB}: the global shared $\beta$ learned
          during $K{=}2$ training transfers to the newcomer, giving it
          meaningful quality predictions from the first request.
    \item \textbf{Disjoint LinUCB} (ablation): independent per-arm
          parameters; the newcomer's arm-specific $A_a, b_a$ start from
          scratch.  Both conditions share identical $K{=}2$ warmup
          priors for the original arms and identical Corralling
          configuration---only the cross-arm parameter sharing differs.
\end{enumerate}
\noindent All hyperparameters ($\alpha{=}0.05$, $\neff{=}50$, $\lambda{=}0.20$)
were val-tuned for the Hybrid policy and kept fixed across conditions,
matching the production scenario where operators call
\texttt{register\_model()} without re-tuning.

\paragraph{Same destination, different speed.}
Figure~\ref{fig:onboarding_k3} (right panel) overlays Mistral's
windowed routing share for both conditions.
Both policies converge to the same steady-state routing mix---the
late-stage CostSave@95\% (computed on the last 500 prompts) is
statistically indistinguishable between conditions.
This confirms that the shared $\beta$ provides a
\emph{convergence speed} advantage, not a fundamental performance
difference: Hybrid LinUCB reaches steady state in approximately
$N_H$ prompts compared to $N_D$ for Disjoint (convergence defined
as windowed share within $\pm 5$pp of its final value).

\paragraph{Hybrid LinUCB enables rapid newcomer adoption.}
The left panel shows the windowed routing share under Hybrid LinUCB.
Mistral's traffic share rises within the first
${\approx}100$ prompts and stabilises at a dominant share.
Gemini's traffic share collapses correspondingly---the router
autonomously discovers that Gemini is rarely worth $29{\times}$ more
than Mistral.
The shared $\beta$ encodes prompt-level structure (e.g., ``hard
prompts need a strong model'') that transfers immediately to the
newcomer.

\paragraph{Disjoint LinUCB converges more slowly.}
Without the shared $\beta$ (centre panel), the newcomer must learn
its quality profile entirely from scratch.  Mistral's share rises
more gradually, and Gemini retains a larger share for longer as the
router explores.  The convergence lag translates to a measurable
transition overhead: Disjoint pays higher average cost during the
first 500 prompts than Hybrid.

\paragraph{Autonomous redundancy detection.}
The most striking finding is the near-complete displacement of
Gemini traffic: its windowed share drops below 10\% within the
first few hundred prompts under both conditions.
This validates a powerful production narrative:
\emph{``We started with a cheap model and an expensive frontier model.
We discovered a mid-tier model that delivers near-frontier quality at
a fraction of the cost.  The router incorporated it without
retraining and autonomously learned to stop using the expensive
model.''}
No human intervention or retraining was needed---the
\texttt{register\_model()} API and the bandit's online learning
handle the transition end-to-end.

\paragraph{Corralling stabilises the transition.}
We use the corralling configuration ($\alpha{=}0.05$, Corralling
enabled) rather than the non-corralling best config ($\alpha{=}1.0$)
because adding a model mid-stream is precisely the scenario where
the meta-learner's hedging between the warm-started expert and the
tabula-rasa expert provides stability.  The tabula-rasa expert can
independently learn the newcomer's quality without being biased by
the warm-started expert's $K{=}2$ priors.
