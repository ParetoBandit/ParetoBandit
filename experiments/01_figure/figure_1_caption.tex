\caption{\textbf{Model quality depends on prompt context, with heterogeneous sensitivity across $K{=}10$ models ($N{=}750$, holdout).}
\textbf{(A)}~OLS regression of mean reward ($\text{mean}(v \times c)$, from a 3--4 judge panel) on standardised whitened PC1 for each model, with 95\% confidence bands.
Lines fan and cross, so no single model dominates the full feature space.
A likelihood-ratio test rejects the shared-slope null ($p < 10^{-15}$; six whitened PCA components, $\text{df}{=}54$).
\textbf{(B)}~Forest plot of per-model contextual slopes $\gamma_m$ with bootstrap 95\% CIs ($10\,000$ resamples).
All ten CIs exclude zero, confirming that every model's quality varies with prompt features---but at rates differing by nearly an order of magnitude.
PCA trained on 80K RouteLLM battle prompts (independent dataset) with per-component whitening ($1/\sqrt{\text{explained\_variance}_j}$); reward computed as $\text{mean}(\text{vote} \times \text{confidence})$ over the judge panel; PC1 is one of 32 whitened components used by the router.}
\Description{Two-panel figure. Panel~A shows ten OLS regression lines of reward versus standardised whitened PC1, with shaded 95\% confidence bands; lines separate and cross, indicating different models are optimal in different feature regions. Panel~B is a forest plot of per-model contextual slopes with bootstrap confidence intervals; all ten intervals exclude zero.}
\label{fig:contextual_heterogeneity}
