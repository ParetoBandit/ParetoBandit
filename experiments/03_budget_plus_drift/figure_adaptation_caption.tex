    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../experiments/03_budget_plus_drift/results/adaptation_dynamics.pdf}
    \caption{BanditGPT adaptation dynamics under a Gemini price drop at
    three budget levels ($K{=}3$; Pacer + $\gamma{=}0.995$; 50~seeds,
    $\pm$1\,SE shading).  The unconstrained BanditGPT baseline (same
    algorithm, same priors, no budget constraint) is shown in green
    dash-dot for comparison.
    %
    \textbf{(a)~Dual variable $\lambda_t$:}
    At moderate budget, $\lambda_t$ drops from ${\sim}0.35$ to
    ${\sim}0.01$ within Phase~2 as post-drop costs fall below target.
    At loose budget, $\lambda_t$ decays from $0.09$ to near-zero
    almost immediately.
    At tight budget, $\lambda_t$ \emph{rises} from $0.46$ to $0.82$
    because post-drop costs (\$2.38e-4) slightly exceed the target
    (\$2.34e-4)---the constraint remains binding.
    %
    \textbf{(b)~Gemini-Pro selection fraction:}
    At tight budget, Gemini surges to ${\sim}84\%$---the highest of
    any constrained condition---because the elevated $\lambda_t$
    penalizes the now-expensive Mistral while Gemini's near-zero
    normalized cost is barely affected.
    At moderate and loose budgets, Gemini rises to
    ${\sim}47$--$49\%$, approaching the unconstrained level
    (${\sim}75\%$).
    %
    \textbf{(c)~Running average cost per request:}
    BanditGPT maintains Phase~1 costs within $3\%$ of the budget
    target (dotted horizontal lines).  Post-drop, moderate and loose
    costs decline well below target (slack constraint); tight costs
    remain within $2\%$ (binding constraint).  The unconstrained
    baseline spends ${\sim}6$--$51\times$ more in Phase~1.
    }
    \label{fig:exp03_adaptation}
\end{figure*}
